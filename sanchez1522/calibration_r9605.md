# R9605 as calibration: cipher and contemporary decipherment on facing leaves

DECODE R9605, RAH Salazar 9/24 f. 39–44, Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, **7 May 1522**.
This is the record Tomokiyo broke the cipher from. Images fetched 2026-09-20.

- `IMG_R9605_I45170_P2.jpg` — left leaf (f. 44) the **last seven lines of the cipher**, right leaf
  (f. 42) the **contemporary decipherment in clear**, headed *Al Rey / De Alonso Sanchez de Venecia
  de vij de mayo 1522*.
- `IMG_R9605_I45176_P2.jpg` — left leaf the **end of that decipherment**, right leaf a full page of
  cipher belonging to the next letter.

So the letter exists in cipher and in a full clerk's plaintext, on facing pages. That is the ground
truth this hand can be calibrated against.

## The alignment at the end of the letter

Decipherment (f., last lines, read from the image):

> … Despues que recebi la de V. Mag[esta]d, que fue a xxv, en fin me mando a mi ser de la venida del
> visorey con todas las postas, que despacho y escrivo y he venido; y lo mesmo hago … de todo aviso.
> **Plegue a Dios no lo yerre en el camino.** De Venecia a vij de mayo 1522.

Cipher (f. 44, last two lines; the words in *italics* stand in the clear on the page itself):

> … *plegue adios* `nuf` `pac` `ɡɣβ4q4` `vog` `veg` `zn·β·aɥ` `nuf` ‖‖‖ *en Venecia a vij de mayo 1522*

Term by term:

| cipher | plaintext | how |
|---|---|---|
| `nuf` | no | code |
| `pac` | lo | code |
| `ɡɣβ4q4` | yerre | spelled |
| `vog` `veg` | **en el** | code — Tomokiyo's own way in |
| `zn·β·aɥ` + `nuf` | **cami** + **no** = *camino* | spelled, then the code `nuf` (no) used for the last syllable |

Two things this settles that could not be settled from the key alone.

1. **Code groups are used for syllables, not only for words.** `nuf` is *no* the adverb in one breath
   and the final syllable of *camino* in the next. Any reading that assumes a code group is always a
   whole word will mis-segment.
2. **The lookalike signs separate.** In `zn·β·aɥ nuf` = *camino* the third sign is `β` = **m**; in
   `nx#Ɋn` = *ahora* (R9635 f. 20r) the fourth is `Ɋ` = **r**. Both are easy to write down as a
   "g"-shape at DECODE's resolution, and they are the single largest source of error in reading the
   spelled runs. The bold `∞` (letter g) is a third member of the same confusion set.

## Why this does not amount to reading the group

R9605 and R9613 are already deciphered — by the Emperor's own clerk in 1522, on the facing leaves.
Transcribing that clear text produces a continuous plaintext of those letters, but it is an edition of
a 1522 decipherment, not a decipherment. The records that are genuinely unread (R9635, R9653 and the
rest of the 9/26 tail) have no such facing plaintext, and reading them continuously means resolving
`β` / `Ɋ` / bold `∞` at every occurrence by eye.

The calibration route is now open and is the next session's work: run the same alignment across the
whole of R9605's cipher and its decipherment — several hundred signs of ground truth — to fix each
homophone's shape in this scribe's hand, then apply that to the unglossed pages.
