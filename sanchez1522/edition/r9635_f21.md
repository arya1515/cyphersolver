# Edition: R9635 f. 21 — Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 September 1522

RAH Salazar 9/26 f. 21 (DECODE image `IMG_R9635_I45364_P2.jpg`). Edition against Tomokiyo's
published key; method and sign values as in `r9635_f20r.md` and `../calibration_r9605.md`.

## Shape of the opening

Both leaves are **full pages of cipher**, denser than f. 20r — roughly thirty lines each. Two things
interrupt the cipher on the right-hand leaf:

1. the decipherer's gloss **obligados … fuessen restituydas sus tierras** written between the lines
   near the head — the same gloss that runs across f. 20r, so the restitution business continues here;
2. a **paragraph written entirely in the clear**, marked in the margin with an `A`, about the English
   ambassador.

## The clear paragraph on Richard Pace

Read from the image (abbreviations expanded in brackets):

> M[icer] Ricardo Paceo, embax[ad]or de Inglaterra, me ha dado el incluso memorial y rogado que
> escriva a v[uest]ra m[agesta]d suplicandole por las cartas que dize para el Papa y para don Joan.
> Sup[li]co a v[uest]ra m[agesta]d muy humilmente, con primero las mande enbiar, pues es persona que
> merece toda buena obra de v[uest]ra m[agesta]d, por ser muy servidor suyo y aficionado &c.

**Richard Pace**, Henry VIII's envoy at Venice, has given Sánchez an enclosed memorial and asked him
to write to the Emperor on his behalf — pressing for the letters Pace wants for the Pope and for
**don Juan** (Juan Manuel, the imperial ambassador at Rome, whose own 1522 cipher Tomokiyo
reconstructed in the same article). Sánchez seconds the request warmly.

This fixes the identification behind the code `hec` = *Inglaterra* met on f. 20r, and it is the same
Pace who opens R9631 in Tomokiyo's list ("Mi-c-e-r [R]-i-c-a-r-do p-a-c-e-o embaxa-do-r del
se-r-e-ni-ss-i-[m]-o"). The English ambassador's arrival and doings are the thread running through
the Venetian despatches of these months; R9621, the one letter Tomokiyo prints entire, turns on the
Signoria's wish for him.

## Cipher on the leaf: what is legible at this pass

Codes read off without difficulty in the opening lines of the right-hand leaf include `xig` (*de*),
`nuf` (*no*), `zem` (*aqui*), `dal` (*un*), `vog` (*en*), `xel` (*del*), `ho` (*que*), `vum`
(*esta*), g-y-`lal` (*republica*), `gad` (*se*), `vuc` (*do*), `pac`+s (*los*), `ped` (*mas*),
`vam vuc`+g (*embaxador*), `gam` (*su*), `gub` (*señor*), `hac`/`hed` (*rey*), `mef` (*paz*),
`fob` (*tambien*), `sef` (*guarda*), `mod` (*os*), `yeh`+q (*cartas*), `dom pud` (*vuestra
magestad*), `A`-words and the nulls opening each paragraph.

**The two leaves are not yet transcribed line by line.** What is established here is their structure,
the continuation of the restitution matter, and the clear Pace paragraph in full.

The hand on f. 21 is smaller and more cursive than f. 20r and the spelled runs between the codes do
not yet resolve cleanly. The codes read without difficulty; the letters need the same picture-book
treatment given to f. 20r, on this scribe's forms. Recorded as fragments rather than smoothed into a
sentence.
