# Sir Ralph Sadler (Edinburgh) to Henry VIII, 22 April 1543 (BL Add MS 32650 ff. 214-216, DECODE R4924) — NOTES

Status: no write-up

**Verdict: closed, already in print.** Session 2026-09-21. The letter was deciphered at the time, and the
decipherment is bound with it. It has been printed twice:

- Arthur Clifford (ed.), *The State Papers and Letters of Sir Ralph Sadler*, vol. I (Edinburgh, 1809),
  pp. 158-160, "To the King's Majesty, 22d April, 1543": the full text in clear (archive.org `statepapers01sadl`).
- Joseph Bain (ed.), *The Hamilton Papers*, vol. I (Edinburgh, 1890), no. 362, pp. 522-523 (archive.org
  `cu31924091786032`): "Sadleyr to Henry VIII. [fol. 214.] ... In cypher. Addressed. Wafer signet. (2) A decypher
  of the same letter. [fol. 218.] Indorsed: 'From Master Sadleyr to the kinges majeste, xxij Aprilis a° xxxiiij'.
  Damaged near the end." Bain summarises the letter, refers to Clifford ("Sadler Papers, vol. i. pp. 158-60") and
  lists corrections to Clifford's text taken from the manuscript.

Add MS 32646-32657 are the Hamilton Papers volumes I-XII, so Add MS 32650 is vol. V, which is Bain's heading.

## Content

Early that morning Sir George Douglas came to tell Sadler that the Governor, Arran, had turned against Henry. Arran
had sent away his "friers preachers" and had written secretly to Cardinal Beaton and the French-party earls (Lennox,
Huntly, Argyll, Moray). Douglas said that his brother Angus and the lords assured to England must look to
themselves. He advised Sadler to have the Scottish ambassadors held in England as surety for his safety, and to
write "all such letters as I should write from henceforth in cypher". This is the first of Sadler's letters from
this embassy sent in cipher. Douglas also offered to deliver the country south of the Forth to an English army that
summer, and blamed the Abbot of Paisley for the Governor's change.

## What was not done

The DECODE images were not viewed and no key was rebuilt. The reading, the contemporary decipher and two printings
already exist, so nothing would be added. If the cipher system is wanted later, the letter together with the fol. 218
decipher is a complete crib for Sadler's 1543 cipher. Bain vol. II has other decipherments of Sadler's letters from
the same embassy.
