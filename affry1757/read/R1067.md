## Summary

D'Affry reports his interview with Pensionary Steyn on the King's orders (the demand for an answer on the passage of
French troops by Maastricht/Namur): Steyn, embarrassed and under attack by enemies trying to bring him down, could not
answer before the week-end. D'Affry then saw the Gouvernante (Anne of Hanover), gave her the King's declaration; she
answered "must it be I who favour the means of doing harm to my father?" (George II); he asked her to use her credit to
obtain the answer the King had a right to expect. He writes that the princely party uses the affair to ruin the
Pensionary and the republican party. News of the troops' approach was carried at once to the States General. Other
periods: an Ostfriesland postal matter, a States General recommendation for owners of a prize he thinks legitimate, a
memoir for Moras on Maastricht; Count Wassenaer's refusal to come back and his younger son's post in Friesland; the
Reischach passport as his strongest argument; the passage of Namur; Wesel evacuated by the King of Prussia and a
conference on the post to Wesel; Amsterdam traders abusing double papers (Dutch expeditions for French privateers,
English papers for English ones).
