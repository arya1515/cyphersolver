## Summary

D'Affry sends "B" a copy of what M. ...tier wrote to him (the loan extract read in R1074). He thinks it useless to attempt money
operations even in England: if found out they would make France's needs look greater than they are and make the British more
careful. Englishmen will still place money in France when the interest is right, and some already hold French life annuities.
He knows that two or three Englishmen are at The Hague, one called Mac..., and he will report whether he leaves France and where
he goes. Two Spaniards have come from Paris, one of them M. de la S..., secretary of the Spanish embassy in Paris, and they will
go on to London. The London letters bring nothing new. The last part, less well read, is a conversation with a man about money
held by M. Be..k(er) and a Compton, which cannot be justified, and about bonds or obligations issued for subjects of the King
of Prussia, and prize (prise) cases.
