## Summary

The first and longer part is an extract of a report from a correspondent (M. ...tier) about a loan. A French loan of about
2 millions had been tried in England. It could have been raised before the proclamation of the new law, but the
negotiation was handled so clumsily that the British ministry heard of it. The writer says the law can still be got round.
France could borrow 4 millions at 4 to 4.5 % at most. But the funds to pay the interest, the way of making the payments and
of bringing the money into France without danger to the lenders call for deep secrecy, perfect knowledge of the country
and reliable agents. He suggests keeping it among a very few people and awaits orders.
D'Affry then adds his own news. Grain is short, and there is a half-read report of ships or troops ready at Portsmouth(?).
An Irishman, "le Sr Mac[donald]", has just come from France and left The Hague for England. D'Affry suspects him of carrying
commissions from the enemy. He served in the Irish regiment Bu[lkeley]. D'Affry warns that some Scots and Irish in the
French army are spies, but thinks it imprudent to act openly and cast suspicion on the others. He will try to watch
Mac[donald]'s movements and correspondents.
