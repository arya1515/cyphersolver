## Summary

The Dutch, after the alarm over the convoy (four warships were to escort it, two were disabled by the storm), are
relieved that it has arrived. D'Affry expects the naval armament of [n] ships to go through without any increase of the
land forces, and says the argument will be pressed harder now that people see that the English will respect Dutch
navigation only in proportion to the protection it has, so convoys through the Channel must be strongly escorted.
In Amsterdam he discussed with an Amsterdam magistrate (Borel?) a matter of [herring?] (group 200), speaking with all
the frankness he could, and ended by telling him that the States General ...; then went to the Amsterdam lodging to
repeat it.

## Comparison with Bussemaker no. 56 (7 Janvier 1757)

Bussemaker: the great aim is to increase the navy without increasing the land army; the envoy presses for better
protection of the Republic's trade; many republicans would want some privileges on herring if they could get the navy
increased without enlarging the army; that would help detach the Republic from England. Agreement: armament "sans
augmentation dans les troupes de terre"; protection/escort of navigation; the Amsterdam conversation about
"preneurs de [200]" fits the herring privileges (200 = harengs, inferred, M).
