## English summary

The States of Holland have adjourned and reassemble on the 20th to deal with the army augmentation, which d'Affry
hopes will fail. The war funds for this year are no larger than last year's, and the proposed means (a sixth added
to the house tax, a general rise in the excises, and cutting interest on public funds to 2%) are so burdensome that
the States are unlikely to adopt them. On herring: he has learnt how important it is to reduce the Dutch fishery's
profit and encourage the French one, but with French sailors short in wartime he thinks a temporary favour to the
Dutch fishery would cost little, would be revocable if the Republic misbehaved, would please nearly all the Dutch,
and would widen the Anglo-Dutch quarrel over the fishery; he would ask for it only on condition that Holland resists
the augmentation. The rest is intelligence: a suspect (Charles de Quintin, travelling from Antwerp to Breda with a
Lyon merchant, flagged by M. de Hatzel) will be arrested if he comes to Holland; a captain of the Löwendal regiment
has arrived; "the Italian" reports that one La Combe (translator of the Lettres philosophiques) and an old man from
Liège have left England for France to report on the French navy; and a Paris article in the Amsterdam gazette
supplement about two ship captains is answered with an extract of d'Affry's passport register (captains of the
Comte de Maurepas and another Bordeaux ship, and a surgeon from Coutras).

## Comparison with Bussemaker no. 59 (1 Février 1757)

Bussemaker: States of Holland adjourned until the 20th, then the augmentation comes up; war funds no more than usual
and the means so oppressive that the augmentation becomes unlikely: a sixth surcharge on the house tax, a general
increase of the excises, reduction of interest on public funds to 2%; France should protect its own herring fishery
against Dutch competition, but not at present, with French sailors lacking, so favouring the Dutch would do no harm;
the favour is temporary and can stop once the Republic no longer deserves it; it would please the Dutch most and,
herring being a bone of contention with England, estrange the two countries; he asks for it only if Holland blocks
the troop augmentation. This matches §§1-8 point by point and fixes 674 = 20, 981 = x (sixième), 42 = générale.
Bussemaker (Fruin) omits the intelligence paragraphs §§3-4 and §§9-13, as his preface says he did for agents and
intriguers.
