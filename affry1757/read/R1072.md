## Summary

D'Affry has had "the Italian" (an informer already known to Rouillé) come to see him. The man says he expects to be
rewarded for a service already rendered, advice on England's designs against French possessions in the two Indies,
passed on through a certain Maham or Mahom(et), commandant or sub-commandant of a fort. He will stay at The Hague at a
ducat a day until Rouillé's orders arrive. For a sum (cent louis appears) he will leave at once for England and report
the state of English land and sea forces, the troops, forts, subsidies and supplies, everything secret in the London
cabinet, proposals made to the ministers, English intrigues in foreign courts, the campaign plans for the year and
England's correspondents in France. He asks for a pension to live afterwards in France or Italy. D'Affry thinks that
if the man is reliable the money could not be better spent, asks for orders urgently, and says that if his services
are accepted he should leave for London as soon as possible.

## Comparison with Bussemaker

Bussemaker has no entry for 6 January 1757. No. 55 (30 Dec 1756: the Republic will stay neutral from weakness, not
from love of France; Prussia's Protestant league) and no. 56 (7 Jan 1757: naval increase without land increase,
protection of trade, herring) do not touch this matter. This letter, entirely about an Italian spy offering to go to
London, is new content not summarised in the printed edition, which is consistent with its being a separate secret
despatch.
