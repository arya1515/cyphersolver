## Summary

It is said that the Gouvernante (Princess Anne, "son Altesse Royale") will go to the States of Holland to speak in
favour of her father George II and urge the need to help [England/Hanover], invoking the danger to the Protestant
religion. D'Affry doubts she will; if she does and proposes a land-forces augmentation, the other side will propose a
naval augmentation instead. Her party made the most noise about the French occupation of Ostend and Nieuport, but
sensible people see that with Ostend closed to the English, Amsterdam and Rotterdam will gain the trade England did
with the Austrian Netherlands, worth several hundred thousand pieces a year. In some towns ministers preached on the
danger to religion, but the people are not roused. The Ostend affair will only matter if the Princess uses it as a
pretext; in that case d'Affry would ask her for an audience, without waiting for orders, to show her that her own and
her son's (William V) interest is to keep the King's goodwill.

## Comparison with Bussemaker no. 76

Bussemaker: "Misnoegen alhier over het bezetten van Oostende en Nieuwpoort, en toch zullen Amsterdam en Rotterdam
daardoor den handel winnen, dien Engeland op de Nederlanden dreef. Il y a quelques villes, où quelques ministres
hollandais ont prêché sur le danger de la religion, mais le peuple n'en paroit pas animé." The reading matches both
points (displeasure at the Ostend/Nieuport occupation; the trade gain for Amsterdam and Rotterdam) and the quoted
sentence group for group. Bussemaker omits the Gouvernante's projected harangue and d'Affry's audience plan.
