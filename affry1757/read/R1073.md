## Summary

A rumour had spread that d'Affry had given the States General a memorial saying the King would regard an increase of
the Republic's land forces as a breach of neutrality. The rumour was killed at once, but d'Affry saw the Grand
Pensionary: the King did not and never meant to meddle in their internal affairs, but out of friendship would regret to
see them commit to heavy expenses whose consequences the Republic should fear. The Pensionary answered that the
augmentation will meet many difficulties, and that if its supporters cannot avoid it they will at least be strong
enough to have a declaration first made to the courts of Versailles, Vienna and London, by which the Republic would
say where it stands (to remain neutral). Nothing is settled but verbally until next month; no interesting news from
London.

## Comparison with Bussemaker

No entry for 25 January. It fits between no. 57 (17 Jan: the republicans hope to slow the push for a land
augmentation by a tax plan that loads its cost on the people) and no. 58 (27 Jan: the Republic must be kept from
increasing its army; Vienna, through the Barrier treaty and Namur, should press it, with great caution). This letter
shows the French line in between: disavowing any threat, but warning against the expense, and the Pensionary's
forecast of difficulties for the augmentation and of a prior declaration to Vienna and London - the same lever no. 58
proposes.
