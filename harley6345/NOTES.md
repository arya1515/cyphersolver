# Harley MS 6345: English ambassadors with Charles V (1523) and Wolsey to Sampson (1524) (DECODE R7760-R7762)

Status: no write-up

Catalogue no. 134 (DECODE refill, rule-scored). Worked 21 Sept 2026.

Outcome: **explained; closed.** There is nothing ciphered to read beyond one code name.

## What the records are

BL Harley MS 6345 is a later letter-book of Wolsey's diplomatic correspondence, copied in one secretary hand. DECODE's
"unknown sender / unknown recipient" for these three records comes from that copy.

- **R7760 (f. 34, 1 image)**, ending "July. 3. 1523": the end of Richard Sampson and Sir Richard Jerningham's despatch
  from the Emperor's court (Valladolid) to Wolsey on the treaty for the joint invasion of France: entry into Guienne on
  20 August, the King's army to be at Calais, joining the Emperor's army within five days, the Emperor's chancellor
  offering to renew the indemnity article. The original is BL Cotton Vespasian C.II f. 149, calendared as
  *Letters and Papers Henry VIII* III no. 3150 (Sampson and Jerningham to [Wolsey]), with the treaty itself at no. 3149.
  It is in clear except for one code name, written `=7h=` ("Monsieur de =7h=", "the said =7h=", "the condition of 27h",
  "sure news of the =7h=") and occurring six times. The calendar says "the article of Bourbon" at the same point, and
  the context fits Charles, duke of Bourbon, whose "condition" (his rising in France) the treaty depended on. So
  **=7h= = Bourbon**.
- **R7761 (ff. 34v-35v, 3 images) and R7762 (ff. 35v-37, 4 images)** are one letter, not two: Wolsey to "my loving
  friend Mr Richard Sampson, Dean of the King's Chapel and his grace's Ambassador to the Emperor, October 1524" (Bourbon
  at Marseilles, John Joachim at the English court, the bishop of Bath at Rome, Gregory Casale, the French King going
  into Italy). The two records share a page (R7761 P2 and R7762 P1 are the same image file). It is entirely in clear
  and names Bourbon in full. The "encrypted characters on the margin" in DECODE's note are insertion marks (an `A` and
  an `&` in the margin, repeated at the insertion point in the text) and marginal subject words ("Emperour",
  "something"), not cipher. I did not find it in the two L&P IV pages for late October 1524 that I checked.

Key record R7764 (Harley 6345 f. 186, 1523) turned out to be another page of the same letter-book: the end of
instructions to the ambassadors in France, and a clear letter "To my Lord Cardinal's grace" dated 13 Sept 1521. It
contains no key.
