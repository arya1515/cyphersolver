# Nicolas Raince to Anne de Montmorency: the ciphered residue of BnF fr. 2984, read

Read 17 September 2026 off the Gallica microfilm (ark btv1b90598430) with Tomokiyo's 1526 key
as re-measured in CALIBRATION.md. 86 of the 106 lines were read glyph by glyph against
numbered-token images (gold/labels.txt); the machine decode was used only to scaffold the
reading, never to fill a gap. Word division and the expansion of abbreviations are editorial.

Conventions
  …        a stretch that did not resolve; nothing is supplied by conjecture
  [ ]      letters supplied by the editor where the sense is certain
  (?)      a reading that fits the glyphs and the sense but is not secure
  (con)    the *con* word sign; (l'empereur), (le pape) likewise
  ·        the unidentified bold double-barred glyph, which always falls at a clause
           boundary and is probably a point or a fourth null (see NOTES.md)
  -        a word broken at the end of a line and continued on the next

Lines are numbered within the segmented region, not from the top of the page: p. 29 line 01
here is line 11 of the leaf (lines 1–10 carry the contemporary gloss and were not segmented).

What the ciphered passages are about
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 13 May 1526 letter, written nine days before the League of Cognac was signed, reports
on the Emperor's side of the negotiation and on the movement of agents: someone "qui estoit
en la court de Savoye est party pour venir icy"; the Castilians and "les bourguignons";
a plan to "prendre le chemin de Valence"; a capitulation already made, which "il[z] seront
cause de la destruction … de la part d'avoir faict capituler"; an agent Herrera(?) who
"doit partir … dans deux ou troys jours pour s'en retourner". Rome's own despatches are
described as going "d'icy et de Venise par devers ledict seigneur", and the Emperor "est
esbay de ce que … n'a escript autrement". The Admiral of France is quoted at second hand
("mondict seigneur l'Admiral luy avoit dict que seroit celuy qui y tiendroit"). The plague
is in Rome ("la peste qui est au logis"), advices reach the Imperials through their own
people ("des advis qui viennent aux imperiaulx par leurs …"), a named man — Pietro Anthonio,
secretary or servant of the same — was taken "un jour, en plaine place pres du palais", and
there is talk of "grant obligation pour les dommaiges qu'ilz disent ledict avoir …".
The clear close of the same letter, on p. 31, expects Andrea Doria at Civitavecchia within
a day with six galleys and two brigantines.

The 20 November letter, two months after the Colonna raid on the Vatican, turns on the
Pope: a pontificate that "seroit la cause de la totale ruine de sa maison" — Clement VII
and the Medici — with talk of doing something "secretement", of flight ("s'en fuire"), of
"le peril à quoy", and of what "l'on penseroit".
