# Philip II to Bernardino de Mendoza, San Lorenzo, 7 September 1589 — the two letters of BnF Français 3641 (catalogue item 32) — **resolved 2026-09-17**

**Result.** Catalogue item 32 ("second letter of 7 Sep 1589, no. 6, only the first has a decipherment") is not an
undeciphered letter. Français 3641 holds the two letters Philip II sent Mendoza that day, each twice: the sent
original in the Spanish general cipher Cg.13 (Devos 1950; Tomokiyo "Cg.13 (1587)") and the 1589 decipherer's fair
copy, which imitates the original down to *Yo el Rey*, the countersignature *Don Martin de Idiaquez*, *Dupp.da* and
the address, and leaves the nomenclator groups it could not read underlined in the text and listed in the margin.

Both pairs were aligned group by group from the native-resolution Gallica images (`f10_cipher.txt` / `f14_clear.txt`,
`f12_cipher.txt` / `f76_clear.txt`). The alignment is consistent with the fragment of the Cg.13 table Tomokiyo
published (letters A 8/n, B 0, C 3, D 5, E 9/q/m; syllables ba–bu 99–95, ca–cu 70–74, la–lu 85–89, ma–mu 60–64) at
every one of the eleven syllables that occur in these letters, and extends it to some seventy syllables, five
ending marks and about fifty nomenclator groups (`key_cg13_partial.tsv`). So the identification rests on the content
of the leaves, not on either catalogue.

What is new, beyond the identification:

- **Nomenclator readings the 1589 decipherers left blank.** Letter A's margin lists ten unread groups (fer, tam, tam,
  ya, fer, fru, tur, san, cor, tum), letter B's fourteen (pur, mes, hon, bu, bor, mess, gu, dun, com, ga, Jam, lo,
  bem, par). Cross-reading the two letters settles several: **pur = respeto** (*por todos los respetos que se pueden
  considerar*; *por todo buen respeto me ha parescido avisaros*, where the copyist of A wrote "puerto"), **bor** is not *porque* as the copyist of A wrote (Phelippes reads *porque* as **dur** on f. 5); *pero* fits both letters, **mes =
  vuestro** (A: *a vuestras manos*; B: glossed "vro"), **san = negocio** (B: *a la forma que el negocio pidiere*,
  and *a los negocios con el cuydado que soleys* where the copyist wrote "a lo necessario" against the plural mark on
  88.; A: *para encaminar[se] el negocio, que seria tan a proposito*), **ya = copia** and **yan = orden** (context),
  and **tur** in A is a miscopy: the cipher has **tum** in both places (*el tum que esto puede tener ... algo del tum
  que tuviere*).
- **Two errors of the 1589 copyist.** In A he wrote *francia* for **dun** (*dos papeles que se han escapado de dun*)
  and also glossed **tam** as *francia*; the two groups cannot both be France. In B, **dun** is the country whose
  *armada* came back by a route known from prisoners taken by ships, whose *designios* are plotted *entre ellos y el
  de Bearne* and whose *armaçones* matter: England, in the summer the Drake–Norris fleet came home. So **dun =
  Inglaterra\*** and **tam = Francia\*** (Andrada, *pasados a Francia*, carrying letters *al Reyno de Francia y al
  principe de Bearne*, and *va con pensamiento de veros*, i.e. of seeing Mendoza in Paris). The copyist's
  *Rey de Inglaterra*, struck and replaced by the code, shows him hesitating over exactly this. In B, the copyist's
  *si se hiziesse alguna negociacion con el* stands where the cipher has 18 = **yo**: *si yo hiziesse*.
- **A struck passage.** In B the encipherer cancelled sixteen groups in line 4 (after *deve de haver*); the copyist
  silently omitted them. They are not read here.
- **Phelippes's own readings on f. 5** (Parma to Philip II, 30 Dec 1588, the same cipher with his interlinear
  decipherment) confirm the syllable values at every overlap and add **bur = persona, dom = dicho, dur = porque, pen =
  mucho, mis = V. M.**, and the x-row at 25–29.
- **fer = Portugués\***, unread by the decipherer and by Viète's office: it qualifies *Manuel de Andrada* and
  *Antonio de Goda*, and Viète's copy of a spy report in Cinq Cents de Colbert 33 f. 113 is headed *Avisos que ha
  venido a dar David, traducidos de [fer]* (Tomokiyo's list); "David" was Andrada's cover name (fr. 3641 no. 26,
  *avisos de Londres ... de los corriespondientes de David*).

What is still unread (asterisked proposals in the key file, none checkable without the Simancas table): tum
(*fundamento*?), fru (*posible*?), cor (*execucion*?), hon (*licencia*?; copyist: *diligencia*), gu, bu, com, ga
(*armada*?), Jam, lo, bem, par. Fourteen groups in two letters; every sentence reads around them.

## The letters, as read

**Letter A (f. 10 / f. 14).** *Dos papeles que se han escapado de [Inglaterra\*] refieren que salieron de alli en
compañia de Manuel de Andrada, [portugués\*], que segun les dixo, pasados a [Francia\*], y va a llevar dos cartas,
una al Reyno de [Francia\*] y otra al principe de Bearne; de [quien / manera] entendieron juntamente que las copias
desta carta vernian a parar a vuestras manos; y que Antonio de Goda, [portugués\*], que es castellano y governador
de un castillo y ysleta que esta a la boca del puerto de Premua [so the copyist; cipher 66 64 n], seria [fru] que
quisiesse hazerme algun servicio con entregarme lo que tiene a su cargo si yo hiziesse alguna negociacion con el. Y
pues el Manuel Andrada va con pensamiento de veros, ya avreys entendido el [tum] que esto puede tener, y a haver
alguno para encaminar[se] el negocio, que seria tan a proposito, no dudo que avreys procurado ponerlo en [cor],
porque por todo buen respeto me ha parescido avisaros desta particularidad que se me ha dado, y encargaros que me
aviseys si aveys entendido algo del [tum] que tuviere. De San Lorenço a 7 de setiembre 1589.*

Two papers smuggled out of England report that their writers left in company with Manuel de Andrada, a Portuguese,
who told them he had crossed to France and was carrying two letters, one for the French court and one for Navarre,
and that copies of the present letter would reach Mendoza; and that one Antonio de Goda, Portuguese, castellan of a
fort on an islet at a harbour mouth, might be willing to hand over his charge if the King negotiated with him. Since
Andrada means to see Mendoza, the King asks him to look into it and report.

**Letter B (f. 12 / f. 76).** Receipt of Mendoza's letters of 27 July, 2 and 8 August (not that of 30 July); the death
of *el Rey mi hermano* (Henry III, 2 August) *ha sido cosa de gran lastima ... por todos los respetos que se pueden
considerar*; with things in France in such confusion Mendoza is to suspend using the *[licencia\*]* he has to leave
the kingdom, *porque siendo tan importante vuestra [gu] en el [bu] para el buen assiento de las cosas*, he is in no
way to absent himself without a new order, but attend to *los negocios con el cuydado que soleys*, steering towards
*el bien publico, que es solo el fin que yo tengo*; there is a great want of news from *[Inglaterra\*]*, their
*[designios\*]* and plots, since the route of their *[armada\*]*'s return is known well enough from prisoners; Mendoza
is to keep up his *[lo]* and send word often, *y de creer es que entre ellos y el de Bearne se deven andar urdiendo
malas cosas en [bem] de la [par]; todo importa entenderlo, y en particular sus armaçones.*

## Dead ends and what was not done

- Native-resolution strips of f. 14 and f. 76 were fetched but the transcriptions of the clear copies rest on the
  2000-px images (legible hands); a second pass may correct a word or two of the copyists' Spanish.
- Phelippes's decipherment of f. 5 was read for the groups above; none of the fourteen open groups occurs there. Ff.
  143–148 (League papers in Cg.13), fr. 3977 and Viète's Cg.13 copies in Cinq Cents de Colbert 33 ff. 56–169 were not
  searched (Gallica throttling).
- "Premua" (cipher 66 64 n = pe/pre-mu-a) is not identified; the description (castle and islet at a harbour mouth,
  Portuguese castellan) suggests a Portuguese fort.
