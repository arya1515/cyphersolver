| R18 | 22/1 | 10 Aug [1588] (duplicate) | On publishing the Council of Trent: strong resistance everywhere over Gallican liberties. In Paris Morosini lobbied the Sorbonne and the royal council, and told Henri III that a Most Christian king should accept the council without conditions. The King answered that the clergy, above all the Archbishop of Lyon, were the ones opposing it. Also: the King is set on holding the Estates General; Guise's party is manoeuvring over the deputies; the Spanish ambassador complains that the English fleet was resupplied in French ports. | "si contentasse ancora di ricevere nel suo regno esto concilio liberamente, sì come è fatto negli altri regni" |
| R19 | 22/2 | 24 Aug [1588] (duplicate) | The clergy's deputies appeared before the Parlement to oppose the council as against Gallican liberty. In an audience the King defended his privileges and benefice nominations. He was told that the legation's faculties must be verified by the Parlement. A Cardinal (Bourbon/Vendôme) pressed for the absolution of his brothers. Factions are gathering followers for the Estates; there is fear of a bloody clash between Catholics; a marriage is proposed between the Count of Soissons and a daughter of the Duke of Guise. | "comparvero al parlamento per opporsi alla publicatione di esto concilio, dicendo che era contrario ... alla libertà della chiesa gallicana" |
| R20 | 22/3 | 9 Sept [1588] | The King gave the Count of Soissons letters clearing him of his service with Navarre and the Huguenots. Paris citizens and the prévôt protested to the Parlement. Cardinal de Vendôme and the Princess of Condé were furious. Morosini advised the King and Vendôme to wait for the Pope's absolution first, and they agreed to hold the letters back until it came. | "che le lettere non havessero a servire se non dopo che il conte fosse ... [assolto] da Nostro Signore" |
| R21 | 22/4 | n.d. [Sept 1588] | The King dismissed his secretaries of state and the chancellor all at once, leaving the court anxious. Mons. de Nevers(?) and the Queen Mother were displeased not to have been consulted. Morosini warned the King that new men would be inexperienced and the old ones could side with his enemies. The King replied that he meant to be obeyed and feared. Also: the King sent his physician to Épernon to get him to give up Boulogne and Metz. | "privandosi Sua Maestà in un istesso tempo di tutti quelli che intendono il vero stato delle cose sue" |
| R22 | 22/5 | n.d. [Sept 1588] (duplicate) | Replies to Montalto. Morosini has kept pressing for the council; the Estates promise to ask for it. His faculties will not be obeyed unless the Parlement verifies them. He held back the Count of Soissons' absolution because the Count refused the prescribed form of abjuration; the Cardinals of Bourbon and Vendôme then urged the King to write to Rome. Also: a Danish ambassador has come, perhaps to draw the King into defending the Queen of England; Morosini rebuts the vice-legate's complaints and answers on Épernon and La Valette. | "non volendo a modo alcuno contentare il conte di Soissons, dicendo che non si deve accusare di peccato che non ha" |
| R27 | 22/6 | n.d. (fragment) | On governments for Épernon (Saintonge, Angoulême, Cognac) and on Boulogne. Épernon's side refuses to give anything back and is said to deal with the Queen of England and Navarre. Morosini hopes for peace between Guise and the others, though with little confidence. | "si mostrano risolutissimi di non volere restituire alcuna cosa" |
| R28 | 22/7 | [early] Oct [1588] (duplicate) | While walking in the palace garden with the King, the Queen Mother and the Cardinals of Bourbon and Vendôme, Morosini learned that the King would send Signor Girolamo Gondi to Rome about pulling Marshal Montmorency away from the Huguenots. Morosini urged the King to win Guise over with money, since Guise is heavily in debt; Guise refused the grant offered. Vendôme reports that Cardinal Joyeuse worked against the Count of Soissons in Rome. | "haveva esortato Sua Maestà a fare ogni opera per ritirare dall'amicitia delli ugonotti quel signore" |
| R29 | 22/8 | 10 Oct [1588] (duplicate) | A messenger is back from Florence about the Grand Duke's marriage to the Princess of Lorraine; Orazio Rucellai will conclude it, and the Queen Mother plans to escort her to Marseille. News that the Duke of Savoy has seized the whole marquisate of Saluzzo; Guise fears this will force peace with Navarre and the heretics. Morosini asks for orders; a Danish ambassador is dealing over merchants' goods. | "questo accidente sarà la rovina della religione cattolica" |
| R30 | 22/9 | 3 Oct [1588], Blois | Morosini took on the job of reconciling the Duke of Guise and Mons. de Nevers, who have been at odds since they married sisters; with the King's approval, both promised to follow his advice. He pressed Guise and the Cardinal of Guise on publishing the council. The clergy is split: the lower deputies want Gallican liberty reserved. He is also working against any alienation of church property. | "disunione ... che era causa ... di mali officii non senza grave pregiudicio della quiete di questo regno" |
| R31 | 22/10 | 23 Oct [1588], Blois | Mandelot, governor of Lyon, is ordered to arrest a man for transfer to Avignon. The Cardinal of Guise asks for the vice-legation of Avignon. The Florentine marriage contract is going forward. The King will send Gondi to Rome about Montmorency, and is embittered over Savoy and Saluzzo. The Épernon and Provence troubles continue. The Estates have sworn a fundamental law keeping only the Catholic religion; the Parlement will not accept the legation faculties. | "per legge fondamentale del regno irrevocabile si è stabilito di mantener sempre la sola religione cattolica" |
| R32 | 22/11 | 24 Oct [1588] | Savoy is keeping Saluzzo and is suspected of designs on Marseille. M. de Poigny is sent to the Duke to demand restitution. Morosini protests over Cardinal Lenoncourt's indult and over church revenues in the Cambrai truce, and the Queen Mother answers him. The King explains why Épernon was removed from Provence, and promises to raise publication of the council "tomorrow". | "ho ancora di nuovo ricordato a Sua Maestà la publicatione del concilio di Trento" |
| R33 | 22/12 | n.d. [Oct-Nov 1588] | The King referred the council to the clergy's congregation. There the chapters, bishops and others quarrelled over exemptions, and they finally asked for publication with four reservations (royal authority, Gallican liberty, chapter exemptions, particular privileges). Morosini argued that it must be published without conditions. The King answered that the Gallican church has privileges it cannot lose. Morosini asks Rome for precise orders. | "si doveva fare senza alcuna eccettione" |
| R34 | 22/13 | n.d. [Nov 1588] | The Spanish ambassador pressed the Queen Mother for union and good understanding between Philip II and Henri III, and showed a copy of Philip's letter. Morosini sees a real opening for the alliance and asks that it be kept secret. The ambassador blamed Savoy for the Saluzzo attack. Gondi leaves for Rome; Morosini commends the Archbishop of Lyon. There is new friction with Guise over the governor of Orléans. | "introdurre una buona intelligenza et unione fra il suo et questo re" |
| R35 | 22/14 | n.d. | A copy, in Spanish, of Philip II's letter to the Queen Mother (given to her by the Spanish ambassador). It urges closer union and friendship between the two crowns against the heretics, for God's service and obedience to the Roman Church. | "exortando a las dos coronas a mayor unión y amistad" |
| R36 | 22/15 | 10 Nov [1588] (duplicate) | A fever kept Morosini indoors. The Savoy ambassador reported the Poigny talks to the Queen Mother: the Duke will not return the Saluzzo places, and claims to have acted by the Pope's order (Carmagnola). Morosini firmly denies that the Pope had any part in it. The King is resolved on war rather than bear the insult; Morosini asks for guidance. | "tutto quello che ha fatto è di ordine di Nostro Signore" |
| R37 | 22/16 | 21 Nov [1588] (duplicate) | Morosini urged the King and Queen Mother to settle Saluzzo peacefully. The Savoy ambassador had offered to restore the places if they were given to a Catholic knight, with Nemours as governor. Guise advised force instead, sending Mayenne into Bresse. The King inclined to Morosini's view and asked that a lieutenant also be named. The Spanish ambassador denies that Philip backs Savoy. | "il Duca di Guisa consigliava il contrario ... bisognava attendere ad ingrossare le forze al Duca di Mayenne" |
| R38 | 22/17 | n.d. [late Nov-Dec 1588] | The Savoy ambassador complained that Mayenne was ordered into Bresse. Morosini urged the King not to start a war before trying every other way, and to fight the heretics first. The King said he gave Mayenne no such order, but called the Duke of Savoy ungrateful. Also: the Cardinal of Guise and the oath for the Avignon legation; Lenoncourt's indult; a truce with the Dauphiné Huguenots is being considered. | "dissuadendola a non volere principiare una guerra se prima non havesse tentata ogni altra via" |
| R39 | 22/18 | 8 Dec [1588] (duplicate) | A courier from Turin came from Poigny. In the council, Nevers insisted on war with Savoy even if Saluzzo were restored; the others would forgive the Duke if he gave the places back. Munitions and artillery are to be sent to Lyon. Nevers also argued that Spain would not intervene. | "quando ancora il signor Duca di Savoia restituisse il marchesato, ... di farli la guerra" |

Notes: "mons di nuisa" in the decipherment is read as Nevers (marked (?) in R21); in R28 it may instead be Guise. Years are inferred from the series (1588).
