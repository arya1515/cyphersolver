| R40 | 22/19 | dup., "li 8 X[bre]" (8 Dec 1588?), [Blois] | The King's council discusses the Duke of Savoy's artillery and troops on the borders of Bresse and Dauphiné and resolves to levy Swiss and 1,500 horse to join the Duke of Mayenne; the Duke of Guise offers 10,000 scudi, and the Estates' treasurers promise money. Morosini goes to the Capuchins hoping to find Henri III and complains that the King no longer talks openly with him. He reports that the King is tightly bound to Guise and is said to have dismissed his heads of finance. | "fu risoluto di spedir subito per fare una leuata di tremilia suizeri" |
| R41 | 22/20 | n.d. [Blois, Dec 1588] | The Duke of Savoy has seized the Marquisate of Saluzzo and offers to give it back once the Dauphiné is free of Huguenots, with his wife or a son as a hostage. The Duke of Guise tells Morosini he must press the King to recover Saluzzo by force, or else retire from court; the Savoyard ambassador has been refused. Mayenne asks what to do, with the Savoyard enemy so near. | "egli non puo per honor suo lasciare di far ogni sforzo per uendicare l'ingiuria" |
| R42 | 22/21 | n.d. [Blois, Dec 1588] | Morosini tells the King at the Capuchins that the Pope is displeased the Council of Trent has not been published; the King answers through Gondi. Guise complains that the King does not trust him, and Morosini urges the King to satisfy Guise. He also reports on the papal bull against procurers of abortion and says the Queen Mother has a fever. | "nela publicatione del concilio di Trento Sua Maesta non habia ancora presa quella risolutione" |
| R43 | 22/22 | Blois, 23 Dec [1588] | Guise is killed with daggers in the King's chamber on the King's order, after warnings that he meant harm to the King. The Cardinal of Guise, the Prince de Joinville, the Dukes of Nemours and Elbeuf, the Archbishop of Lyon, the prévôt des marchands and a Paris president are arrested, and the Cardinal de Bourbon is put under guard. The Queen Mother is left speechless, the city is locked down, Morosini seeks an audience in vain, and the Cardinal of Guise is feared dead too. | "il Duca di Guisa nela camera del Re e stato per ordine di Sua Maesta amazato a colpi di pugnale" |
| R44 | 22/23 | dup., 24 Xbre [1588], [Blois] | The Cardinal of Guise was put to death only this morning. Because he was a churchman, Morosini leaves the response to the Pope's judgment. The Cardinal de Bourbon is under guard and ill, and Joinville and the Archbishop of Lyon are to go to Amboise. Guise's secretary, under examination, said the Pope had written to Guise in favour of Savoy over Saluzzo, which angered the King. The King tells the legate he is now king and must be obeyed. | "questa matina solamente e rimasto priuo di uita" |
| R45 | 22/24 | n.d. [Blois, Jan 1589, after Catherine de' Medici's death] | Morosini defends his conduct. The Archbishop of Lyon refuses lay examiners because he is primate of France. The Estates' article excluding Navarre carries a clause reserving the Bourbon succession. Morosini offers condolences on the Queen Mother's death and presses the King to declare Navarre a heretic and incapable, since an envoy of Navarre is at court seeking an accord. He also urges publication of Trent without conditions. | "dechiarare il Nauarro heretico et incapace ala sucessione di questo regno" |
| R46 | 22/25 | Blois, 24 Jan 1589 (dup.) | The Pope rejects the clause "saluis iuribus regis et regni", and Morosini works on the King's confessor and the Keeper of the Seals, who promise to argue his case. Saluzzo is set aside for now. At length Morosini complains of the Pope's displeasure and the false reports reaching Rome, and he begs leave to come to Rome to justify himself. | "non solo non accetta la clausula saluis iuribus regis et regni" |
| R47 | 22/26 | n.d. [Blois, Jan 1589] | Morosini continues his defence and denies ever dealing with the English ambassador. He explains why the Council could not be forced through the parlements and the Estates. He had suggested that the King publish it at once and then send a personage to Rome to ask for his privileges. | "l'Ambassadore d'Inghilterra il quale ne conosco ne so mai di hauere ueduto" |
| R48 | 22/27 | Blois, 30 & 31 Jan 1589 | Morosini again asks for his leave. The Parisians have put themselves under Mayenne's protection, and the King is leaving for Amboise with his prisoners. Morosini protests about moving the Cardinal de Bourbon and the Archbishop of Lyon. The King says he will keep the Cardinal comfortably in a fine castle, and agrees to send letters ordering all bishops to publish the Council of Trent without conditions. | "che lei accetta il sodetto concilio di Trento et che loro lo facesero publicare" |
| R49 | 22/28 | n.d. (fragment) [early 1589] | The Archbishop of Lyon refuses to answer the royal commissioners or a bishop, saying he is primate of France. His relatives ask Morosini to plead with the King not to prejudice ecclesiastical dignity and to refer the case to the Pope. | "egli era primate di Francia et che non poteua esser esaminato" |
| R51 | 22/30 | Tours, 27 March 1589 | A count's gentleman brings news that two cavalry companies of Mayenne's (under Sagona and Farlandra, who was badly wounded) have been routed and their baggage taken. Orléans and Paris are in the League's hands, and Mayenne remains in Paris. The King wants peace, while Navarre is taking places. Guards have been placed on Morosini's house. | "Sua Maesta desidera grandemente la pace" |
| R52 | 22/31 | Tours, 6 April 1589 | The castellan of Angers refuses the League's bribe, and Marshal d'Aumont secures the castle for the King, who writes him a letter in his own hand. The count is sent to Brittany against the Duke of Mercoeur; Alfonso Corso is in Auvergne; Épernon's command is disputed; Mayenne is still in Paris. The King keeps Holy Week and Easter devotions and touches for the scrofula. | "il castelo che e riputato il piu forte di Francia" |
| R53 | 22/32 | n.d. [Tours, spring 1589] | **Read only in part (second key, not fully reconstructed).** In the clear part, Morosini waits anxiously for letters from Rome granting his leave. The cipher part says the vice-legate of Avignon has arrived at Tours and lodged near Plessis, and that heretics and Catholics now mix at court. Something is said about Mayenne near Le Mans. | "si e fata una mescolanza ala corte di heretici et di catolici" |
| R54 | 22/33 | Moulins, 11 May 1589 | **Read only in part (second key, not fully reconstructed).** Only fragments can be read: heretics are gathering strength at court alongside the Catholics. Morosini voices personal fears and asks that something be made known. | "mescolati gli heretici co i catolici ala corte" |
| R55 | 22/34 | Moulins, 11 June 1589 | A gentleman of the Grand Duchess of Tuscany reports that the Comte de Soissons (?) was captured in bed by the Duke of Mercoeur, and that a League count was killed by a gunpowder explosion. The King is marching on Paris, where Mayenne is and the city is barricaded, with 4,000 horse and 17,000 foot; Swiss and Casimir's cavalry are coming, and Épernon has taken Montereau. Morosini also passes on, with doubts, a rumour of a plot to kill the King. | "fu fato prigione dal Duca di Mercurio" |
| R56 | 22/35 | Moulins, 16(?) June 1589 | Morosini is distressed by Montalto's letter about his secretary. The monitorium has reportedly been posted at St Peter's, and Cardinal Joyeuse and the French ambassador are leaving Rome. Morosini thinks of retiring to Lyon, but the Lyonnais will let him stay only three days. Moulins has sworn loyalty to the King before M. de la Guiche; the Archbishop of Lyon may be freed; Alfonso Corso has crossed the Rhône. | "pensaria di ritirarmi a Lione per metermi in sicuro" |
| R57 | 22/36 | n.d. (enclosures dated "dix di giugno" [1589], Moulins) | This record holds copies of papers sent to M. de la Guiche. The first says the King's Swiss army (about 12,000 Swiss and 2,000 lansquenets, some 17–18,000 men with 12 guns) is marching, paid with money lent by Strasbourg. The second is the oath of the inhabitants of Moulins to live and die Catholic and loyal to "Henri, by the grace of God King of France and Poland". | "prestato il giuramento di fedelta al Re nostro sire Henrico" |
| R58 | 22/37 | Moulins, 5 July 1589 (dup.) | The King has taken Jargeau ("Giorgio") and its Loire bridge, hanged a captain and pardoned the governor. Other Loire towns have surrendered, and the King is heading for Paris, where four faubourgs are said to favour him. Mayenne has taken Montereau and Nemours is raising money at Lyon for the League's Swiss. The count is said to have escaped from Nantes castle in a basket, which Morosini doubts. | "si dice che il Re habia preso Giorgio" |
