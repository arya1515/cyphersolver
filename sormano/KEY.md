# Sormano cipher key (BnF fr. 3096, ff. 105–124)

Key reconstructed by George Lasry (2023), published as an image on Tomokiyo's
Cryptiana page "French Ciphers during the Reign of Francis I"
(https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/francis.htm, key image code/GL/BnF_fr3096_f113.png,
local copy `key_lasry3.png`). Verified 17 Sept 2026 against the interlinear
decipherment on f. 113r (no. 62): first crib line decodes
"de vn capitaneo suizari ..." exactly.

Simple substitution with homophones, no nulls observed, continuous script.

Notes:
- п (a) vs π (n): the n-form's top bar overhangs both legs; the a-form is square.
- τ (b) vs T (v): the v-form has the large serifed top bar.
- x (t) vs ✗ (x): the x-plain form is lighter, the saltire heavier; x is rare in Italian.
- No separate z, k, w, y in the key; z appears rarely and may be spelled with s.
- Letters u/v share homophones (Italian usage).

## Corrections established on the leaves, 17 Sept 2026

**The fourth E homophone is a crossbarred long-s, and it is not `f`.**
It is written as a long `ſ` carrying a horizontal crossbar, so it reads as an
`f` to the eye. `f` proper is the *double* loop `ſſ`. Ground truth on f. 113r
(no. 62): the cipher run glossed `de vn capitaneo suizari` opens
`ꜿ f T π Δ V 6 p x υ π Γ 1`, and the crossbarred long-s in second position sits
under the `e` of the gloss's `de`. Reading this form as `f` corrupts roughly one
letter in twelve, and it is the single most costly misreading in this hand.

**There is a null.** A tall sign with a large top loop and a long left-curving
descender (`ɧ`) occurs throughout nos. 63, 65 and 66 and twice in the second
glossed run of no. 62. It is *not in Lasry's published table* and carries no
letter value. Three checks, each exact only when the sign is dropped:

| cipher | plain |
|---|---|
| `H 1 Ǝ x ɧ Q п` | vostra |
| `Δ ɧ 1 π ʃ ʈ Q x ʒ` | conforto |
| `Δ E ɧ k` | che |

The earlier note "no nulls observed" is therefore wrong and has been struck.

**The large double circle `◎` is a nomenclator, standing for DUCA** (Alfonso
I d'Este). It must not be confused with `⊙`, the single circle with a centre
dot, which is an ordinary `i` homophone — `⊙` is the `i` of `aquila`
(`V ▽ H ⊙ ϙ υ`) on f. 113r. `◎` resolves every context in which it occurs:
`con questo ◎` = con questo Duca; `fusimo col deto ◎` = fusimo col detto Duca;
`andai dal deto ◎` = andai dal detto Duca. Because it often falls where the
clear hand resumes, it can be mistaken for a passage terminator; it is not.

Forms still easily confused, and to be resolved from the Italian rather than the
shape: ɣ (e, 4th form) vs ɣ (v); ϙ/9 (l) vs 4 (i); ʒ-with-ring (o) vs 3 (a);
ϰ (e) vs π (n).
