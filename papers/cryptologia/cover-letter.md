Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript "Reading Armstrong's Coded Postscript to Madison (1808): Reconstructing an Unpublished United States Diplomatic Code from Archival Pencil Decodes" for consideration as an article in *Cryptologia*.

The paper completes the reading of a coded postscript that John Armstrong, United States Minister to France, added to a private letter to Secretary of State James Madison on 30 August 1808. The postscript is printed undeciphered in *Founders Online* and stands on Satoshi Tomokiyo's list of unsolved historical ciphers, where about a third of its groups had been read from one letter with known plaintext. The code, a numerical one-part system of some 1,600 elements used at the Paris legation from 1804 to 1810, was never published and no code book is known to survive.

The contribution is the source and the method rather than the plaintext. The State Department decoded incoming despatches interlinearly in pencil, and those pencil decodes survive on National Archives microfilm M34. An image score for faint pencil locates the annotated frames among 393; 607 code groups are recovered, each with a confidence grade; the alphabetical layout of the code fixes most of the remainder; and a second coded letter of 1808 serves as an independent check. The postscript reads in full. Two digits in the printed transcription are corrected from the manuscript. The full table is given in an appendix and released with the paper, and it makes some forty other coded Armstrong despatches readable.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published. I should disclose that an informal account of the result, without the tables in this paper, has been posted on my personal website and reported to Mr Tomokiyo, who has marked the item as solved on his list with a link to that account. The manuscript is my own work.

I have prepared the main document anonymously for double-anonymised review, with author details on a separate title page. The paper uses the Taylor & Francis Interact template with Chicago author-date references. It has two figures, both of public-domain archival material (Library of Congress, James Madison Papers; National Archives, Record Group 59), and two tables plus an appendix table.
