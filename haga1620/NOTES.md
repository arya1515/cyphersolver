## System

Simple substitution for letters with a 24-sign alphabet of Latin-looking signs (the letter shapes are reused for
other values: e is written like 7, r as d, s as z, n as t, t as a long ʃ), and a numeric nomenclator of 116
codes written in parentheses, e.g. (44). The key is on R2118 and fits at once: φdK7ɛℓ7t = *arbeiden*,
ʃδ22897t = *tusschen*, 2δ2ɛu7t2ɛ7 = *suspensie*. The letters are mostly clear Dutch with enciphered spans.
DECODE's note about "zodiacal and alchemistic signs" does not match what the key and letters show.

Nomenclator codes 37-45 sit in a column cut off by the photograph's edge; their numbers are inferred from order.

## Result

Both catalogued ciphertexts read, 2026-09-21, in one session, from the key on R2118.

The two records hold **four despatches**, not two: the scans are bound out of order and each record carries
more than one letter.

- **R2113**: p.1 is the clear ending of the letter of **1 Feb 1620** (no cipher). Pages 3 and 2, in that order,
  are a different letter, dated **31 March 1620** at the foot of p.2; its opening page is not in the scans.
- **R2115**: page order is p.3 (opening), p.2, p.4 (which ends a despatch of **24 April 1620** and starts the
  next), p.1 (close, **4 May 1620**).

What the cipher hides is Haga's advice on the Bohemian revolt and on the Spanish truce:

- 31 March: the Porte would let Transylvania be resigned to Bethlen Gábor's brother; Transylvania must stay in
  good correspondence with Hungary so that it does not fall to Homonnai "ofte anderen dependenten van t huijs
  van Oostenrijck"; the Turks would think better of that side if the Hungarians also elected *regem Bohemiae*
  and stayed in strict alliance with [62] (Transylvania), Hungary and Bohemia united under the Elector
  Palatine. Then: [44] and [45] are working through [4] (the Capitan Bassa) for a suspension of arms between
  the Turks and the kingdoms of Naples and Sicily; [3], [4] and [7] (Grand Vizier, Capitan Bassa, Mufti) ask
  him urgently whether [50] means to extend the truce with [70] (Spain), make peace, or take up arms again —
  the Porte would gladly see the last, "om Spangie van weder zyde te mogen infesteren".
- 24 April / 4 May: the envoys of the confederated Bohemian and Hungarian lands from the Pressburg Easter diet
  were received without delay by the Grand Vizier and the Sultan, and the Hungarian Estates' ambassador, held
  back until then by the Imperial ambassador's persuasion, was admitted with them; the Porte is resolved to
  treat with the crown of Hungary, Bohemia and the confederated lands. In cipher Haga urges that the Hungarian
  Estates proceed "op het alderdesperaetste tot de electie ende croeninge van een nieuw coninck"; order has
  gone to [19] (the Bassa of Buda) to keep good correspondence with them and to assist "sonder andere last";
  nothing done in the name of [43] (the Emperor's ambassador) is to be believed; and that no peace be made
  with [43].

Code values, read off R2118, are confirmed by use: [19] Buda, [62] Transylvania, [70] Spain, [3] Vizier Azem,
[4] Capitan Bassa, [7] Mufti, [58] the States General, [43] the Emperor's extraordinary ambassador.

Open:

- **[50]**: the key column reads 50 as the French king's ordinary ambassador, which does not fit "de treffves
  met [70] te verlengen". Either the numbering of that column is off by one or two rows in the photograph, or
  the code is used loosely. Unresolved.
- Codes 37-45 are in the column cut off by the photograph, so [44] and [45] rest on the inferred order.
- A few short cipher words: `ℓ7tδℓ7d7t` (R2113 p.2 line 13, reads "denvderen", perhaps for "hinderen"), the
  opening phrase of the ciphered advice on R2115 p.3, and several `?`-marked spans in both readings.
- The clear Dutch was read for sense, not transcribed verbatim.
- **m** is the "4"-shaped sign: the key's m cell is off the photographed sheet, and this fills it (becomen,
  met, mijn, vermogen, teenemael, geconformeert).

R2114 (11 May 1620, 6 pp.) is marked Decrypted on DECODE and was not re-read here.
