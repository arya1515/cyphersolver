## Modern English summary

Haga reports to the States General on the Hungarian–Bohemian embassy at the Ottoman Porte. Envoys of
the Bohemian and Hungarian "contreyen" (the confederated estates), sent from the Easter diet at
Pressburg (Bratislava), have arrived in Constantinople with letters and reports of what was agreed
there. They intend to send a formal embassy to the Porte and to conclude a perpetual peace with the
Sultan. They were received quickly and without delay, given audience by the Grand Vizier and by the
Sultan himself, at which not only the envoys of the confederated lands but also the ambassador of the
Hungarian estates — whom the Imperial (Habsburg) ambassador had until then managed to keep back by
persuasion — was admitted. The Porte answered that the ambassadors to be sent will be welcome and
that it is resolved to treat and make friendship with the Crown of Hungary, Bohemia and the
confederated lands.

In cipher Haga stresses that nothing is more necessary than that the Hungarian estates proceed at
once — "op het alderdesperaetste" — to the election and coronation of a new king (i.e. Bethlen Gabor,
named in clear elsewhere on the page). Order has already been given (code [19]) to keep up good
correspondence with them and to assist them without needing further instructions from here; nothing
that may be done in the name of [43] (apparently the Emperor/Imperial side) is to be believed, and
they are to be warned that no peace be made with [43] and not to wait any longer over the crown of
Hungary. Haga also ciphers a passage on an embassy/mission (of the pope?) and on countermeasures,
saying that through "our devoirs" it will come to nothing, and on the "practijcken" (intrigues) that
are being pursued. A later passage deals with the attitude of Venice, [62] and [46] and with what he
himself has pressed for at the Porte.

The last page, still partly ciphered, concerns Poland: the Polish ambassador has finally obtained
licence; Cossack/Polish raiding and captives; reports that the Khan of Tartary has marched in person
with his whole force and fallen upon Poland; Ottoman preparations continue, but since neither the
Sultan nor the Grand Vizier will go in person, progress will be slow. Dated Constantinople, 4 May 1620.

## Signs that did not fit the key

- **m**: the key's missing letter. It is the "4"-shaped sign: {47ʃ} = "met", {δ7d4φt7t} = "vermanen",
  {ʃ77t74φʀ} = "teenemael", {x7ɟt?ɟd4 77dʃ} = "geconformeert". So **m = 4**.
- The **p** sign (key: "u/ɕ-like") and the **i** sign (ɛ/e-like) are hard to tell apart in this hand;
  most of the remaining `?` marks come from that pair, e.g. in {ɕdφ8ʃ…} = "practijcken" vs
  {ℓɐ7} = "die".
- **ij** is written as two i-signs: {ʃɐɐʃ ʃɟʃ ʃɐɐʃ} = "tijt tot tijt"; {2ɐɐt} = "sijn".
- The cross-shaped signs for **o** (ϯ) and **n** (t-like) are nearly identical at this resolution and
  are the other main source of uncertainty.
- No sign appeared that is outside the key once m = 4 is admitted.
