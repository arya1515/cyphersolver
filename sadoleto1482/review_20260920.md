# Sadoleto: re-examination of 20 September 2026

Status: in progress. This is an extension and correction of the partial reading,
not an end-to-end solution of the four requested documents.

**Later continuation:** see `verified_groups.md` for verification of `dom`
and X's Venetian-government referent; these supersede the provisional labels
below. `cipher_inventory.md` adds another cipher line on R1102 page 4 and

## The important coverage correction

The seven cipher lines previously transcribed from R1102 are **not the complete
ciphertext of that letter**. In `img/IMG_R1102_I5657_P2.png` (4320 by 3240), they
are at the bottom of the left manuscript page, labelled 2. The facing page,
labelled 3, has a four-line cipher continuation at the top and substantial
additional cipher interspersed with clear writing farther down. Vestigia 1286,
image `ASMO Ambasciatori Busta 1 (110).JPG`, shows the same spread.

Consequently, the previous description of R1102 as a seven-line cipher and the
published phrase "the last seven lines" underestimate the unread material. A
fraction solved cannot be computed from that seven-line transcription.

## Additional readings of R1102

Conventions here follow README: C = read using the key reconstructed from
contemporary known plaintext; M = doubtful reading; I = contextual inference.
The older key's H/M/L column expresses its own confidence scale and must not be
confused with the repository's H = supplied by a primary key source.

### Page 3, opening four cipher lines

Conservative working text (punctuation and spaces editorial):

> ducati. ma voleva potere [soldare] et [cavare gente]
> de questo regno. et questo l'aveva saputo sua [S.]
> per [subtil via] et non volse per cosa del mondo che
> io monstrasse saperne cosa alcuna.

This is a **working transcription**, not a claim that every sign has been
independently checked. `ducati`, `ma voleva potere`, `de questo regno`, and the
closing secrecy clause are readable with the existing key (C). The bracketed
phrases and the abbreviation after `sua` remain M. No expanded identity is
assigned to that abbreviation.

Most securely, the first word supplies the currency after the previous page's
`cento milia`: **100,000 ducats**. It no longer needs to be supplied solely from
the 31 August letter. The first two lines appear to add a condition about
recruiting/removing troops from this kingdom; that interpretation remains
provisional pending a complete glyph transcription of the bracketed words.

Source rectangle: (2280, 365, 4150, 665), in original pixel coordinates. Native
crop `img/continuation_top.png` covers most of the passage; `img/cont0.png` and
`img/cont1.png` preserve enlarged overlapping halves.

### Page 3, middle monetary passage

Readable extracts, with omissions explicitly retained:

> [...] cento milia
> ducati ogni anno sino a sei o septe anni [...]
> questa suma che li domanda et non seria gran suma alla
> liga considerata la [infinita utilita] [...]

The annual payment and `sino a sei o septe anni` are C readings from the image.
The bracketed description of its utility is M. These are parts of a **later
passage**, separated from the opening Venetian-offer report by clear text. Do
not attach the six/seven-year term to the Venetian offer without completing
that intervening text and its referents.

Source rectangles: (2320, 1350, 4240, 1460) for the annual duration,
(2320, 1450, 4240, 1595) for the following two lines. Crops:
`img/money0.png`, `img/money1.png`.

### Page 2, informant's title

The old ASCII-like label `494` is visually misleading. The first sign resembles
the looped-stem d immediately preceding it in `da`; the middle sign resembles
the comma-shaped o in nearby `como`; the last resembles the 4-shaped m. Thus
**dom Francesco** is a plausible alphabetic reading (M), without a new code
entry. The last sign still needs independent comparison, and the expansion to
`don` or `dom[ino]` is not established by this observation alone.

A strong contextual candidate for the informant is **Francesco d'Aragona** (I),
not an otherwise unidentified cleric. The 20 June letter printed by Mátyus,
Vestigia III (2020), p. 160, explicitly places Don Francesco, Ferrante's son,
with Sadoleto at Buda. Ercole's 26 September reply (Vestigia 1250, second image)
also mentions Don Francesco. Neither reference is itself a decipherment of
R1102; the identification remains a candidate.

The repeated group formerly rendered `3ί2ии` remains **X**. Neither "Venetiani"
nor "Signoria" is verified as its literal expansion. Inspection of the R1101
known-plaintext passage did not establish a matching sign sequence. Likewise,
new guesses for the connective words in page 2 lines 3–4 were not accepted.

## R1103 and prior art

The five cached Vestigia 1294 images were surveyed together, and the existing
enhanced line strips of the four-line cipher were re-examined. The faint block
remains ambiguous; no new continuous sentence was accepted. The cached images
include repeated photographs and do not provide a new clear-text witness.
This establishes a limitation of this attempt, not that software enhancement
or further archival work can never recover the text.

The literature check reopened [Vestigia III](https://real.mtak.hu/119767/9/Vestigia3.pdf):
Mátyus p. 150 supports the omission of the Sadoleto correspondence from the old
publication programme, while his pp. 160–163 print the separate 20 June letter.
Fresh searches for Sadoleto with cipher/decipherment terms and on Tomokiyo's
cryptiana domain found no independent matching solution; the project's own
19 September page repeatedly surfaced and was not treated as corroboration.
This search is not proof that no decipherment exists anywhere.
