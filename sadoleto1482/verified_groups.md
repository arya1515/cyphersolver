## The title is alphabetic `dom`

R1102, manuscript page 2, cipher line 2, immediately after `da`:

| Sign | Value | Direct local comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Looped stem | d | Immediately preceding `da` |
| Comma-shaped sign | o | First o of `como` later on the same line |
| 4-shaped sign | m | m in that same `como`, also m in `milia` |

The literal result is **dom Francesco** (C). This is not an unread three-digit
nomenclator entry. Preserve `dom` in diplomatic transcription; expansion to
`don` or `domino` is editorial. Identifying Francesco as Francesco d'Aragona
remains I, supported by the separately published 20 June letter and Ercole's
26 September reply, not by a surname in this cipher passage.

coordinates. The working key's d/o/m values predate this identification.

## X refers to the Signoria of Venice

The distinctive group previously approximated as `3ί2ии` occurs twice in
R1102's first seven cipher lines. A matching occurrence is now located in
**R1106, manuscript page 3, cipher line 3**, following the alphabetic words
`in guera a`.

The contemporary clear witness **26b / Vestigia 4004** has the corresponding
sentence in its second line. It explicitly names the **Signoria di Venetia**
in a phrase concerning war with it. This verifies the group's referent as
Venice's government (C), independently of R1102's offer and its historical
plausibility.

The clear witness is not letter-for-letter identical with the cipher's
surrounding wording: compare cipher `in guera a X` with the clear copy's
`in guerra cum la [abbreviated Signoria] di Venetia`. Therefore the secure
result is the **semantic value**, not an assertion that X expands to one
particular spelling or grammatical phrase. In readable editions use
`[Signoria di Venetia]`, marking this as the expansion of a single group.

## Other small corrections to the seven-line reading

The unboxed strips `img/L/b2_L05_2.png` and `b2_L06_0.png` show the word divided
at the manuscript line end as `fi- / gliolo`: **figliolo**, not `figlio`.
The following parenthesis is present in the manuscript, not supplied by the
editor. Its reading remains `(e bastardo)`.

`b2_L06_1.png` reads **per loro capitaneo**: the third sign of `loro` is the
r sign, not the plain c stroke of the previous `loco` transcription.
The adjacent `b2_L06_2.png` supports **de l'armata** (editorial apostrophe),
followed on the next line by `per mare`. These readings use the existing
alphabet (C).

The difficult connective phrase at the end of line 3 and start of line 4 is
still not securely read. It must remain a gap rather than being reconstructed
solely from grammatical expectation. Resolving the two groups above does not
make R1102 or the four-letter target an end-to-end solution.
