# R1106 and the contemporary decipherment slips

20 September 2026. Status: in progress. This is a paragraph-level witness map,
not a complete glyph-by-glyph collation. It distinguishes the existence and
scope of prior reading from the completeness of the present transcription.

## Alignment map

Coordinates below refer to the original 4320 × 3240 photographs and enclose
regions, not tight word boxes. Spelling in the anchor column is a readable
transcription; it is not a claim of letter-for-letter agreement between witnesses.

| Page 3, last mixed passage, approximately `(2360,1370,4170,1770)` | 26b, closing lines | Last cipher-bearing lines include `potro stare nel...`; following clear lines end the paragraph with `lo officio mio...`, also present at the end of 26b | Matching ending region; the subsequent original paragraph beginning `Cossi...` is ordinary clear prose |

The two slips thus track **all three principal cipher-bearing paragraph
regions identified in the original**, including the continuation across the
page break. No separate unmatched cipher region was found in this survey.
That is stronger evidence than merely finding a single matching code group,
but it does not establish that the slips preserve every cipher word. The
profile's `plaintext.partial` remains `unknown` until the internal spans have
been collated. It would also be misleading to say that 26a alone is a complete
clear copy of R1106: it ends before page 3's cipher.

## Reading method and remaining uncertainty

The primary colour images were inspected. A blue-channel local-background
contrast view, `img/clear26b_full_contrast.png`, makes the faded right side of
26b easier to examine. It subtracts a Gaussian-blurred background (radius 18)
and amplifies local darkness fivefold; no strokes were generated or filled in.
This is a viewing aid, not an independent witness.

The first clear witness and the cipher can differ in wording or order. The
already verified `in guera a X` / `in guerra cum la Signoria di Venetia`
correspondence demonstrates why literal reconstruction should not simply copy
the slip. A full edition still needs parallel transcription of each encrypted
span and its clear counterpart, with variants retained. This map makes that
work finite and identifies the appropriate photograph for each region.
