# Additional primary control: 24 June 1482

Status: in progress. This is a supporting letter, not one of the four targets.

Vestigia 1280, ASMo Ambasciatori Ungheria b. 1, Sadoleto no. 4,
24 June 1482, to Ercole d'Este. The previously recorded literature lead
reported a marginal decipherment. That annotation is now directly visible.

All eight catalogued web images were retrieved by `fetch_june_control.py`.
The files are `img/v/control1280_1.jpg` through `_8.jpg`. The contact sheet
`img/control1280_contact.jpg` shows three principal views: first page,
middle spread, and address side, with duplicate versions. The middle spread
is photograph `(96)`; its downloaded web image is **4096 × 3072**, so the
4320 × 3240 coordinates used for other cached originals do not apply here.

The cipher occurs near the end of page 3, just above the date and closing.
Small ordinary writing in the left margin sits beside the cipher passage.
Both are visible in `img/control1280_cipher_context.png`, extracted from
`control1280_2.jpg` at `(2050,1570,4050,2250)`, without enhancement.

Several graphic forms resemble the working Sadoleto alphabet. This gives an
additional primary comparison source, but does not yet prove complete key
identity. The marginal annotation has **not** been fully transcribed or
aligned word by word. No new sign value or target plaintext is accepted
solely from this inspection. Next useful work is to collate this short
passage and its annotation, retaining uncertain marginal letters explicitly.

The download completed successfully. The initial restricted-network attempt
failed; the approved retry retrieved the images. No DECODE credential was
needed for this public Vestigia source.

Applying the existing alphabet tentatively gives `vno de qui` at the
right-hand opening (v/n/o, d/e, q/u/i). This is a **provisional** local
reading, not confirmation that the whole June key is identical. It may use
v for vocalic u. The following line has familiar signs but its complete
wording is not accepted here.

The margin appears to mention `Carrara` and ends with an abbreviation
consistent with `Imp[eratore]`; both readings remain provisional. The first
word is overwritten or corrected. No secure word-for-word alignment between
this annotation and the entire cipher has yet been made. In particular,
the marginal name must not be forced onto the cipher's following signs to
derive new letter values. The annotation might summarize or comment on the
passage rather than reproduce its exact text; that possibility is not yet
resolved. The earlier label "marginal decipherment" records the literature
lead, not a completed demonstration of a literal plaintext pairing.

This check supplies no new accepted key entry and resolves none of the four
targets' remaining gaps. Further comparison requires resolving the marginal
wording and the cipher independently before aligning them.
