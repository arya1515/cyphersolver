# Espagnol 318 no. 92, ff. 116r–116v: transcription and reading

Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba to Lorenzo Suárez de Figueroa, 17 Aug 1500 (the heading says "la
respuesta que enbio a los señores en xvij de agosto 1500"). Transcribed 21 Sept 2026 from `full/f116r.jpg` and
`full/f116v.jpg` (line strips cut at the ink-profile line centres, 1:1 at 4155 px), against the *Cifra general de los
Reyes Católicos* (Galende Díaz 1994, app. doc. 1, p. 167 alphabet; pp. 167–173 codes, `keys/cifra_general.txt`).

## Conventions

* `code` in lower case: a nomenclator group, with its key value after `=`.
* Upper-case letters: single cipher signs, given by their value in the key's alphabet (the sign shapes are not
  reproduced; the sign-to-letter table used is below).
* `?`: a sign read but not placed; `[…]`: a stretch not resolved; `«…»`: clear text in the letter itself.
* Glosses: the later interlinear hand on f. 116r (lines 8–14), recorded in `ct92_glosses.txt`.

## What the letter says

The Great Captain answers Suárez's letter of the 14th, which came with a papal brief and two letters of the Venetian
ambassador pressing him to go against the Turk. He is ready to go, but the cipher sets the price: he will not move
"without great remuneration" (*no deve pasar … si no grande remuneración*), because Their Highnesses' interest is
served (*a beneficio de la christiandad*), and time presses: *"por estar en tanto peligro el negocio, e a tanto
lexos."* Venice holds ports in Apulia, *Brindizi y Otranto*, and he wants ports in Italy *donde esta armada se pueda
recoger quando el tiempo lo requiriese, por no aver de bolver tan lexos*, where he can land *nuestros dolientes y
heridos y ropa e vituallas* and refit the ships, *sin los ynconvenientes de estar en casas agenas*. When the Pope
called him for the siege [of …] he asked the same through Garcilaso (de la Vega, the ambassador at Rome) and got it
willingly, because the castellans were Spaniards and servants of the King. Suárez should raise it with the Venetians
*con color de nuestro descanso y reparo*. On the verso: Venice ought to satisfy them, *segund su necesidad no creo que
nada desto les sea ynposible*; if the season allowed and the danger were not so near, much could be done with the
fleet; the ambassador of Venice at Naples has offered it; he washes his hands of it (*yo me desnudo y descargo*) and
is content that the business is in Suárez's hands.

That is the Cephalonia campaign of autumn 1500: the Spanish fleet joined the Venetians against the Turk after this
exchange, and the bargaining over Apulian ports is the price Gonzalo sets for it.

## Corrections and corroboration (21 Sept 2026, second pass)

* **Tomokiyo's unpublished partial reading.** The source of cryptiana's `spanish.htm` carries a commented-out reading of
  the opening: *"[Paresce?] me que tan buena obra como esta con tanto diferencia e trabaio desto armada no deve pasar
  caso solo cargo sin grande -emuneracion"*. It fixes lines 6–7: `zap` is `zag` = *me* (the hand's g has a p-like
  tail), `bac` is `vac` = *armada* (code v written like ɓ), `xie` = *cargo* and `xir` = *diferencia* are right as
  printed, `baq` + N = *sin*, and the sign read Y in *yolo* is S (*solo*). His guess for `zie` is *Paresce* (`zix`).
* **Sanuto, *I Diarii* III** (archive.org `idiariidimarinos03sanu`). Rome, Paolo Capello, 25 Aug 1500 (cols. ~708–709):
  the Spanish ambassador said *don Consalvo … voria aver dal papa una cruciata, per poter tenir l'armata … voria ditto
  capetanio precedesse il nostro in arma'; 2.° aver qualli porto li piace a li bisogni; tertio, vituarie*. Off Corfù,
  early October (cols. ~982–983): *voria saper dove avere porto e vituarie, per i miei danari … non saria bene che le
  duo armade facesse porto in uno luogo; io voria averlo seperato.* On 13 Sept Venice ordered its Brindisi castellans
  to receive the Spanish fleet, *tamen custodissano la terra e 'l castello*. This is the demand of this letter,
  reported from the other side. The *cruciata*/jubilee (granted, col. ~749) may be what the unread stretches of
  ll. 8–11 ask for.
* **Zurita**, *Historia del rey don Hernando*, 1500: the Venetian envoy at Messina *movió alguna plática de ofrecer al
  Rey Católico a Brindez*.
* **Not found online:** the key manuscript (RAH 9/15 is not in the RAH Biblioteca Digital), and any printed clear copy
  of the letter (CODOIN, Zurita, *Noticias inéditas del Gran Capitán*, archive.org full text; Serrano y Pineda's RABM
  series was not reachable).

## Open points

1. Codes still not resolved after the second pass (which settled `zap` = `zag`, `bac` = `vac`, `xie`): `zie` (×5;
   Tomokiyo guesses *Paresce*), `zer`, `zeb`, `zems`, `xob`, `boy` in l. 8, `bog` (×3; probably `boz` = todo),
   `vog`, `bue`, `bi?`.
2. Stretches of letter signs not resolved: ll. 11, 18, 20, 21, 27, 28, 31–32, 34, 36 of f. 116r; f. 116v l. 10.
3. The q-with-o sign reads R in *remuneración*/*remito* and N in *necesidad* (v4, v9): either two signs the eye
   merges at this resolution or a scribal slip.
4. The personal-symbol and duplicate tables (p. 167 *Duplicate*, p. 173) were not needed except for LL (Ŧ).

Status: read in part. The subject, the argument and about three quarters of the words are secure; the remainder needs
a sign-by-sign pass at higher magnification, or a comparison with BRAH 9/15 ff. 7–9 itself for the misprinted codes.
