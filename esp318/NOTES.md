# BnF Espagnol 318: the five ciphered letters, 1497–1504 (catalogue no. 9)

Session of 17 September 2026. Target as set: "solve Espagnol 318 nos. 5, 93–95 … open the ark, count symbols,
check for a gloss." The volume (ark `btv1b52503046q`, 486 canvases, 127 folios, ex-Ruffo Scilla collection, bought
by the BnF in 1857; Morel-Fatio no. 172) holds **five** letters wholly or partly in cipher, not four: the catalogue
entry missed no. 92. This note fixes what each of the five is, what is already published, and where the remaining
work sits.

## Result in one paragraph

**No. 5 is not an open problem: it was solved and printed in 2020.** Ivan Parisi identified the cipher as the one
issued to Joan Ram Escrivà, the Spanish ambassador at Naples, and printed the whole Italian text; the letter's own
first line names "micer Joan Scrivà". **No. 92 is an open problem that this session closed in principle**: its cipher
is the *Cifra general de los Reyes Católicos* printed by Galende Díaz in 1994, which Tomokiyo had proposed on
stylistic grounds without a demonstration. The identification is now proved against eight interlinear cribs written
on the leaf itself, and a specimen line reads continuously. **No. 95 has a key but no text**: George Lasry
reconstructed its alphabet in 2022 and Tomokiyo published the key as an image; nobody has printed the plaintext.
**Nos. 93 and 94 remain unread**, but they are no longer "unidentified ciphers": both are standard ciphers of
Ferdinand's secretariat (homophonic symbol alphabet + CVC "monosyllabic" code groups), and their code-initial range
points at the *Gran cifra* of 1501–1504 rather than at either of the two keys already in print. So of the four
targets, one was already closed, one is now keyed and readable, one has a key in print, and two are genuinely open
with a named suspect for the key.

## No. 5 — closed

Ivan Parisi, *Un cifrario in prestito per una lettera segretissima di Federico d'Aragona re di Napoli ai Re Cattolici
nel BnF, Espagnol 318*, "Studi di storia medioevale e di diplomatica", n.s. IV (2020), pp. 157–177; open access,
DOI 10.54103/2611-318X/14378 (PDF in `lit/parisi2020_ssmd.pdf`). The point of the article is the anomaly: the king of
Naples had his secret letter enciphered **in the Spanish ambassador's own cipher**, by the ambassador's hand. Parisi
pins the cifrista by a Spanish speaker's mistake — the failure to use the double-*s* cipher in *possano*,
*obedientissimo*, *promesso*. Contents: Federico confirms the pre-agreement for the marriage of his nine-year-old son
Ferrando, duke of Calabria, to one of the Spanish infantas. The nomenclator elements Parisi lists are legible on the
leaf at 3000 px and match what one reads there directly: `qix` del, `qao` duque, `qav` de, `qae` con, `qed` cosa,
`qie` como, `rae` el, `rof` en, `ru` mensajero, `saf` Italia, `sul` la, `tad` no, `tay` parte, `vee` que, `vet` si,
`vog` sobre, `xer`/`xoq` Vuestras Altezas, `xit` una, `pam` bien.

## No. 92 — the cipher identified and proved

The leaf is headed, in the sender's hand, "la respuesta q[ue] enbio a los s[eño]res en xbij de ag[osto] 1500", and
opens in clear: *"Muy mag[nifico] Señor: a los xiiij d[e]l presente recebi v[uest]ra letra con hun breve d[e]l papa y
otras dos cartas del embax[ad]or veneçiano con uno suyo, dando priesa en my yda a la resistençia del turco…"* — then
alternates clear and cipher for two pages.

Tomokiyo notes that the Great Captain "used this cipher" (Ferdinand/Isabella's cipher of c. 1500–1502) in this letter,
but marks the letter undeciphered. The identification can be proved rather than assumed, because a later hand wrote a
**partial decipherment between the lines** of f. 116r. Reading those interlinear words against the printed key gives
eight independent hits and no misses:

| gloss on the leaf | cipher | *Cifra general* |
|---|---|---|
| lo | `yuy` | Lo |
| y tpo | `h` `boy` | (E =) y · Tiempo |
| por | `zez` | Por |
| estar | `xiz` + `7` | Esta + r |
| en | `xuy` | En |
| tanto | `baz` | Tanto |
| el negoçio | `xix` `zol` | El · Negocio |
| de | `xup` | De |
| puede | `zuy` | Puede |
| no | `zik` | No |
| — | `bob` | Que |

`7` = R and `h` = E come from the key's own symbol alphabet, and the glossator's "y" for `h` is the ordinary *e*/*y*
alternation of the conjunction, so it confirms rather than contradicts. A gloss-anchored line decodes straight
through:

```
ct   h  boy      zez    xiz  7  xuy  baz      ff k …
pt   y  tiempo   por    esta r  en   tanto    p  g …
```

that is, *"…y tiempo, por estar en tanto…"* — exactly the words the glossator wrote above it. The key is therefore
settled and the letter is readable; what remains is the labour of transcribing the symbol alphabet through both pages.
f. 116v ends half in clear — *"…no desconfio que mucho se pudiera aprovechar … se ha ofreçido … yo me desnudo y
descargo y me satisfago en que n[uest]r[o]…"* — with `zin` = Nápoles, `xar` = después, `biz` = toda, `xuy` = en among
the codes, and below it the inventory mark "Inventº 11 Ottº 53".

The key is transcribed here as `keys/cifra_general.txt` / `.json`: 683 code groups from Galende Díaz's plates
(pp. 167–173), plus the twenty-one personal symbols (Papa = A, Emperador = B, Rey de Françia = F, Rey de Spanya = G,
Rey de Napoles = □, Don Fadrique prinçipe de Altamira = S†, …). `decode.py` applies it to a transcription.

## Nos. 93 and 94 — what they are

Both are written the way the secretariat's ciphers are written: single invented symbols for letters, with homophones,
interleaved with groups of two to four Latin letters that are nomenclator entries. In no. 93 the groups read
`gto pie roe hug heg mat gnd gab zoe mae cpm lnq hoe hac zup qup gre zen gne` …; in no. 94 `gag rah mnb mms nob gik
luy mei nen mok pol pat par paq vaz mal nyb nyl gob gib lob moe nak rag lue` …. The standalone symbols are
`+ ÷ ʒ ff ıı :/ ⧻ 7`.

The initial range of the code groups is the discriminator. Three keys of this office are in print or reconstructed:

* **Escrivà no. 1** (the key of no. 5): initials p-, q-, r-, s-, t-, v-, x-.
* **Cifra general de los RRCC** (the key of no. 92): initials v-, x-, y-, z-, b-, c-.
* **Gran cifra of the Gran Capitán, 1501–1504** — Bergenroth's reconstruction, BNE MSS 20.211/52, re-found by the
  CNI in 2018: initials h-, l-, m-, p-, q-, r-, s-, u-/v-.

Nos. 93 and 94 fall outside the first two and inside the third. One concrete hook: Bergenroth reads **LUQ = capitán**,
and no. 93 has the group `lnq` — *u* and *n* being the same two minims in this hand. Bergenroth also has `muy` = de,
and `muy` occurs in no. 93. That is a lead, not a demonstration: both Bergenroth's list and the CNI's are partial
(Bergenroth's own sheet counts 89 unidentified symbols), and most groups in nos. 93–94 are in neither. Testing it
needs a full transcription of one of the two.

A second candidate, untested because unpublished: Galende Díaz's file BRAH 9/15 opens with **"Cifra del visorrey"
(ff. 1–6)**, which he describes but does not reproduce. No. 94 is a viceroy's letter.

The dates make either plausible: no. 94 is Messina, 27 April 1503 — the day before Cerignola — and no. 93 is Venice,
24 February 1504, in the weeks after the Garigliano.

## No. 95

Solved "approximately" by George Lasry in May 2022; Tomokiyo prints the key as an image only
(`lit/crypt/img/GL_BnF_es318_f122.png`, saved here), and no plaintext has been published. The alphabet is homophonic,
one to three signs per letter, plus unassigned signs and the code groups `bac bnh bon dah daf dae oht bed dal boo del`.
Applying it to the opening line of f. 122r yields Spanish — the run `m̅ ß ≐` gives *que* and `7 ≐` gives *de* — so the
key is sound, and the letter is waiting for someone to transcribe thirty lines of *scriptio continua*.

## What is in this directory

* `full/` — IIIF `full/full` images of all thirteen cipher folios (4100–4200 px wide). The 1400 px canvases are not
  enough for these hands; the 3000 px ones in `img/` are usable; the full ones are comfortable. Gallica returns
  HTTP 429 after about six full-size requests in a row — pause 20 s between them.
* `manifest.json`, `lab2canvas.json`, `notice.html` — the ark's manifest, the folio→canvas map, and the BnF notice
  with the hundred item descriptions (items are numbered, folios are not: the map has to be made from the notice).
* `keys/cifra_general.txt|.json` — the *Cifra general de los Reyes Católicos*, 683 codes + 21 personal symbols.
* `decode.py` — applies the key to a whitespace-separated transcription; `crop.py`, `strip.py`, `lines.py` — the image
  tools. Line pitch comes from the autocorrelation of the ink profile (f. 116r 113 px, f. 118r 128 px, f. 120r 100 px,
  f. 122r 114 px); three-line half-width strips at 2.6–3× are the readable unit for these hands.
* `lit/` — Parisi 2020 (PDF + text), Galende Díaz 1994 (PDF + page renders in `lit/gal/`), Tomokiyo's Spanish-cipher
  and Lasry pages, the Bergenroth Gran Capitán key photographs.
