# Harley MS 287: Lord Cobham at Ostend, 20–22 March 1587/8 (DECODE R8477, R8482–R8487)

Status: read in part (cipher runs of ff. 70r–72v read from context; f. 11 is a different item, not read)

Catalogue item 132 ("Unknown sender to unknown recipient, 7 ciphertexts, 20–22 Mar 1587"). Sibling item 131
(R8490–R8496, Cobham to Walsingham, May–June 1588) is worked in `cobham1588/` by another session, which uses the
same cipher.

The year is Old Style: 20–22 March 1588 New Style. The writer is Henry Brooke, 10th Lord Cobham, one of
Elizabeth's peace commissioners with Derby, Croft, Dale and Rogers at Ostend in spring 1588 (Mr Controller
[Croft], "Mr Doctor Rogers" going to the Duke of Parma, "my Lord Burghley's servant" are named in clear). The
addressee is not named; "your honour", thanks for "the Scottish letters", and the volume (Walsingham's
correspondence) point to **Sir Francis Walsingham**, as in item 131.

DECODE marks these "Partially decrypted"/"Non-decrypted". There is no interlinear decipherment on any of these
seven leaves. CSP Foreign vol. 21 pt 4 (1931) calendars Cobham's letters of 22 and 24 March to Burghley from SP
77, not these Harley letters (checked `british-history.ac.uk/cal-state-papers/foreign/vol21/no4/pp199-212`,
`pp212-221`). No printed decipherment found.

## The cipher

Simple substitution into English with a few homophones, graphic signs, words separated, cipher runs inserted in
clear text. Key, rebuilt from context here (glyph names are descriptive; forms confirmed against the glosses on
f. 89, R8493, by the `cobham1588` session):

| plain | sign(s) |
|---|---|
| a | ∪ (cup), ⊥ (inverted T) |
| b | + |
| c | 8 |
| d | ∞ |
| e | 7 |
| f | H (barred h) |
| g | Ӿ (barred x), 3 |
| h | ϸ (crossed stem with descender) |
| i | ⊥, ǂǂ in some words |
| l | ϕ-form with short stem |
| m | ∝ (looped x) |
| n | ǂǂ (double-cross) |
| o | z (2-form) |
| p | ϕ-form with long stem |
| r | V |
| s | ∧ (a second, narrower form of the caret) |
| t | ∧ |
| u / v / w | `..` (two dots) ; w also ẋ (dotted x) |
| y | γ |
| x, k, q, j | not seen |

`:` stands after a letter inside some words (commaunded, instructions, Oudenburgh); read as u. The
l / p / m-forms of the ϕ family and the two carets are hard to tell apart on the scans; they were resolved word
by word from context, not by glyph.

## Reading (modern spelling of the deciphered words; cipher parts in *italics*)

Grades as in README Conventions: H high, C context, M uncertain.

**f. 70r (22 March).** "... What effect this message will work I know not, but doubt the worst, and therefore do
wish and think it expedient *that some of these ships were commaunded to attend on this coast nor upon events*
(C), for I am persuaded *his will never yield to send 42 hither* (C). ... That the same *…* from *…* to *…* as
the *…* do voluntarily bring it, and that only so long as they ..., there is *not any money* stirring here (H).
The present *…* will be *sent to …* for the *…* of so large a *…* by the half at the least. The last *…* from the
*…* of the *…* five weeks longer, as the *…* tell me ... The Duke is expected within a day or two to be at
Bruges. The *new …* sent *…* is said to be almost ended, and will be *…*." — Several short runs on this leaf are
only partly read (proper names and a code number 42).

**f. 70v (22 March).** "*This preparation, together with the reinforcing of his garrisons* hereabout (H),
*Stanley's repair unto Turnhout(?)* (M) make such *[at] Oudenburgh with eleven companies of footmen* (H), and
the *Duke's* coming to *Bruges* doth *threaten* somewhat *…* (C). And in mine opinion *this town* is fit to be
*[stopped]* through some *attempts upon the haven* (H). Which being once *stopped*, the *town* being already
abandoned of *succour by land*, cannot *…* a *resolution* (C) *[against the] enemy many days* together (H). My
greatest *grief* is that in all *this town* I am *not* furnished of *any one spy* to *employ abroad* for the more
*certainty of the enemy's proceedings* (H). And from Flushing I hear *very seldom* by reason that the *passage*
is dangerous and *subject to interception* (H). I pray your honour to think upon these: *nor our supplies(?)*
doth assure us of *nothing, as it* makes account so long as we remain in any *town which is not within* the
King's possession (C). By my servant William I wrote in my private letter a special motion for *our remove to
Flushing* (C) where we might better stand on terms for her Majesty's honour, this place being subject to all
inconveniences ... send away the *commissioners(?)* (M)."

**f. 71r (Middelburg intelligence).** Clear account of Grenier's intrigue and the Latin tag *Non semper temeritas
est felix...*, then: "In my opinion *the ships would be well guarded* (C), and when all *ships of charge as well
of the … as merchants be at sea* (C), there would be *2 or 3 pinnaces appointed to [watch] … and direct the
channel* (C). *… these few days … sent … into Italy* (M)." The last cipher line reads *sent host into Italy*
only tentatively.

**f. 71v (20 March).** "There are new companies lately added to the old garrison *of Oudenburgh(?), our near
neighbours* (C), which were very strong before. This drawing of his forces by land so near, and the *provision
of stone and fagots* (H) to *come from Eecloo* (C) *by water* (H), make us *suspect some attempts against this
town and haven* (H): we think it *therefore expedient that some of the ships did continue upon this coast, to be
ready for service upon occasions* (H). Also we pray your *honour* (C) *would remove us hence or dismiss us to some
other place of more surety* (C)."

**f. 72r (20 March).** "*This bearer hath* laboured his own dispatch and would fain have had *instructions
promised(?)*, but I, fearing that under *colour thereof he would proceed in his charge* (H), did let him
understand what we had generally written unto my Lord; and *what* soever he *shall say more or less besides* our
letters (H) ... As by the *…* of *your own hand* (H) in your letter of the 16 March I perceive *you oppose
yourself still against such resolutions* (H); for mine own part I would think myself most unhappy to be a *means
to conclude an irreligious peace* (H). Touching *[two names]*, if they shall be delivered over *to the States* (H),
I am of another opinion for the reasons sent in my last by my man William ... things standing as they do, the
*[peace, sign □]* will *…* *them*, and then what dishonour and disfavour will it be for her Majesty. *[name]* is
a *…* man, and hath means and will to *deserve his place* (C). *The other …*"

**f. 72v (20 March).** "... hath assured means to a special service, *…* being *…* as I wrote unto you. I am
satisfied touching 500 ... *instructions* no man shall draw ..." (short runs, C).

## What stays open

- The two names in "Touching *ϕ⊥Ӿz∧ϕ and +∪V#7⊥*" (f. 72r) and the name on the next line: a person or towns
  "to be delivered over to the States"; the letters do not fit Flushing/Brill/Ostend/Bergen cleanly.
- About a third of the runs on f. 70r, and the last line of f. 71r.
- A proper glyph-by-glyph transcription (this reading was done from the scans by context, word by word).
- f. 11 (R8477): a different letter and a numeral/letter code; not read.
