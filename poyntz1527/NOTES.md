# Lee, Ghinucci and Poyntz to Wolsey, Valladolid, July 1527 (BL Cotton MS Vespasian C IV ff. 174-177 and 182-185; DECODE R8565, R8567)


Catalogue no. 110 (DECODE refill, rule-scored class B). Worked 21 Sept 2026.

Outcome: **read at the time; closed.** Nothing new to read.

## What the record is

DECODE R8567 (8 images, ff. 182-185 old foliation 162-165, headed "Hispania 1527", endorsed "1527 June 18 or 19 July /
S^r Fra: Poynts") is not a cipher letter. It is a long English despatch in clear, the joint report of Edward Lee (the
King's almoner) and Sir Francis Poyntz, with the French envoy the bishop of Tarbes, on their audiences with Charles V
and Chancellor Gattinara ("Mons. de Burlans" = Granvelle? the text calls him Mons. de Burlans), from their arrival to
the 18th/19th July 1527: Francesco Sforza, the two millions of the ransom, the treaty of Madrid, the bishop of
Worcester (Ghinucci), Cardinal Colonna. The "unknown recipient" is Wolsey ("your grace", "the King and my lord
Cardinal").

The only cipher is three short runs on image 6 (f. 184v), in the Lee-Worcester sign cipher, each with a contemporary
decipherment written in darker ink on the line above it:

> it can not be so well bestowed. we know well you will kepe hit full well secrets from the frenchemen. your grace
> may consider that he durst not have proposed this thing, being so high, and afterward tell them of what he had done

(plaintext.txt, 44 words.) Spot check with S. Tomokiyo's reconstruction of the Lee-Worcester cipher (1526-1529),
attached to the DECODE record as a document: the run `σ τ c c ⊥` under "can not" is c-a-n-n-o, which fits. The
DECODE "Partially decrypted" status is this interlinear decipherment.

Prior art: read at the time; the letter should be calendared in Letters and Papers Henry VIII vol. IV (July 1527) and
the key is Tomokiyo's (cryptiana). Images are BL material behind the DECODE login: kept git-ignored in img/.
Removed from the catalogue 21 Sept 2026.

## Catalogue 111: R8565, Valladolid 17 July 1527 (ff. 174-177)

Worked 21 Sept 2026. Outcome: **deciphered at the time and in print; closed.** Nothing new to read.

DECODE R8565 ("Fran. Poynts (Hispania) to unknown recipient", 17 Jul 1527, ff. 174-177, 7 pp., "Partially decrypted",
inline cleartext and inline plaintext both "Yes", associated key "Lee-Worcester Cipher (1526-1529)") is not a
Poyntz letter to an unknown recipient. It is part of the joint despatch of **Girolamo Ghinucci (bishop of Worcester),
Edward Lee and Sir Francis Poyntz to Cardinal Wolsey, Valladolid, 17 July 1527**, calendared in full in Letters and
Papers Henry VIII vol. IV part 2, no. 3271 (British History Online, "July 1527, 11-20", pp. 1483-1486), with the note
"Pp. 23. The cipher deciphered by Tuke. Add. Endd. One part of this despatch is separated from the other."

Where it sits. L&P cites the old Cotton foliation, which here runs eight below DECODE's new one: R8566 (new ff.
178-181, old 170-173, per lee1526/) is L&P no. 3270, Lee to Henry VIII, "Vesp. C. IV. 170", and both carry the
"daughter of Portugal" matter. So R8565, new ff. 174-177, is old ff. 166-169: L&P's footnote 1 puts the separated
part of no. 3271 at "f. 166", starting "Were sent for to court on the 15th July" and running to the signatures and
"17 July 1527". The public DECODE thumbnails fit: eight images, seven written pages headed "Hispania 1527", mostly
English in clear with about five short bands of interlinear decipherment (images 2, 3, 5, 6, 7), three signatures at
the foot of image 7 (Ghinucci, Lee, Poyntz), and image 8 the address leaf with the endorsement. The full images are
behind the DECODE login; there is no cookie on this machine, so only the 200-px thumbnails were seen. The one check
still open is to confirm on image 1 that the text opens with the 15 July audience (L&P 3271 second part) and not
"Wrote in former letters of the arrival of Ave Maria" (L&P 3272, which L&P foliates "f. 174", old = new 182 on
the same offset, i.e. the first leaf of R8567; worth checking if R8567 is reopened, since its notes read it as a
Lee-and-Poyntz despatch and give a different old foliation, 162-165).

What the cipher hides. L&P prints the deciphered passages of this part in italics (Tuke's decipherment):

- the French "would not have brought him to show any demands. He professes willingness to do everything for the
  King", and the ambassadors "avoid giving him any token but that the King and your Grace are still wholly his";
- De Buclans (one of the Emperor's commissioners) asked "what security they should have that he would not give
  up the duchy [of Milan] to France, and suggested that the Emperor might give it along with the daughter of Portugal
  to the duke of Richmond" (Henry Fitzroy);
- "De Tarbes has now shown us the fourth instruction ... he withheld it; for he is to say to the Emperor, before the
  English ambassadors, that they desire his amity more than that of any other prince."

The companion letter L&P no. 3272 (Ghinucci, Lee and Poyntz, "chiefly cipher, deciphered by Tuke", Tuke's decipher
in the Record Office) carries the rest: Charles's intercepted report from his ambassador in France that De Tarbes
may offer the Viceroy's conditions undiminished, that Lautrec may conclude in Italy, that Wolsey pressed Francis to
marry the Princess Mary, and the offer of the daughter of Portugal for Richmond with 400,000 ducats.

Cipher: the Lee-Worcester sign cipher, S. Tomokiyo's reconstruction (document on R8566). DECODE corrections queued
under poyntz1527 in decode_updates/queue.json (R8565 and R8567). Removed from the catalogue 21 Sept 2026.
