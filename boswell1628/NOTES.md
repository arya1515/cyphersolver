## The document

Page 1: ten numbered lines of invented signs, no word division. Page 2: the address "To my Especiall & most Estee-
med Cosin William Boswell Esq.", a seal, the endorsement **"S[i]r Ra: Boswele. 24 Jan. 1627"** (i.e. 1627/8) with
a note (Bradburn?), a list of eight topics in the same hand ("1. Hopyan. 2. Pettyt. 3. Co[urt] of wards … 4. Yorkshe:
lands 5. S[i]r Ant: Aucher 6. Morgan 7. … 8. E. of Dorset"), and a contemporary tally of every sign with the line
numbers it occurs in, i.e. the working sheet of whoever deciphered it (Boswell, or a Deciphering Branch clerk).

The sender is Sir Ralph Boswell, a kinsman ("Cosin"), not the King; DECODE's own note already doubted Charles I.

## The reading

Woodard's key, re-derived by aligning his transcription with his plaintext (every transcription letter maps to one
plaintext letter; 442 signs, 24 distinct):

    a U/V  b P  c O  d N  e S  f A  g T  h E  i R  j D  k Y  l B  m I  n F  o H  q G  r K  s M  t C  u L  v W
    p S (2x, this session)   w ? (2x)   x ? (1x)

Text (modernised spacing; grades H unless marked):

> Upon Saterday being our fifteenth of the presente, our[s] greate Duke returned from Plimmouth to Whitehall,
> contrary to the opinion of very many who conceaued he [w x] would not haue appeard heere before he had heard how
> the pulse of the Parlamente had gone. But being confident of the King's fauour, [f]eares not the many enemies that
> he hath in the Lower House, especially hauing so greate a party in the Higher House by reason of the Bishops and
> the new Lords, which for the most [part] are d of his owne ma[s]kinge, and to take away the ill opini[on] of such
> lo[r]des.

- p = S: fixed by "HI[S] OWNE"; its other use gives "OUR[S] GREATE" (H for the sign, C for the sense).
- second w = "part": "for the most [part] are(d) of his owne making" (C). One sign for a whole word: a small
  nomenclator or an abbreviation mark. The first w and the only x ("conceaued he [w x] would") are unread.
- Writer's slips: OFINION (opinion), HEARES (feares), MASKINGE (makinge), LOLDES (lordes), OPINI (opinion).
  The text breaks off mid-sentence at the end of line 10.

## Date

"Saterday … our fifteenth of the presente": in the Julian calendar, Saturday fell on the 15th in Dec 1627 and
Mar 1628 (Jan 1628 was a Tuesday). The endorsement gives receipt on 24 Jan 1627/8, so the letter is of 15 Dec 1627
or a few days later. Buckingham came back from the failed Île de Ré expedition by way of Plymouth in November
1627. When he reached Whitehall has not been checked against a printed source here. The Parliament whose "pulse"
was feared was the one being planned that winter; writs went out on 30 Jan 1628.
