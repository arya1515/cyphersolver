## What it says

A criminal matter and a piece of news.

**The criminal matter.** A *licenciado Bernardino*, "gran bellaco", has turned up in Rome. Hurtado
and the Duke [of Sessa] want him arrested; the Pope will order it if the man is inside Rome but
leaves it to the Duke if he is outside, and when the Duke finds he is in the city, lodging with his
brother, and asks for a night arrest, Adrian VI declines — *aqui no seria bien* — and offers a
formal *suplicacion* instead. Hurtado asks the Emperor to send an information of the man's guilt so
the case can be pressed. It is a small, exact picture of what an imperial agent could and could not
get out of this Pope.

**The news.** *Las nuevas de Rodas que escrive el visorrey* — the viceroy of Naples' despatch on the
siege of Rhodes, forwarded to the Emperor in copy.

That last line ties this correspondence to the Venice side of the same archive: Alonso Sánchez's
R9653 of 20 November 1522 carries the Rhodes news from the other direction, out of Candia and the
Signoria (`../sanchez1522/read_r9653.md`). Rome and Venice were reporting the same disaster to the
Emperor in the same fortnight, in two different ciphers, bound now in the same volume.
