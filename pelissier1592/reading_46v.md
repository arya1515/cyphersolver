## Summary

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, reports what passed with the Spanish ministers once the court had
reached Burgos. The page opens in the middle of a conversation (probably with Don Martin de Idiaquez, named at the foot
of f. 46r). Idiaquez urged that the Leaguers be pressed hard, spoke of the taking of Pont-Audemer (reading uncertain)
and of "our forces superior to the enemy's", and said the Estates were being held back. Pelissier answered that he had
understood the deputies were to assemble the previous month. He was told the Estates had been put off until the King
arrived at Burgos. There Pelissier saw Don Juan (de Idiaquez), who gave him a private hour. Pelissier argued that
nothing matters more to a state than its reputation, which frightens enemies and keeps subjects in their duty. He
recalled the Duke of Parma's army as it stood in 1590–92. Its flourishing state had dismayed the enemy, so that most
of the royalist Catholic nobility went home and several towns began to treat. Had the army wintered in France,
code-group [315] (the Béarnais/Henri IV, see below) would have been ruined, and the Germans would never have dared to
come to his aid. Its withdrawal to the Low Countries ended all these dealings and let the Catholics lose heart. Many
League towns, fearing exposure if the enemy returned, began their own negotiations. The page ends on the losses in
Picardy (La Fère, Soissons) and what would have happened had they not been provided for.
