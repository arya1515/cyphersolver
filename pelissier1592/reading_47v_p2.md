## Summary (English)

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, is reporting to Jeannin on his audience with the King and his exchanges
with the King's principal minister at Burgos. The King (or his minister, speaking for him) said that history would show no
other king who had given such powerful and lasting help to France with such sincere affection. If the help had not
brought what was hoped for, the fault lay with those it was sent to, some of whom had acted too slackly. The Spanish side
rejected Pelissier's arguments for holding the assembly of the Estates and his other remedies. They said these moves
would only create suspicion that it was a trick to hinder them. Pelissier praised Philip's zeal for the Catholic cause
but asked that what he said be kept from others of the Spanish nation. He complained that the King's minister feared the
French of the League would take it very badly that the Spanish were delaying the one thing that would bring the
Catholics peace: the election of a legitimate (Catholic) king to oppose the heretic and tyrant Henri IV. There is
mention of the late Cardinal (probably Bourbon), of committing all the forces to a relief (délivrance), and of
"faulsement" claims about holding the Estates. The minister warned Pelissier to be careful and would not say how the King
would take things. Pelissier also records the King's professions of affection, the question of whether forces would be
enough to keep the Estates free, and his own wish to have things out with the minister. He notes that the Spanish
ministers "have delicate ears when one tells them" things, and that even good advice is suspect to them.
