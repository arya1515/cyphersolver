# f. 49r (canvas 106): reading, second pass

Pelissier to Jeannin, Burgos, 13 Sept 1592, page 7. There are 50 cipher rows and 4 blocks of clear text.
Deciphered stretches are in {braces}, and clear text is in plain type. [?] marks a word I could not recover and (?) a doubtful word.
[315] is the three-digit code group. On this page it stands for Henri IV (see the notes below), not the King of Spain.

## Summary

This page is part of a discourse on the war in France in 1591-92. The cipher mostly narrates events and the clear text
gives the argument. Code group [315] is Henri IV, "the Béarnais". He gathers his forces, takes Chartres and other towns,
and takes Noyon in sight of the League army. German forces join him before the League can stop it, and the page records
complaints about the Prince of Ascoli's handling of the relief force. A commander, probably Mayenne, raises 15,000 foot
and 3,000-4,000 horse and wants to act while waiting for "Son Altesse" (Parma), but he is refused. Another figure is
criticised for "living so dissolutely that everyone cried out about it". Next comes the 1592 campaign. Caudebec is taken.
Henri IV turns and camps next to the Catholic army, offering battle every day, and lack of supplies finally forces that
army across the river (the Seine). It withdraws from around Rouen to near Château-Thierry. Henri's own army breaks up
near Châlons-en-Champagne, with most of the Germans and Swiss leaving him, yet he stays master of the countryside. The
clear text then blames Parma for letting many chances to fight go by. This has let enemy rumours spread in France that
Philip II defends the Catholic cause only for his own interests: to bring his rebels in the Low Countries to heel by
keeping the French busy, so that they cannot help the rebels. The page ends with a call for the "good and repose" of
Christendom(?) and a warning against fomenting one of the parties.

## Rows fixed this pass (6 rows, 8 tokens)

- C2: `pi r o r o S 80` became `pi fr o r 56 S 80`. The image shows a small plain r, then δ ξ 56, which gives "forces".
- C3: `z H q o 4y 30` became `z + q Q 4y 30`. The glyph is "-H" (H with a lead-in stroke), which is h, and the loop is a
  curled ∂, which is r. This gives "Chartres". I also changed `dz E ph r` to `dz E q r`: the glyph is the ϙ loop, which
  gives "par le mo-".
- C8: `q o 3 56 S pi 3 lam` became `q o r 56 S pi r lam`. Both glyphs are the zigzag ξ, which gives "forces".
- C9: `z 4 H e3` became `z + e3`. There is no 4: it is the "-H" h-glyph, which gives "empesché".
- C12: `e3 3 o` became `e3 r o`, the ξ in "heretique". `e3 o oH` became `e3 d oH`: it is the looped d, which gives "mieux".
- C47: `g 7 e3` became `g y e3`. The glyph is ɤ with a crossbar (b), which gives "rebelles".

## Key observations (vs TOKENS.md)

1. **"-H" = h.** An H with a lead-in stroke or bar (C3 Chartres, C9 empescher, probably C15 empescher, C24 chevaux) is a
   separate glyph from the plain ℋ, which is q (qu'il, quelques and so on). TOKENS.md lists H only as q.
2. **w (ʊʊ) also = ff.** Examples: C42 "a**ff**ectionnés" (`T w e3 z 4`), C44 "de**ff**ence" (`6 t w g s z e3`), and
   probably C40 "a**ff**aires". It is still "et" elsewhere (C9 "et son dessein", C42 "et autres").
3. **T sometimes = g.** Examples: C13 "reli**g**ion" (`D g II 33 T pi Lo s`), C16 "Allemai**g**ne" (`HH T pi T 4 g`),
   and probably C47 "ran**g**er". This may be a separate T-like g-glyph (the key's "Tz" g cell). I did not change these
   tokens because I could not see a clear difference in shape.
4. **pi vs tt.** Many "π" written with a tailed right leg must be s (tt): lors, dispositions, s'estoit, ses, Chartres,
   temps, les. TOKENS.md is right in principle, but the first pass wrote almost every π as `pi`. A decoder run that
   allows pi=s (penalised) fixes many rows.
5. **D and o as r.** ∂ (D) is often r (perdre, prince, l'oreille, honneur, pour, redressées), not only e or o.
6. **δ as c.** A large δ with a tail stands for c in several places (C8 "secourir", C9/C10 "prince", C47 "subiect"), as
   the C24 note says. I left these tokens unchanged.
7. **56 is sometimes y/i.** C10 "d'Ascol**y**" (`dz 56 Lo II 56`) and perhaps C44 "heres**y**e". It is c elsewhere.
8. **[315] is Henri IV, not "ROY" (the King of Spain).** C32 has "le [315] ayant incontinant ... tourné la teste et
   s'estant campé pres l'armée catholique", and C16 has "[forces] d'Allemaigne ... iointes au [315]". Both only fit Henri
   IV (the Béarnais) in 1591-92.
9. **7 vs y.** A crossed ɤ was read as 7 at least once (C47). Other 7s standing for b may be the same misreading.
