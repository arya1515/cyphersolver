## Summary

This is the last page of Pelissier's letter to Jeannin, written from Burgos, where Philip II's court was, on 13 September 1592.
The cipher opening warns that some people ("ilz") are slowly undermining the authority of someone, probably Mayenne. They have
first taken away control of finances and military command, then used intrigue to erode his credit, meaning to bring him as low as
they can ("Dieu veulle que je me trompe"). In clear text Pelissier says that a certain person "has a flea in his ear" and keeps his
thoughts to himself. He learned this from that person's Dominican confessor. A figure at court on his own business (the
[310]/ambassador passage) has been sent away with no answer to his demands. Another man, whom Jeannin knows and who has been
posted to Madrid to wait for orders, is unhappy. Pelissier argues that treatment is equal everywhere, so nobody should be
jealous. The wisest should make the first move so that everyone gives up some private interest and they unite. That would be the
true way to escape the resolutions already taken "par deça". The clear text then complains that the promised facilities in Madrid
have not come. He is staying on at court to get a final answer on the 50,000 écus that Don Juan and Don Martín de Idiáquez keep
dangling. He had asked leave to go home, but since he has been told to stay he will wait out the month. If he is still not given
the means, he will take his final decision, since his complaints have been in vain. The postscript reports that someone
("le Baston"?) has been sent to Palencia, well pleased. The King's warm welcome and honours, down to sending him horses when he
came to audiences, make Pelissier suspect that the man has private advantages of his own. Jeannin knows him better and can judge.
