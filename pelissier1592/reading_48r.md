## Summary

Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, sends Jeannin his analysis of the League's position. He argues that new
counsels are needed, a "third means", because the harm has sunk too deep and past efforts have only lowered the League's (or
Spain's) reputation abroad. He does not think now is the time to assemble the Estates General to elect a Catholic king. The
last army came to nothing, and the people, the clergy and the nobility are all worn out and discouraged. They would sooner
accept an accord that sets up a king against the one "who claims to be" king (Henri IV). Pelissier expects the conflict to
end only with the extermination of one of the two rival royal lines, or in the ruin of both sides. He therefore backs a plan
already put to Philip II: keep two armies in the field. One would face the enemy, push him back from the Seine towards the
Loire and carry the war onto his territory. The other would take the Seine towns, free Paris and Rouen, and then reduce the
provinces one after another. He answers the cost objection (nearly 300,000 écus a month): the effort would last only a few
months, and after a victory the armies could be cut back. He adds that the King should be warned how dangerous it is to
risk a battle with an army made up of foreign nations (subjects of the Low Countries?). The page ends by asking that His
Majesty have an opposing force made up (the sentence runs onto 48v).
