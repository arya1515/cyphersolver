## Summary

Pierre Pelissier, the League's envoy at Philip II's court, writes to President Jeannin from Burgos on 13 September 1592.
He reached Valladolid just as the King was leaving. Don Juan de Idiáquez told him that business would have to wait
until Palencia, about eight leagues away. There, in cipher, Pelissier again pressed his own situation. He had too little
money to stay longer in Spain without the King's help, and his credit was gone because he had not repaid money borrowed
in Madrid. The King's long silence told him that his request was not welcome, so he asked leave to go back to France.
Philip replied that he had his own ministers in France who reported everything and received his orders, so it was up
to Pelissier whether he went or stayed. Pelissier answered that he had come on Mayenne's orders, not for his own
pleasure, and would like to leave with the same kind of order. Philip's answer was "yo lo creo", with a fuller reply
promised for the next day. At his lodging Idiáquez asked for news from France. Pelissier told him about the League's
weakness and the enemy's control of the countryside, called the rumours of peace false, and said Mayenne intended to go
to Champagne for the Estates-General. Idiáquez said Madrid had seen copies of Mayenne's circular letters and the orders
sent from Rome. He then announced that the King wanted Pelissier to wait for further news and had granted him 500
ducats, about 450 écus, payable in Madrid, and that Pelissier could withdraw with the foreign ambassadors. Idiáquez
would write to Francisco de Idiáquez to have the sum paid. When Pelissier asked for a longer talk on the state of
France and the dangers if nothing was done, Idiáquez said it could not happen there because the court was leaving for
Burgos. The page ends as Pelissier begins talking with Don Martín de Idiáquez about the weakness of the Catholic party.

## Notes on the key (possible corrections to TOKENS.md)

1. **∞ (tokenised `oo`) = et** at C31, confirmed by the gloss "et des incon". C30 ends with the same glyph (the scribe
   repeated the row tail). The glyph may be the same as `w` (ʊʊ); if it is, `oo` = m holds only for the plain "oo".
2. **Bold Ɨ = o**: C7 gloss "con" over `z Ɨ 8`, C10 "moyens"/"auoit", C18, C27b "ou". The same was found on f. 48r.
3. **τ (small hooked T) = g**, as the 12/"Tz" form: C7 "conge", C16 "tesmoignast".
4. **‡ standing on a δ** was sometimes transcribed as `#` + `pi`/`o` (C5, C12). It is a single null (#o).
5. **q = f** ("9" form) is needed in C9 "difficile", C14 "faire" and C22 "foiblesse", so the "rarely f" in the table is
   not rare on this page.
