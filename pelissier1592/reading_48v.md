## Summary

Pelissier, the League's agent at Philip II's court, is setting out for Jeannin the military plan he is urging on
the Spanish king. Spain should raise a force in France: mostly French, "in great number, horse and foot", to
weaken the forces of the Béarnais (code 315) as long as they are paid, plus a body of Germans. It should add
Spanish and Walloon troops under good captains, so that the League army can risk a pitched battle. A victory
would ruin the enemy, who could get no reinforcements, at a moment when everyone is tired of the war. A defeat
could be absorbed because the surviving army, made up of Philip's own subject nations, could gather the remnants
and halt the enemy's advance. The present piecemeal Spanish aid only ruins the provinces. With the two sides
evenly matched, they only take small towns that the enemy retakes. It would be better to keep a real army together
in the field, which would restore Catholic morale and lead to "a lasting settlement". Pelissier then turns to the
king question. If Philip showed some particular preference in "the nomination of the king", everyone would fall
in line, some out of goodwill and some out of obligation for his help. He adds the advantages of having a king
present in the state, and ends with advice that a great monarch must know the temper of the nations he rules and
uses, so as to govern them more easily.
