# "Frederic, Re di Prussia" to an unknown recipient: BL Add MS 61575 f. 95 (DECODE R8774)

Catalogue item 98 (scored by rule). DECODE: "Non-decrypted", 1 page, cipher type unknown, symbol set numerical,
plaintext "French?", author "Frederic, Re di Prussia", dated 1702–1723 (the span of the volume, not the letter).

Status: explained, 21 Sept 2026. **Not a cipher.** The page is plain French.

## What the page is

One page downloaded with the DECODE session cookie (`decode/IMG_R8774_I40590_P.jpg`, git-ignored; the BL image is
not in the public domain). There are no numbers on it. It holds seven lines of French in a large, careful hand,
written syllable by syllable in spaced columns and spelled by ear. Under the text is a long rule and an ink blot
where a signature might be. Folio number 95 is at top right.

Transcription (`transcription.txt`, line for line):

    par cel le cy je vient vous re
    mer cier en cor eun ne foy de
    la Pay ne que vous aves eue avec
    moy en me mon trant d'es cria re
    en sif fer et vous as seu re
    que je se roys tous jours re cog naissent
    vous sou haytant eun bon voya ge.

Reading (`reading.txt`):

> Par celle-ci je viens vous remercier encore une fois de la peine que vous avez eue avec moi en me montrant
> d'écrire en chiffre, et vous assurer que je serai toujours reconnaissant, vous souhaitant un bon voyage.

"By this I come to thank you once more for the trouble you took with me in showing me how to write in cipher,
and to assure you that I shall always be grateful, wishing you a good journey."

## Why DECODE lists it as a cipher

The note thanks someone for teaching the writer *to write in cipher* ("d'escriare en siffer", with *s* for *ch*,
as a German speaker might spell it). It was presumably filed with the Marlborough cipher material in Add MS 61575
for that reason, and catalogued on DECODE as a ciphertext from the volume's heading. The syllable layout is a
writing exercise, or a draft laid out to be put into a syllabic cipher later. Either way, nothing on the page is
enciphered.

## Open

* Who wrote it. DECODE's "Frederic, Re di Prussia" is not supported by anything visible on the leaf (no
  signature survives under the blot). The childish hand and phonetic spelling suggest a young German-speaking
  prince, perhaps a Prussian crown prince, but that is a guess. It needs the BL description of f. 95.
* The recipient: someone who taught ciphers and was about to travel, perhaps a Marlborough secretary. Unknown.
