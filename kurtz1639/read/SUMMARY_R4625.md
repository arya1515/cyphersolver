# R4625: summary

## 1. Date, place, addressee
- **Addressee:** "Hochgeborner Herr, Gnädiger Herr" (p.7) and "E. H." throughout, which fits Trauttmansdorff.
- **Date:** no date line is legible in the transcription. The clear text on p.7 mentions "22. Jan.", so the letter is late January or early February 1639. The place is not shown but is probably Hamburg.
- **Pages:** pp.2, 3, 5 and 6 carry a long memorandum. P.7 is a separate covering letter. Pp.1 and 4 are blank.

## 2. Summary
This is a political memorandum on the Lower Saxon Circle and how to keep it on the Emperor's side against the Swedes. Kurz argues that the Circle should be kept divided (*den Crais disjunct zu halten*) and that the House of Saxony (Brunswick-Lüneburg) should be kept in check. Halberstadt, Magdeburg, Hildesheim and Wolfenbüttel should be held so the Empire keeps its authority. Troops should be fed "head for head" (*Kopf für Kopf*) from the Circle, with the rest drawn from the hereditary lands or Silesia. He is against moving six regiments without provision. He notes that Halberstadt wants to be neutral.

He compares what other rulers maintain: the King of Denmark keeps some 8,000 men in Holstein, paid by the estates and Dithmarschen; Pomerania has 1,000 musketeers and 200 horse; Brunswick keeps troops summer and winter. He also mentions the archbishopric of Bremen, and England as unreliable. He sets out "three grades" of war in the Circle:
1. The Circle asks for the defence of the Mark (Brandenburg), which it cannot manage "ex ipsa imbecillitate petentium".
2. A promise under the imperial constitutions, but the Circle has no constitution ("sunt sine capite").
3. Neutrality: "inter duos litigantes tertius gaudet".

The imperial army is described as all but gone ("mit auss der Welt"), Hatzfeldt's included, and there is talk of retreat into Bohemia. If troops cannot be quartered in the Emperor's name, they could be quartered in the name of the Bishop of Halberstadt, with the Stift Halberstadt as a base. He warns that the enemy will not soon be seen out of Pomerania and that, without resolution and magnanimity, the Circle "and we will be lost together". The covering letter on p.7 regrets that he could not bring some regiments to the Elbe before the Kreisschluss, "for the army is quite ruined, et venter caret auribus".

## 3. Sample passages
1. [den Crais disjunct zu halten] "to keep the Circle disunited." This sits in the clear phrase "cum hoc unicum sit remedium".
2. [Halberstadt, Magdeburg, Hildesheim und Wolfenbüttel müssen uns im Crais ...] "Halberstadt, Magdeburg, Hildesheim and Wolfenbüttel must (keep) us in the Circle."
3. [die kaiserliche Armada ist mit auss der Welt, der Hatzfeld auch] "the imperial army is as good as gone from the world, Hatzfeldt's too." The last clause is part clear text.
4. [vel Caesaris vel episcopi nomine im Crais den Fuess zu erhalten] "to keep a foothold in the Circle in the name either of the Emperor or of the Bishop." Also [nomine episcopi Halberstadiensis im Crais losen] "quarter in the Circle in the name of the Bishop of Halberstadt."
5. [etliche Regimenter an die Elbe zu bringen ... aber die Armada ist gar ruinirt] "to bring some regiments to the Elbe ... but the army is quite ruined."

## 4. Confidence
About 60% of the cipher reads coherently, and nearly every place name and key noun reads. Many short fragments are corrupt or of uncertain sense: "getelcksolleab fuehren", "uhrnotturnhit", "teohinessichretriernsende", "tecian", "iesrckerie", "sosrcksein", "gestftgehetdaruberseugrungt", "ofseferiertsiichesnoch", "eintermaurseinmitdenapintenconiu", "icrhochfurstdurchlnimmertenschofson", "qundoqurterinprocaemre", "segneutralitusnonpetentezemptonemtu" and "unrenzusngt". A Latin phrase on p.6 seems to render "salus populi suprema lex esto" as "mluspopulipremalezestu". Because the text interleaves clear and cipher heavily, the syntax is often hard to follow even where the words read.
