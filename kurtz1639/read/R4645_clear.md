# R4645: clear-text pages (P3, P4, P5, P7, P8; clear lines on P6 and P9)

## Summary

R4645 holds the Hamburg cipher letter of 13 Feb 1639 (P9, P1, P2, P6) and, as enclosures, **copies of two letters
by the Swedish field marshal Johan Banér (signed "Johan Banner")**. Both concern the Swedish army's quarters in the
Lower Saxon Circle and the town of Lüneburg.

- **Banér letter A, 10 Jan 1639** (P7, continued on P5 left). To the burgomasters and council of Lüneburg. Dated
  "Neuhauß" [?]. Banér recalls that in August 1636 Lüneburg submitted to the Swedish Crown, and in 1637 broke away,
  drove the royal garrison out of the town "mit allerhandt hostiliteten" and seized the Kalkberg. He says he
  could hold this against them as enemies but asks them to let his army and allies pass through the Lüneburg
  territory and to let his men buy provisions, bread, beer and salt in the town for cash, instead of turning them
  away with hostile words. If they refuse, he will give no further warning.
- **Banér letter B, 29 Jan 1639** (P3, continued on P8 left; endorsement on P4). To the envoys of the princes and
  estates of the Lower Saxon Circle assembled at Lüneburg, answering their letter of 25 Dec [1638] brought by a
  trumpeter. They asked him to take his army out of its present quarters and out of the whole Circle. Banér
  replies that the Circle's own principals have been arming against the Swedish Crown, that he offered
  reconciliation which was refused, that he must use his arms against those who show hostility, and that he
  cannot leave the Circle while that posture continues; he will abide by what is fitting. Dated "auf dem
  Closter zu [Alden?]stett" [?], 29 Jan 1639.
- **P6 / P9, the Hamburg letter of 13 Feb 1639**: the clear lines are the closing of the cipher letter (partly
  Italian/Latin, very cursive hand), the date "Hamburg 13 Feb. 1639", a signature, and a four-line postscript
  marked #. P9 carries only a salutation line.

Note on page order: the existing tr/ notes pair P3 with P5 and P7 with P8. The text runs the other way: P7 ends
"welche zu ihrem be-" and P5 begins "hueff, Proviant ..."; P5 is dated 10 Jan (the archival note on P7 reads
"1639 Jan. 10."), and P8 is dated 29 Jan (the note on P3 reads "1639 Jan. 29."). So A = P7+P5, B = P3+P8.

Legibility: the two Banér copies are in a clear chancery hand and are read almost completely; roughly 15-20 words
are marked [?] and none wholly illegible. The P6 closing and postscript are in a fast personal hand: about half
the words are doubtful and several are [...].

Conventions: line breaks as in the MS; `/` for the virgule; abbreviations kept as written (`Königl:`, `Mt:`,
`H.`); u/v and long s normalised to u/v and s; [?] = doubtful reading; [...] = illegible.
