# R3815: summary

## 1. Date, place, addressee
- **Place/date:** "Datum Hamburg den 6. Febru. Ao. 1639" (clear text, p.2 right).
- **Addressee:** "Allergnedigister Kaiser und Herr", so Emperor Ferdinand III. The subscription reads "E. K. M. Allerunterthänigster ..." and the signature is not transcribed.
- **Pages:** p.1 and p.2 (the body, ending in the date), and p.3, a cipher-heavy sheet that continues a letter, probably an enclosure or continuation. P.4 is blank.

## 2. Summary
Kurz tells the Emperor that his earlier letter has certainly arrived and that he is sending the answer and what was resolved after it. He does not think it necessary to answer point by point: his own relations will explain matters, and so will what General-Wachtmeister Count Puchheim and (Hans?) Mandelslo put to Banér. On Denmark he writes that, as far as he can tell, the King is "ganz perplex" and bound (obligat) to aim for peace, and that the Danes may drag their feet so as to escape a *conjunction*, meaning an alliance with the Emperor. The Danish Reichsrat and the estates are irresolute and faint-hearted (*pusillanimes*) after the "accident", so little of substance can be hoped for. Kurz has still had no answer from Vienna about the Holstein complaint. The King of Denmark has also made a declaration to the English envoy.

The p.3 sheet is about stopping the enemy (the Swedes) from crossing over and shipping troops. It says that if "their princely graces" (probably Duke Georg of Brunswick-Lüneburg) resolved to act with some 8-9,000 men against an exhausted, starving enemy, much could be done, though "vox populi" says otherwise. It asks whether the Emperor wants General-Feldzeugmeister Salis, or another commander, sent to Hildesheim with imperial letters. Kurz is waiting for Salis's final answer at Hamburg about the Danish arrival, in the presence of the English envoy, and wants Duke Georg brought in. It also mentions the pass and troops going after the Kreis resolution (*Kreisschluss*), and Field Marshal Count Hatzfeldt.

## 3. Sample passages
1. [nit erachtet ewer Maiestät dienst zu ... alles ... was darinnen begriffen ... zu beantworten] "(I have) not thought it (necessary) for Your Majesty's service to answer everything contained therein." The stretch "zulolin allessoga rgenapa" is garbled.
2. [der König in Dennemarck ... ist ganz perplex ... obligat ... zum Friden] "The King of Denmark, as far as I can tell, is quite perplexed ... obliged ... to peace."
3. [der Reichsrath und [Stände] ... des Accidents halber irresolut und pusillanimes und schwerlich etwas Hauptsächliches zu hoffen] "The Council of the Realm and the estates ... irresolute and faint-hearted because of the accident, and hardly anything substantial to be hoped for."
4. [so hetten sie mit einem ... acht oder neun ... [tausend] Mann ... und darzu noch ermatteten und erhungerten Feind zu thun] "then they would have (with) some eight or nine (thousand) men ... and moreover an exhausted and starved enemy to deal with." "ahoinneun ulintmann" is corrupt.
5. [mit kaiserlichen Schreiben nach Hildesheim ab[schicken]] "(send) to Hildesheim with imperial letters." Also [dass ich zu Herzog Georgen kommen könnte] "so that I could come to Duke Georg."

## 4. Confidence
About 65-70% of the cipher reads as coherent German. It is sound where it names things: Denmark, Reichsrat, Hildesheim, Herzog Georg, Hatzfeldt, "pusillanimes". It breaks down in p.1 l.3-4 ("zulolinallessoga rgenapa"), in "puschwritficirtt", "wafenallon?nrung", "zuloloechungdesgraventenhalefelgs", "stusrerumveren" and "tenachtteahoinneun ulintmann". The clear text is itself only roughly transcribed, and parts of p.2 right are elided.
