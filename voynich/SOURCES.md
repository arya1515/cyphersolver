claim carries the strength the sweep assigned (strong / moderate / weak / refuted) and its source. Paywalled Cryptologia

## 1. Hoax / meaningless text

**Question.** The text of Beinecke MS 408 (Voynichese) is meaningless: a medieval (or later) hoax or constructed gibberish produced by a low-technology generative procedure (Rugg's table-and-grille, Timm & Schinner's self-citation/autocopy, or intuitive glossographia), rather than an encipherment or encoding of a natural or constructed language.

**State of the discussion.** No consensus, but the debate has moved. (1) Low-level statistics are now agreed to be non-diagnostic: Zipf, binomial word length, low h1/h2, adjacent-word similarity and repeat rates are reproduced by human gibberish (Gaskell & Bowern 2022), by explicit generators (Rugg & Taylor 2016; Timm & Schinner 2020) and by a hand cipher from Latin (Greshko 2025). The Yale linguistics group, previously the main academic opponent of the hoax view, conceded in 2022 that the 'too non-random to be meaningless' argument is refuted, while its companion paper showed the same anomalies are compatible with encipherment. (2) The hoax/meaningless position is a serious minority view held mainly by Timm & Schinner (peer-reviewed in Cryptologia 2020, 2021, 2023) with Rugg, Daruka and, conditionally, Gaskell; most manuscript scholars (Davis, Clemens), Zandbergen (formally agnostic) and Bowern/Lindemann lean toward an encoded meaningful text, largely on higher-level evidence: section/topic and scribe co-clustering, page-adjacency similarity, long-range keyword information (Montemurro & Zanette), the cost and multi-scribe collaboration implied by a 200+ page vellum codex, and the failure of any generator to reproduce all features simultaneously. (3) Hoax proponents answer that those higher-level features are confounds of per-scribe/per-section self-citation and have not been tested against MS-length structured gibberish. (4) 2023-2026 output is thin on peer-reviewed tests: Timm & Schinner 2023 (rebuttal essay), Greshko 2025 (cipher feasibility), Timm 2026 and Parisel 2025-26 (preprints). The December 2026 Malta conference CFP still frames the question as open ('hoax, natural language, or enciphered') and bars proposed solutions. Field's operative position: meaninglessness is neither established nor excluded; the burden has shifted to whichever side can reproduce or explain the page/section/scribe-level structure with a fully specified model. (Checked: primary texts of Gaskell & Bowern 2022, Bowern & Gaskell 2022, Bowern & Lindemann 2021, Timm 2014/2016, Zandbergen 2021 and voynich.nu entropy pages, Montemurro & Zanette 2013, Reddy & Knight 2011, Lindemann 2022; abstracts only for Rugg 2004, Rugg & Taylor 2016, Schinner 2007, Timm & Schinner 2020/2021/2023, Daruka 2020, Greshko 2025. Not checked: paywalled full texts of the Cryptologia papers; user must verify quoted numbers against them. All output unvalidated until user review.)

**Decisive tests proposed.**
- Pre-registered generator bake-off at full manuscript length: run (a) the Timm-Schinner self-citation generator, (b) Rugg/Zandbergen table-grille or wheel output, (c) Naibbe-enciphered Latin/Italian, (d) long-form human gibberish, and (e) enciphered medieval herbals through one identical metric battery that includes the higher-level statistics both sides invoke: Montemurro-Zanette information peak and scale (~807 words), topic/illustration/scribe co-clustering (LDA/NMF as in Sterneck et al.), ADJPAGESIM page-adjacency similarity, Currier A/B separability (Parisel's beta-binomial test), positional f/p and gallows rules, within-word character-position bias (charbias_words_mean, the one metric gibberish failed), word-length autocorrelation and repeat-distance decay. A model that reproduces all simultaneously would decide the mechanism; systematic failure of every meaningless generator on the same feature set would be strong evidence for encoded content.
- MS-length human gibberish experiment (Gaskell & Bowern's stated open test): have several writers produce 30k+ words over weeks with plant/astronomical illustrations and section breaks, then test whether keyword clustering, illustration-topic correlation and A/B-like drift emerge spontaneously; if yes, the higher-level anti-hoax evidence collapses; if not, it survives.
- Illustration-text mutual information under confound control: predict illustration class (herbal, balneological, astronomical, pharmaceutical) or plant identity from text using held-out pages while controlling for scribal hand, quire and physical adjacency; self-citation predicts prediction accuracy falls to chance once adjacency and hand are partialled out, whereas encoded content predicts residual signal, especially for labels that recur across the herbal and pharmaceutical sections depicting the same plant.
- Copy-order test of autocopy: self-citation predicts that similar-word density is a function of physical writing order (including across page and bifolio boundaries in the original quire sequence) and that Currier B pages were written after A; test against codicological page order, ink/multispectral layering and Davis's hand assignments; a shared vocabulary across hands and non-adjacent pages that tracks subject rather than writing order would falsify simple self-citation.
- Ultimately decisive: a key that deciphers held-out passages into coherent text with out-of-sample validation (as the Malta 2026 CFP implies, no proposed solution so far has met this bar); short of that, demonstrate a lower bound on text-to-image mutual information significantly above what any calibrated structured-gibberish null achieves.

## 2. Cipher

**Question.** The Voynich manuscript text (Beinecke MS 408) is enciphered natural language. Sub-question: which cipher classes are excluded by reproducible statistics, which are merely unlikely, and which remain live as of September 2026.

**State of the discussion.** Strongest counter to the hypothesis first: no proposed cipher, including the best current model (Naibbe, 2025), simultaneously reproduces Currier A/B, the line-position effects (Currier 1976; Feaster 2022), the near-zero token-to-token predictability (Rozanova & Temerev 2026), and the word-length/entropy profile, while human-generated and algorithmic gibberish (Gaskell & Bowern 2022; Timm & Schinner 2020) match 41/42 tested metrics. The enciphered-language hypothesis is therefore live but not favoured over structured meaninglessness.

Definitively excluded (reproducible, transcription-robust): (1) monoalphabetic substitution of any European language (entropy invariance; Bennett 1976; Zandbergen; Lindemann & Bowern 2021); (2) substitution plus conventional scribal abbreviation, or of vowel-less European/Semitic plaintext (Lindemann & Bowern 2021); (3) Vigenere-type polyalphabetic substitution (would raise, not lower, h2 and cannot generate the slot grammar; Stolfi 2000); (4) letter-level transposition or Cardan-grille rearrangement of a real plaintext (H0 mismatch, rigid within-word order, line-position dependence; Parisel 2026). Merely unlikely / constrained: verbose homophonic substitution with nulls (feasible in principle per Naibbe and Polygraphia III, but incomplete on Currier B, line effects and token predictability); nomenclator/codebook or constructed language (compatible with word grammar, no positive evidence); an unenciphered low-entropy non-European language (nearest h2 2.77 vs 2.1-2.5); within-word anagramming (Hauer & Kondrak) as a component. Live and unresolved: meaningless structured text vs multi-step cipher. Every published claimed decipherment 2014-2026 (Bax, Hauer-Kondrak, Gibbs, Cheshire, Ardic, Caveney, Gladyseva, Schechter, LLM-assisted) lacks a reproducible key and fails Fagin Davis's minimum criteria.

**Decisive tests proposed.**
- Pre-registered blind decipherment: a claimant publishes the complete key/procedure; an independent party applies it to 10 unselected folios (5 Currier A, 5 Currier B) and to a control of shuffled-glyph Voynichese; acceptance requires grammatical plaintext in the claimed language at a rate significantly above the control and consistent across both Currier languages (or an explicit, documented key change explaining A/B).
- Line-boundary test for cipher class: encipher long Latin/Italian samples with each candidate system (Naibbe, Polygraphia III, Pelling verbose scheme) as a continuous stream and separately with a per-line reset; check whether Currier's zero cross-line repeats in 25k words, the 1/10 line-initial depletion of ct-words, and Feaster's rightwardness scores emerge. Only per-line procedures can pass; a stream cipher that fails excludes that class.
- Token-predictability discriminator: compute Rozanova & Temerev's next-token information (VMS <1% of token entropy; controls 2-10%) on Naibbe, Polygraphia III, self-citation and human-gibberish outputs of matched length. If every plaintext-preserving cipher stays above ~2% while gibberish generators reach <1%, verbose substitution is excluded; if Naibbe-type output also reaches <1%, the test cannot separate cipher from hoax and unicity becomes the issue.
- Transliteration-robustness requirement: any claimed statistic or decipherment must hold across ZL, GC (v101), IT, Currier, Cuva and the Zattera slot alphabet using Zandbergen's STA/IVTFF tooling; results that flip between alphabets (as hapax share does, 14-20%) are inadmissible.
- Unicity-distance computation for the surviving class: for a verbose homophonic cipher with the Naibbe key space (6 unigram + 36 bigram homophones over ~26 letters plus nulls), compute the unicity distance against ~38,000 tokens / ~155,000 glyphs. If the ciphertext is shorter than unicity, no unique decipherment is provable in principle and the field should stop adjudicating 'solutions' and instead test generative models.
- A/B key-switch test: fit Parisel's folio-level boolean switch (vowel after ch/sh) as a table change in a Naibbe-type cipher and check whether one switch reproduces the full A/B signature (final -dy/-89 rates, qo- doubling, Zattera's section-specific grammars). Success supports 'two keys, one system'; failure supports two scribal generation habits.

## 3. Natural language

**Question.** Voynichese (Beinecke MS 408) is statistically language-like at the word and document level (Zipfian frequencies, normal unigram word entropy, medium morphological complexity, page topics, section-level keyword clustering) but is aberrant at the character level and in word sequencing (conditional character entropy h2 ~2.0-2.2 bits vs >2.5-4 for every tested language; rigid word-internal slot grammar; near-absent bigram word order; strong line-position effects; per-folio Currier A/B switch). The natural-language properties it does have are exactly those that human gibberish (Gaskell & Bowern 2022), self-citation generators (Timm & Schinner 2020) and verbose ciphers (Greshko 2025) also reproduce; the properties it lacks are those a one-to-one script of any known language would have. The evidence therefore supports 'non-random, structured, and not a simple substitution of a known language' but does not currently discriminate between an enciphered/encoded natural language and a structured meaningless text; the only features not yet reproduced by any published generator are the 0.2-bit edge-glyph coupling across token boundaries, the ~70% hapax vocabulary, and the large-scale topic/section structure at manuscript length.

**State of the discussion.** No consensus, but the disagreement has narrowed to a specific shape. Agreed facts (all camps): Zipfian word frequencies; normal unigram word entropy and top-word proportions; medium MATTR/type-token profile; near-binomial word lengths; h2 of ~2.0-2.2 bits (EVA) far below every tested language; rigid word-internal slot grammar; near-absent bigram word order but strong glyph coupling across word boundaries; line- and paragraph-position effects; a real Currier A/B split now shown to be largely a per-folio switch; page-level topics and adjacent-page similarity. Interpretive camps: (1) Yale group (Bowern, Lindemann, Sterneck; Annual Review 2021) - enciphered/encoded natural language of medium morphological complexity in a non-structure-preserving script, with the document-level structure as the key argument; but Bowern's own 2022 papers concede that low-level statistics are reproduced by human gibberish and that the correct null model is gibberish, not shuffled text, and that no tested cipher matches Voynichese on all metrics. (2) Hoax/structured-nonsense camp (Rugg & Taylor 2016; Schinner 2007; Timm & Schinner 2020, 2023, and their 2021 Cryptologia review calling B&L deficient) - self-citation or grille generation reproduces Zipf, binomial lengths, line effects and A/B; not yet shown to reproduce section keywords at manuscript length, edge-glyph coupling, or the hapax profile. (3) 2025-2026 quantitative wave (Greshko's Naibbe cipher; Parisel's directional/boundary and A/B-switch papers; Rozanova & Temerev's units paper) - converging on 'cipher-like layered constraints' in which glyphs are not letters, tokens are not words and spaces are not uniform, with both a verbose homophonic cipher and a self-citation generator reproducing the entropy/unit-scale/weak-order profile and both failing on edge coupling and vocabulary openness. Net: the evidence excludes random text, a one-to-one substitution of any known language, heavily agglutinating or purely isolating plaintexts if tokens are words, and simple grille output; it does not currently discriminate enciphered language from structured meaninglessness. | moderate | Long-range information structure: content-like keywords cluster across sections at the same scale as in natural-language texts (Montemurro & Zanette), and network/intermittency metrics are compatible with natural languages and incompatible with shuffled text (Amancio et al.). | Montemurro & Zanette 2013; Amancio et al. 2013; Landini 2001 | The null model is wrong: both papers compare against shuffled text, and Gaskell & Bowern 2022 argue the proper null is human-produced gibberish, whose word-placement bias within a sample (wordbias_mean) already matches or exceeds meaningful text and whose properties would drift over months of writing, producing section-level clustering; self-citation copies vocabulary from the current section by construction. Pelling (Cipher Mysteries 2013) argues the 30 'informative' words may be the least informative (cipher container layer) and that M&Z assume a homogeneity across A/B that does not hold. M&Z's argument that these features were 'out of reach of sixteenth-century mathematics' is a non sequitur: a generative procedure need not be aware of the statistics it produces. Amancio also assume no polyalphabetic encryption. |
| moderate | Pages have topics and are in order: within-page word bursts and adjacent-page similarity match meaningful texts, and unsupervised topic models align with illustration sections and scribal hands. | Reddy & Knight 2011 Tables 6-7; Sterneck et al. 2021; Bowern & Lindemann 2021 §4.3 | Scribe and topic are confounded (scribe 1 = Voynich A herbal; scribes 2-5 = B), so clusters may reflect individual scribal habits or vocabulary drift rather than content; Gaskell & Bowern 2022 explicitly say gibberish at manuscript length has not been tested and that participants spontaneously reused an invented 'label' word around its illustration ('pitshol'). Currier A/B vocabulary differences alone would produce section-level clustering. No quantitative purity/agreement metric is reported by Sterneck et al. (*). |
| strong | The line is a functional unit: word length and glyph choice depend on position in the line and paragraph, unlike prose. | Currier 1976; Vogt 2012; Reddy & Knight 2011; Bowern & Lindemann 2021 §4.1 | Interpretation splits three ways. Bowern & Lindemann treat the effects as 'typographical' positional allographs comparable to scribal punctuation, i.e. compatible with encoded language. Timm 2014 derives them from copying the first/last word of the line above. Gaskell & Bowern 2022 found character-position-in-line bias is the single most important variable separating gibberish from meaningful text, but the VMS value was not significantly different from meaningful text on that metric. Vogt attributes the overall length drop-off to a 'short words make long lines' selection effect present in natural text too. |
| strong | The Currier A/B distinction is real and reduces largely to a per-folio binary switch in a few character-pair choices, with the same slot grammar in both. | Currier 1976; Bowern & Lindemann 2021; Parisel 2026; Lindemann 2022 | A per-folio boolean switch in a phoneme-like slot looks more like a cipher key or scribal convention than a dialect difference (Parisel reads it as a 'low-resolution projection of a higher-dimensional generative system'); Timm & Schinner explain A vs B as drift and increasing repetitiveness of the copying process over time (B written later, more repetitive); Bowern & Lindemann themselves say A and B are 'two methods of encoding at least one natural language' rather than two languages. |
| weak | The character stream is optimized right-to-left at the n-gram level even though the manuscript is written left-to-right. | Parisel 2025 (arXiv 2509.10573) | The author concedes the RTL signal 'could be a byproduct of a cipher or a scribal artifact rather than a natural language', the comparison set is four languages, and Hebrew/Arabic themselves fail to show a consistent RTL signature under the method, which undermines its validity as a directionality detector. Single transcription (EVA) only. |
| weak | Voynichese is an abjad (consonant-only script), explaining the binomial word lengths and the two-class letter structure. | Reddy & Knight 2011 §3.1, §4.1; Hauer & Kondrak 2016 | Bowern & Lindemann 2021: abjads have higher h2 than alphabets (Hebrew/Arabic ~3.5-4 per Zandbergen's ranges), whereas Voynichese is lower; almost every Voynich word contains one of the Sukhotin 'vowels' (a, o, e, h, i), unlike abjad text; the HMM split is likely driven by word-final-only glyphs (m, n, g), i.e. positional allographs, not vowel loss. Hauer & Kondrak produced no grammatical sentences; Bowern & Lindemann call the Hebrew claim 'unconvincing'. Lindemann 2022 ranks Semitic as 'possible but less likely' by MATTR/MCW. |
| moderate | Voynichese is a syllabary-like or verbose (many-to-one / homophonic) encoding of a European language, which would reconcile normal word-level statistics with abnormal character-level statistics. | Bowern & Lindemann 2021 §6; Bowern & Gaskell 2022; Greshko 2025; Rozanova & Temerev 2026 | Bowern & Gaskell 2022 found no single manipulation matches Voynich on all metrics and that 'no manuscript is as close as the other samples of Voynichese'; several gibberish samples were equidistant with the enciphered Latin. A verbose cipher must also explain why 15th-century plaintext would leave no decipherable trace after a century of attack. |
| weak | Long-range correlation/Hurst-type statistics do not match any single natural language. | Landini 2001; Schinner 2007; Arutyunov et al. 2016 | Methods are heterogeneous (character series with/without spaces, binary encodings), sensitive to transcription and sample length, and no study reports a Voynich Hurst exponent with confidence intervals against matched-length controls; the same 'beyond one line' correlation is what self-citation from the line above predicts (Timm & Schinner). Gaskell & Bowern's positive word-length autocorrelation is the only long-range statistic tested against human gibberish, and it favoured gibberish. |
| moderate | The vocabulary is open and hapax-rich, yet built from a closed set of near-neighbour variants. | Reddy & Knight 2011; voynich.nu/a4_word.html; Timm 2014; Rozanova & Temerev 2026 | For the hoax side, Timm's copy-and-modify process naturally yields many one-off variants ('strange words usually occur only once'); for the language side, Lindemann 2022 reports the hapax proportion is 'middle range' among languages. Rozanova & Temerev found neither their self-citation generator nor Naibbe reproduced the 70% hapax rate, so this is presently unexplained by any tested model; it is also inflated by uncertain word spaces and transcription disagreement (45% of tokens). |
| moderate | At the sub-paragraph scale Voynichese is statistically closer to human-produced meaningless text than to meaningful text (Gaskell & Bowern 2022). | Gaskell & Bowern 2022 (CEUR Vol-3313 paper4; data at github.com/danielgaskell/voynich) | The authors state the study 'cannot and does not attempt to prove that the VMS is gibberish'; samples are too short to test topic/section structure, which 'pose[s] a serious challenge to proponents of the hoax hypothesis'; 20 of 42 participants had been taught VMS statistics (contamination effects found small); Latin-alphabet writing cannot mimic Voynich glyph typography; and the companion paper (Bowern & Gaskell 2022) shows several phoneme-conflating ciphers of Latin/Uzbek land equally close to the VMS, so proximity to gibberish is not proximity to gibberish alone. Bowern & Lindemann 2021 (fn. 4) report that sustained language-like nonsense becomes very difficult beyond ~100 words. |

**Decisive tests proposed.**
- Manuscript-length gibberish test: have volunteers (or a Timm-Schinner self-citation generator tuned to observed human behaviour) produce ~38,000 tokens over weeks with illustrations, then run Montemurro & Zanette's information-vs-scale measure (VMS peak ~807 words), Reddy & Knight's ADJPAGESIM (VMS B 38.8%) and Sterneck-style NMF topic/section alignment. Gaskell & Bowern 2022 name this as the untested gap; if gibberish reproduces the section-level structure, the Yale group's main argument for language collapses; if it cannot, the hoax hypothesis loses its last unaddressed feature.
- Joint-profile benchmark: require any generator (grille, self-citation, Naibbe-class cipher) to reproduce simultaneously h2 ~2.0-2.2 (EVA), binomial word lengths, Zipf, ~70% hapax types, 0.2-bit final-to-initial glyph MI across boundaries, ~80% end-to-start boundary rate, first/second-word line effects, paragraph-initial gallows in ~85% of paragraphs, and the per-folio ch/sh switch, evaluated separately for Currier A and B. Rozanova & Temerev 2026 and Parisel 2026 publish code and controls for this; a model passing all is decisive for its class, and the two features no model yet passes (edge coupling, hapax rate) are the discriminators to target.
- Re-tokenize on 'certain' spaces only: using Rozanova & Temerev's separator classification (uncertain separators 0.494 internality index, image-gap AUC 0.905) and the Zandbergen-Landini transliteration, recompute MATTR/MCW, word-length distribution, Zipf slope and bigram predictability. If the binomial shape and medium-morphology placement survive, word-level language arguments stand; if they collapse into a different distribution, the entire word-level case (Lindemann 2022, B&L §3) rests on an artefact of assumed word boundaries.
- Localize the A/B switch: test whether Parisel's per-folio boolean switch aligns with Davis's five hands, with quire/gathering, with illustration section, or is arbitrary per folio. Scribe-aligned = dialect/orthographic habit (compatible with language); quire- or arbitrary-aligned and independent of scribe = cipher-key or generation-parameter behaviour; bimodal within a single scribe's run is strong evidence against a natural dialect reading.
- Constrained decipherment attack on the verbose-cipher class: extend Rozanova & Temerev's calibrated substitution attack (null for Voynich) to Naibbe-type many-to-one schemes with a 15th-century Latin/Italian language model; a recovered plaintext segment is decisive for language, while a demonstrated information-theoretic ceiling (ciphertext unigram/bigram capacity insufficient to carry Latin at observed token counts) would be decisive against that cipher class.
- Illustration-adjacent word-length test: Gaskell & Bowern observed 17/31 gibberish writers choosing word lengths to fit around drawings; measure whether Voynich tokens abutting illustrations (e.g. f29v) have a length distribution or hapax rate significantly different from mid-paragraph tokens, controlling for line position. A significant fit-to-space effect favours composition without prior text; its absence favours copying/encoding of a pre-existing text.
- Cross-scribe consistency of collocations: check whether the few syntagmatic regularities B&L report (qo- words after -y words in B; aiin after 1-2-glyph words; o- before gallows) hold with the same strength across Davis's scribes 2-5 and across quires written at different times. Stable rules across independent hands are hard for intuitive gibberish and unnecessary for self-citation; they are expected for a shared cipher or language.
- Independent replication of Gaskell & Bowern with Voynich-like typography: repeat the gibberish protocol using an invented glyph set with positional allographs available, uncontaminated participants, and 1,000+ word samples, then retest the one metric gibberish failed (within-word character-position bias) and the random-forest classification; if uncontaminated writers with allographs reproduce the slot grammar, the last small-scale feature separating Voynichese from gibberish disappears.

## 4. Physical object and provenance

**Question.** The physical object and its documented history establish, independently of any reading of the text, that Beinecke MS 408 is a genuine early-15th-century European (Alpine/N. Italian-German) production written by several hands on freshly prepared calfskin with period inks, whose 17th-century Prague-Rome provenance is archivally secured; this rules out a modern (Voynich-era) forgery and constrains any hoax scenario to a coordinated 1420s-era multi-scribe project, while leaving cipher vs. constructed/natural language vs. contemporary meaningless text undecided.

**State of the discussion.** Specialist consensus (Beinecke/Yale, Zandbergen, Davis, Clemens, Bowern) holds that MS 408 is an authentic early-15th-century (c.1404-1438) European codex written by several hands on freshly made calfskin with period inks and pigments, that it is misbound relative to its original order, that it was in German- and French/Occitan-speaking hands within decades of manufacture, and that its ownership is continuously documented from c.1600-1665 (Tepenecz, Baresch, Marci, Kircher) to 1912 and 1969. The modern-forgery hypothesis is rejected by all mainstream researchers and defended only by a small group (chiefly SantaColoma). Genuinely open questions: exact place of production (N. Italy vs. German/Alpine), whether the five-scribe count and singulion quire model hold up to independent replication, the original folio order, the reading of f116v, and the nature of the text itself, which the physical evidence constrains to a 1420s multi-scribe production but does not classify as cipher, language, or contemporary hoax. Caveats: the radiocarbon result has no peer-reviewed publication; McCrone's spectra were never released; Davis's scribe attributions are expert judgment. Checked: radiocarbon figures, McCrone pigments, MSI 2024 findings, provenance documents, forgery counterarguments, origin arguments (via web sources). Not checked: full text of Davis 2020 (PDF not text-extractable; scribe-to-section mapping given from memory and asterisked), the released 2009 lab report itself, Clemens 2016 essays directly. User must verify starred items against Davis 2020 and the primary lab report before citing.

**Decisive tests proposed.**
- Independent, blind replication of Davis's five-scribe attribution (e.g., automated palaeographic clustering on the 2014 high-resolution scans) and a test of whether hand boundaries coincide with Currier A/B boundaries at the line level rather than the page level.
- Parchment DNA/isotope analysis of bifolia (advocated by Pelling) to identify animal groupings, which would test the singulion hypothesis, reconstruct original quire composition, and localize the herd/region of the calfskins.
- Full multispectral and XRF imaging of all folios (only 10 imaged in 2014) to recover erased marginalia, further ex libris or catalogue marks, and to compare ink chemistry of quire marks, month names, f116v and main text at scale.
- Peer-reviewed publication of the 2009 Arizona radiocarbon data (raw fractions, pretreatment, calibration) and release of McCrone's spectra so both can be independently re-evaluated.
- Archival search in Prague, Vienna and Innsbruck records for a pre-1600 trace (Rudolf II's Kunstkammer inventories, Tepenecz's library catalogue, Widemann/Dee-Kelley sale records) to test the 600-ducat purchase claim and close the 1440-1600 gap.
- Systematic corpus comparison of the month-name spellings against dated Occitan/Catalan/French documents, and of the f116v hand against dated German charters c.1420-1460, to narrow when and where the annotations were added.
- A physical test of ink-parchment interaction age (e.g., iron migration halos, gall-ink corrosion depth) on a few folios, which would directly address the residual 'old vellum, modern ink' objection.

## 5. State of the art 2022-2026 and tools

**Question.** Between 2022 and 2026 Voynich research converged on a shared, transliteration-grounded statistical description of Voynichese (low conditional entropy, rigid word-slot grammar, line/paragraph/position effects, a real but non-primitive Currier A/B split, gradual drift across the codex) while failing to converge on a generative mechanism. Three mechanisms remain live and each now has a reproducible model that matches part of the signature: (1) meaningless-but-structured generation (Timm & Schinner self-citation; Gaskell & Bowern human gibberish), (2) verbose homophonic/cipher-like encoding of a Romance/Latin plaintext (Greshko's Naibbe cipher; Parisel's directional-constraint results), (3) an unusual natural or constructed language (Ponnaluri 2024, minority). No claimed decipherment since 2022 has gained any specialist acceptance; the most consequential new empirical inputs were material (2014 Lazarus Project MSI released 2024; Yale XRF 2025) rather than textual, and the biggest infrastructure change was Zandbergen's IVTFF 2.0 / STA1 / RF reference transliteration, which every serious 2024-2026 quantitative paper now uses.

**State of the discussion.** Strongest counter to any 'consensus' framing: the field has no agreed mechanism and the three camps each gained a reproducible model in 2022-2026, so agreement is on the description, not the explanation. What is broadly accepted among the specialists cited here (Zandbergen, Bowern, Davis, Timm/Schinner, Pelling, the voynich.ninja core): (1) the codex is a genuine early-15th-century object, not a modern forgery (radiocarbon 1404-38; Davis 2024 MSI/XRF); (2) the text is not a simple substitution of a known language (Marci's own f1r alphabet attempt notwithstanding); (3) Voynichese has a rigid word grammar (Zattera slots, ~87% conformity), anomalously low conditional entropy, strong line/paragraph/drawing-position effects (Steckley 2024), and a real Currier A/B difference that is now seen as one axis of a continuous or higher-dimensional drift rather than two discrete languages (Parisel 2026 and Timm 2026 agree on this from opposite camps); (4) Zipf/Heaps/brevity-law compliance proves nothing about meaning (Gaskell & Bowern 2022; Timm & Schinner); (5) the ZL/GC/RF IVTFF 2.0 files and STA1 are the standard data, and results should be reported against a named file version; (6) no decipherment since 2022 (or before) has met the bar, and LLM-generated 'solutions' are now explicitly excluded by voynich.nu and the 2026 conference. Live disputes: (a) meaningless generation (self-citation; human gibberish) vs verbose homophonic cipher (Naibbe) vs natural/constructed language, with Rozanova & Temerev 2026 arguing both leading generators fail on edge-glyph coupling and hapax-rich vocabulary; (b) whether Davis's five scribes are real palaeographic entities or a re-labelling of Currier A/B plus label pages (Timm 2026 vs Davis 2020/Farrugia 2022); (c) directionality (Parisel's RTL-inside-words claim vs LTR writing); (d) what the units are (glyph vs multi-glyph, space vs uncertain space); (e) whether computational methods such as LSA can do codicology (Davis/Layfield vs Pelling's demand for physical/DNA evidence); (f) whether the 1599 Widemann sale is the VM's entry into Rudolf's library. **Decisive tests proposed.**
- Re-run Parisel's currier-models scripts against ZL3b, GC and RF1b separately and against Timm's self-citation output, Greshko's Naibbe output and Gaskell's gibberish samples: if the per-folio ch/sh switch appears only in the VM, it is a property of the source, not of transliteration; if a generator reproduces it, the switch is not evidence for meaning.
- Positional/line-effect test on generators: score Naibbe, self-citation and human gibberish on Steckley's drawing-adjacent token-length shortening and on Gaskell & Bowern's short-offset word-length autocorrelation; the current reports say Naibbe fails autocorrelation and self-citation was never tested on drawing adjacency.
- Edge-glyph mutual-information and hapax-ratio test (Rozanova & Temerev): any proposed mechanism must reproduce 0.2-bit token-edge coupling with <1% token-succession entropy and ~70% singleton types simultaneously; publish as a shared benchmark on the RF1b file.
- Scribe-hypothesis falsification: blind palaeographic re-coding of k-crossbar and n-flourish variants on a random folio sample by independent coders, compared with Davis's assignments and with Currier/label-page partitions; Timm predicts variants on nearly every page, Davis predicts page-level segregation.
- Full-codex multispectral and XRF campaign plus bifolio parchment DNA/collagen matching to fix quire order (the Pelling-Davis dispute); the 2014 ten-folio set is insufficient for quire reconstruction.
- Archival test of Guzy: locate any inventory or correspondence naming the Widemann 1599 barrel's contents; a title or description matching the VM would convert a circumstantial link into provenance.
- Decipherment gate (applies to every future claim): publish a key that decrypts held-out folios not used in its construction, on a named IVTFF file, with the plaintext independently readable by a philologist of the claimed language; none of the 2022-2026 claims meets this.
- Transliteration-sensitivity audit: repeat any headline statistic (entropy, slot conformity, Currier split) across ZL, GC, IT and RF and with uncertain spaces treated three ways (space, no space, dropped); results that flip are transliteration artefacts.
