# Reading of Charles IX to Philibert du Croc

Source: George Lasry's plaintext overlay, published by Satoshi Tomokiyo on Cryptiana, 25 May 2022. This is a diplomatic working transcription of that overlay, checked against the DECODE and Gallica facsimiles. Capitalization and `V/U` follow the overlay. Brackets mark material that is hidden, unread, or too uncertain to assert; the overlay itself prints several groups as question marks.

## Normalized sense

Charles says that du Croc knows the men with whom he must deal and needs no further advice. He should follow his earlier and current instructions, especially where the peace of Scotland is concerned. Because pressure cannot presently be applied on behalf of “my sister” without harming her, Charles turns to the welfare of “my nephew”: James VI is to be well brought up, retain the traditional affection of his predecessors for the French king and his house, and be surrounded by a peace, government, and council favorable to that end.

Du Croc is to ensure discreetly that James neither marries nor concludes a marriage before Charles is informed and can communicate his wishes. He must retain the hearts of the Scots and prevent any diminution of their accustomed attachment to France.

The postscript makes the strategic aim explicit: the English must not be allowed to encroach on Scotland. Du Croc is to act prudently so that the Scots recognize France's desire for their preservation, while avoiding incidents that could obstruct the friendship. Several final clauses—including part of the passage about the English ambassadors and a possible treaty—remain too incomplete in the published overlay for a responsible continuous translation.
