# Sir Henry Norreys to Cecil, 1567-68 — BL Add MS 4136 ff. 160-161 (DECODE R9251, part of R9250)

Status: read in part (21 Sept 2026). Catalogue 90.

## What the record is

DECODE R9251 ("Sr. Henry Norreys", 9 Mar 1567, 3 pp.) is three pages of Patrick Forbes's 1730s deciphering
file (Add MS 4136; see [smith1562](../smith1562/NOTES.md)). Only the top of p.1 (f.161) is Norreys's. The rest
is Throckmorton 27 Aug 1562, Middlemore 8 Apr 1563 and Smith 7 Mar 1562/3, all in other ciphers.

- The date is Old Style: "9 Mar. 1567/8" = **9 March 1568** (DECODE's "1567" is the OS year).
- The Norreys series starts on f.160 (DECODE R9250 p.2): 4 June 1567, 6 July 1567, 10 July 1567,
  6 Feb 1567/8 (groups 1-11 on f.160, 12-23 on f.161), then 9 Feb, 24 Feb and 9 Mar 1567/8 on f.161.
- Forbes copied only the ciphered words, numbered. The target letter has three items: (1) a name sign,
  (2) a five-sign word, (3) a name sign.
- Transcription, ad hoc token names: `transcription.txt`. Readings: `reading.md`.

## The key

No Norreys key is on DECODE. The volume's key records (R9257 Percy, R9260 Throckmorton 1-2, R9261 Smith/Croft,
R9262 Throckmorton 3) do not match. **The key is Tomokiyo's partial reconstruction of the Cecil-Norris cipher
(1568)**, made from *Cabala* (1663). There the "Postscript" printed after Cecil's letter of 27 Sept 1568 is really
the decipherment of its cipher passages (Davys). Source: cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/elizabeth.htm, "Cecil-Norris
Ciphers (1567-1570)". Table, as used here (signs may be written in any orientation):

a T ⊤ ⊢ ⊣ 1 · b ξ ~ · c ≠ # · d L ⅃ Γ t · e π ⊔ ⊏ · f ϖ ∈ · h c ∩ · i δ ♭ 9 · l ⌐o o-o ∞ · m G ω ⌒ ⊃ ·
n m ш · o λ · p 4 · r 2 3̲ ♃ · s 3 3̄ 5. · t 5 3̈ s̈ 8 · u 7 7̈ · y 9

Name signs (Norreys's letters): ꝺ = the French King (6 Feb 5, 13; 9 Feb 5; 9 Mar 1), Xr = the Protestants,
Ec = the Prince of Condé, fk = the Admiral (9 Feb 6, 8, 9 and 24 Feb 2, 3, 4, same order in both CSP entries).

## Open

- 9 Mar (2) and (3), as graded above; 4 June 1567 groups 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 and 10 July (2) are not read. The
  4 June tail matches "… France, Flanders, and England" in length only.
- 6 Feb groups 7, 9, 12, 16, 17, 20, 23; 9 Feb 10, 11; 24 Feb 1, 7.
- The originals in TNA SP 70/96-97 probably carry interlinear decipherments; not seen (State Papers Online).

## Steps

1. DECODE images of R9251, R9250 and the key records fetched (cookie).
2. Throckmorton 1-3 and Smith alphabets compared: no match.
3. Seven Norreys extracts tokenised; CSP Foreign viii entries fetched from BHO as cribs.
4. Name-sign order across 9 and 24 Feb gives Protestants / Prince / Admiral. A crib search over the calendar
   vocabulary (work/csolve.py) was inconclusive.
5. Tomokiyo's Cecil-Norris table (from *Cabala*) applied: ruin, demaund, reason, remain, Master Stewarde fall
   out and match the calendar. The target's three items were read against no. 2054.
