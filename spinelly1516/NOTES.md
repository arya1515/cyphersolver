# Thomas Spinelly to Henry VIII, Brussels, 1 February 1517 (BL Cotton MS Galba B V ff. 40-41; DECODE R8416)

Catalogue no. 100 (DECODE refill, rule-scored class C). Worked 21 Sept 2026.

Outcome: **read.** The one cipher passage in the letter is deciphered in full; it had not been printed.

## The record

DECODE R8416 ("Thommas Spynelly to unknown recipient", 1516, London, Non-decrypted, 4 pp.) is Spinelly's autograph
despatch to Henry VIII ("Please it your hyghnes to understand"), dated at the foot "Written at ~~Methlyn~~ Bruxells
the fyrst day of february M.D.XVJ", signed "your most humble and ... seruant Thommas Spynelly". The year is old style:
the letter reports the Emperor Maximilian at Lier and Mechelen with the young King Charles, the French embassy at
Vilvoorde and the cession of Verona to Lautrec (January 1517), so the date is 1 February 1517 new style. The place is
Brussels, not London; the recipient is the King. Old foliation 33-34 shows on the leaves.

Image order on DECODE: P1 = f. 40r, P3 = f. 40v, P4 = f. 41r, P2 = f. 41v (end of the letter, subscription).

The letter is in clear English except five lines on f. 40v (P3), after "and of this opinion is the". Nothing is
written between the lines; there is no contemporary decipherment.

## Prior art

Letters and Papers Henry VIII vol. II no. 2862 (Brewer 1864; British History Online, Feb 1517 1-10) calendars the
letter under "Galba, B. v. 33" and summarises the cipher sentence: "about the 15th of this month the Emperor will go
to Cambray". The calendar gives no text of the passage, does not name the Audiencier's companion (Marliano) and does
not mention the King's entry into Brussels. So the gist was known; the passage itself was not printed. Checked 21
Sept 2026, after the decipherment had been made.

## The cipher

A simple substitution with invented signs, most of them Greek or Latin letters used for other values, plus a few word
signs. Word division is kept. `transcription.txt` has the signs; `plaintext.txt` the reading.

| plain | sign | plain | sign | plain | sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | φ | i/y | e | r | c |
| b | τ | k | λ | s | ʃ |
| c | 4 | l | v (triangle), σ? | t | k |
| d | ʒ | m | y | u | δ (w = δδ) |
| e | o | n | b | | |
| h | ρ | o | 7 | p | q |

Word signs: ϑ = of; ꝁ (k with stroke) = this; w̄ = that; ✱ (star with superscript) = the Emperor; ʒ at the end of line
2 after "the" = probably the King (Charles); δ̄ = ll (shall).

## Reading

> and of this opinion is the Audensier and misser Loys de Merlien. The which Audensier saythe that the [King] in the
> laterende of this wicke shall make his entree in to this town, and [the Emperor] about the xv day of the present
> monethe so to Cambray.

The Audiencier (named only by office, here and in clear throughout the letter) is the audiencier of Charles's
chancery, probably Philippe Haneton (C). "Misser Loys de Merlien" is Luigi Marliano, Charles's physician and councillor, later bishop of Tuy.

Grades: the alphabet is certain (about 40 letter tokens with every value used at least twice, all words English).
Doubtful: σ as a second l in "Loys" (C); the word sign after "the" at the end of line 2 read as the King (C, the
subject of "make his entree"; the Emperor has his own sign later in the sentence. The clear postscript on f. 41v
says "his magestye"'s entry was put off to await German princes, which may mean the Emperor); ✱ = the Emperor (H, from L&P's summary); "xv day" for xbʒφe (H, L&P "15th"; b here as the numeral v).

## Steps

1. Downloaded the four images with the DECODE cookie (bordeaux/decode/cookie.txt); images git-ignored.
2. Read the pages: clear English despatch, cipher only on f. 40v.
3. Crib: the 9-sign word repeated twice with equal signs at positions 4 and 8 = "audensier" (spelled so in clear
   on the same page); φbʒ = "and"; kρo = "the"; k7 = "to"; then "present monethe", "Cambray", "entree", "this town".
4. Prior art checked afterwards: L&P II 2862, gist only.
