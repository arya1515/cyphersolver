# fr. 15572 f. 201 — the same despatch as f. 196, read: Matignon, the siege, the dead

Canvas 208 right. The clear runs a line and a half further than on f. 196 — *"…faict une aultre
despesche du xxix.e du moys passé … commandement de ce siège, auquel il a … depuis qu'il est
arrivé, tant travaillé … qu'il y a gaigné"* — then 30 lines of cipher, then clear again for the
last lines, which end *"…la batterye de quinze canons"* and name **de Croy**.
Transcription in `f201_cipher.txt`.

## The check: the two leaves agree, figure for figure

ff. 196 and 201 were transcribed independently, weeks of work apart, from different images.
They share **23 runs of eight consecutive identical figures** (840 and 987 figures respectively).
Two independent transcriptions cannot agree on eight-figure strings by accident: this confirms both
that they carry the same despatch and that the transcriptions are substantially right.

## What is established

- **The first proper name out of the cipher: "le mareschal de Matignon."** It is not the language
  model filling a gap — of the figures spelling it, every one but a single `y` is in the key
  (`w-`→d, `h`→e, `4+`→m, `a`→a, `Ze`→t, `o`→i, `y`→g, `.v.`→n, `7`→o, `6`→n).
- **The code 35 = "parce que"** fires here in a sentence that needs it: *"parce que la ville est
  grande et mal aisée à assiéger."* That is Tomokiyo's published nomenclature value, confirmed in
  running text.
- **The despatch's subject**: the siege of a large town, hard to invest, with a battery of fifteen
  cannon (in the clear); the writer has lost officers not to shot but to sickness, among them the
  man who had served as **mareschal de camp de l'armée**; the maréchal de Matignon is being pressed
  to come.
- **Probable identification.** Mayenne and Matignon made an agreement *"au camp devant Castillon"*
  on 30 August 1586, so a joint siege by that army in 1586 is documented, and a large walled town
  with a fifteen-gun battery fits. This is circumstantial and is recorded as probable, not proved:
  the cipher does not name the town.

## Coverage, honestly

f. 201 reads better than f. 196 — perhaps two-thirds of it as continuous French. The names other
than Matignon still break off: "de la Garde", "de la B[…]ault". `de Croy` is in the clear, not the
cipher.
