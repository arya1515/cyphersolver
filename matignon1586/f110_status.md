# fr. 15572 f. 110 — the one leaf still unread, and exactly why

Canvas 116 right; foliation confirmed on the leaf (117 struck, **110** valid). A full page, **54
lines, wholly in cipher** from the first line — no clear opening, no marginal decipherment, nothing
to crib from. It ends "De Bellebourg … jour de Mars 1586". The hand is far smaller and denser than
any other leaf in the target: about a hundred figures to the line.

## What is established

**It is the same cipher as the other nine leaves.** Decode a sample with the solved key and score
it; then do the same with scrambled versions of that key — same symbols, same letters, values
shuffled. Chance would put the two together.

| | score per figure |
|---|---|
| f. 110 (791 figures), solved key | **−3.31** |
| f. 110, scrambled keys (mean of 3) | −5.38 |
| *gap* | **+2.07** |

For comparison the same test gives +3.80 on f. 154 and +3.06 on f. 124r, both of which read. So
f. 110 answers to this key far better than to a wrong one, but much less cleanly than a leaf whose
figures I have read correctly.

## Why it does not read, and what that means

**The obstacle is my transcription of this hand, not the cipher.** Two measurements say so:

1. Seeding a solver with the solved key and letting it move, on f. 110 it **moves 23 of 27 shared
   labels away from the key**. On the leaves that read, the key stays put. So the names I am giving
   this scribe's shapes do not correspond to the figures the key is keyed on — I am misreading the
   glyphs, not decoding them wrongly.
2. A cold solve of 975 transcribed figures reaches **−2.34 per figure** — at the −2.40 that a
   *correct* solution scores elsewhere — yet the text is not continuous French. A solve that hits
   the right score without producing language is fitting noise: it is what an internally consistent
   but wrong figure-stream looks like.

Eighteen of the 54 lines are transcribed (`f110_cipher.txt`, 975 figures). Fragments surface —
*que, des, il, par, sa, cœur, mars* — but nothing that can honestly be called a reading.

## What would finish it

Not more search, and not more of the same transcription. What this leaf needs is the **exemplar-set
work** that was done for the other hands: cut the leaf's own glyphs, cluster them, and label the
clusters by matching against figures whose values the key already fixes — so that the figure stream
is right before any decoding is attempted. That is a day's careful work on one page, and it is the
only leaf in the target that still needs it.

**Stated plainly: nine of the ten leaves are read; this one is not.**
