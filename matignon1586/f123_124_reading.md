# fr. 15572 ff. 123–124 — "Sire…": four pages of cipher, read with the solved key

Canvases 129 right – 131 left, four pages, **4,278 figures** — the largest single block in the
target. Foliation confirmed on the leaf (137 struck, 124 valid). The despatch opens in clear,
*"Sire, la dernière que j'ay eu l'honneur est du huict[iesm]e de ce moys…"*, then runs in cipher for
four pages. Transcriptions in `f123r_cipher.txt`, `f123v_cipher.txt`, `f124r_cipher.txt`,
`f124v_cipher.txt`; decoder output in `reading_f123_124.txt`.

**These leaves are in Cipher-1** — see `ciphers.md` for the retraction of my earlier claim that they
were a second key, and for the control that settled it.

## What the despatch says

The clearest stretches, from f. 124r and f. 124v:

> **Sire, quelque bo[nne] resolution de les secourir** … **en [R]ouergue** … **province** …
> **que lesdits** … **il luy pla[i]t** … **aucun secours, je suis contraint de** … **je vous puis
> dire que je puis les secourir** … **escus** … **janvier** … **recevoir** … **secret** …
> **points** … **pouvoir** …

It is of a piece with the rest of the correspondence: a commander writing to the King about
relieving places he cannot relieve, money he does not have, and a province — the Rouergue — he
cannot hold.

## Coverage, honestly

Uneven, and the unevenness is mine, not the cipher's. **f. 124r reads well**; f. 124v reads in
fragments; **f. 123r reads badly** — it was the first page I transcribed in this hand and the figure
identifications are poor. Re-transcribing f. 123r and f. 123v against the now-familiar hand would
raise the yield considerably, and needs no new cryptanalysis: the key is known and verified.
