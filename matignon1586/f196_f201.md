# ff. 196 and 201 are the same despatch: an in-depth pair with two cribs

Canvases 203 right and 208 right of ark `btv1b9061879d`. Tomokiyo lists both as undeciphered
letters in Mayenne–Forget's Cipher-1 and does not say they are related.

## They are the same letter

Both open, word for word, in the same clerk's clear hand:

> **Sire**, Depuis le partement du S.r de Bosseval, Monsieur du Mayne vous a faict une aut[re]
> despesche du xxix.e du moys passé p[ou]r vous rendre raison du commencement de ce siege, auquel …

f. 196 is the copy that goes into cipher first; f. 201 is the other copy of the same despatch,
and the two ciphered blocks are the same plaintext written out twice.

## Crib 1 — the opening

f. 196 breaks into cipher immediately after *auquel*. f. 201 keeps going in clear for another
line and a half:

> … auquel **il a depuis qu'il est arrivé tant travaillé qu'il y a gaigné** …

So the first figures of f. 196's cipher are that clause. They read as it: the sequence
`12` · a · d · e · p · u · i · s · … · `13` · l · e · s · t gives **"il a depuis qu'il est"**,
with the code groups `12` = *il* and `13` = *qui* falling exactly where Tomokiyo's nomenclature
puts them and the spelled letters agreeing with the key derived from f. 143 (ϖ = d, R = p,
ꝗ = u, n = l, Ƶe = t). That confirms the key on a second leaf, independently of f. 143.

### The alignment, checked on the leaf

The second cipher line of f. 196 was segmented into glyph boxes (`seg.py`) and read box by box.
Against the crib's *arrivé tant travaillé qu'il y a gaigné* the tail aligns exactly, nine figures
for nine letters, with `13` = *qui* sitting between them:

| figure | n | Ɛ | ơ | ɣ | ƅ | b | ʒ | ·v· | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| letter | l | y | a | g | a | i | g | n | e |

Five of those — n = l, ·v· = n, t = e, Ɛ = y, ơ = a — are exactly the values derived from f. 143,
recovered here from a completely independent crib. The spelling is *trauaile* with one *l*, which
is what makes the count come out. On a second pass — the block flattened with `flatten.py` and tiled with a band tall enough that
mis-centring cannot clip it — the **whole line** resolved, thirty figures for thirty letters, and
the decoder returns the crib text from the figures alone:

> `D se eps f t ct a .v. m Ze L a BOX x b he G 13 n E a r a B g8 .v. t g .v. 7 f L g`
> → **r riue tant trauaile qu il y a gaigne un ouru…**

That fixes twenty-one figures of this hand with certainty and independently of f. 143:

| figure | Δ | ʃe | ε | ʃ | t | Ꝭ | ơ | ·v· | m | ℋe | £ | ⊐ | x | ƅ | ʰe | G | 13 | n | Ɛ | ʋ | ʙ | ʒ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| letter | r | r | i | u | e | t | a | n | t | t | r | u | a | i | l | e | qui | l | y | g | i | g |

Note ε and G are two different round figures, **i** and **e**, and ʃ and the ʃe ligature are two
more, **u** and **r**: exactly the confusable pairs that the eye cannot separate without a crib.
The working key for this hand is `key196.json`.

One thing is not yet accounted for: about five figures stand between the clear *auquel* and the
`12`, where the crib has nothing. Either the two copies diverge slightly at that point — the
duplicate need not be word-perfect — or those figures are being mis-segmented by eye. Settling it
needs the glyph segmenter (`seg.py`) rather than the eye, and it is the first thing to do when
this is picked up: the crib fixes the plaintext, so the segmented boxes of these two lines can be
*labelled* letter by letter, which is exactly the labelled atlas the confusable shapes need.

## Crib 2 — the ending

f. 201 comes **out** of cipher for its last three lines, where f. 196 stays in cipher to the foot
of the page:

> … qu'après demain l'on commencera la batterie de quinze canons, dont il y a apparence qu'ils se
> rendront. Mayans, par accoustumé à bien assiéger les places …

That clause is therefore a second crib, this time at the end of f. 196's block.

## Why this matters

1. **Two cribs**, at the head and the foot of a 32-line block, on a leaf nobody has read.
2. **The pair is in depth.** The same plaintext enciphered twice, by a cipher with several
   homophones per letter, means that at every position the two figures must decode to the same
   letter. Aligning the two blocks turns a one-copy reading problem into a constraint-satisfaction
   problem with double the evidence — and, in practice, a transcription slip in one copy is
   corrected by the other. That is worth more here than any improvement to the language model,
   because transcription is what limits this cipher (see `NOTES.md`).
3. It fixes the date: the despatch reports the opening of a siege, the previous one being of the
   29th of the preceding month, with a battery of fifteen cannon to open "après demain".
