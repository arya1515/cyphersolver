# ff. 196 and 201 are the same despatch: an in-depth pair with two cribs

Canvases 203 right and 208 right of ark `btv1b9061879d`. Tomokiyo lists both as undeciphered
letters in Mayenne–Forget's Cipher-1 and does not say they are related.

## They are the same letter

Both open, word for word, in the same clerk's clear hand:

> **Sire**, Depuis le partement du S.r de Bosseval, Monsieur du Mayne vous a faict une aut[re]
> despesche du xxix.e du moys passé p[ou]r vous rendre raison du commencement de ce siege, auquel …

f. 196 is the copy that goes into cipher first; f. 201 is the other copy of the same despatch,
and the two ciphered blocks are the same plaintext written out twice.

## Crib 1 — the opening

f. 196 breaks into cipher immediately after *auquel*. f. 201 keeps going in clear for another
line and a half:

> … auquel **il a depuis qu'il est arrivé tant travaillé qu'il y a gaigné** …

So the first figures of f. 196's cipher are that clause. They read as it: the sequence
`12` · a · d · e · p · u · i · s · … · `13` · l · e · s · t gives **"il a depuis qu'il est"**,
with the code groups `12` = *il* and `13` = *qui* falling exactly where Tomokiyo's nomenclature
puts them and the spelled letters agreeing with the key derived from f. 143 (ϖ = d, R = p,
ꝗ = u, n = l, Ƶe = t). That confirms the key on a second leaf, independently of f. 143.

### The alignment, checked on the leaf

The second cipher line of f. 196 was segmented into glyph boxes (`seg.py`) and read box by box.
Against the crib's *arrivé tant travaillé qu'il y a gaigné* the tail aligns exactly, nine figures
for nine letters, with `13` = *qui* sitting between them:

| figure | n | Ɛ | ơ | ɣ | ƅ | b | ʒ | ·v· | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| letter | l | y | a | g | a | i | g | n | e |

Five of those — n = l, ·v· = n, t = e, Ɛ = y, ơ = a — are exactly the values derived from f. 143,
recovered here from a completely independent crib. The spelling is *trauaile* with one *l*, which
is what makes the count come out. On a second pass — the block flattened with `flatten.py` and tiled with a band tall enough that
mis-centring cannot clip it — the **whole line** resolved, thirty figures for thirty letters, and
the decoder returns the crib text from the figures alone:

> `D se eps f t ct a .v. m Ze L a BOX x b he G 13 n E a r a B g8 .v. t g .v. 7 f L g`
> → **r riue tant trauaile qu il y a gaigne un ouru…**

That fixes twenty-one figures of this hand with certainty and independently of f. 143:

| figure | Δ | ʃe | ε | ʃ | t | Ꝭ | ơ | ·v· | m | ℋe | £ | ⊐ | x | ƅ | ʰe | G | 13 | n | Ɛ | ʋ | ʙ | ʒ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| letter | r | r | i | u | e | t | a | n | t | t | r | u | a | i | l | e | qui | l | y | g | i | g |

Note ε and G are two different round figures, **i** and **e**, and ʃ and the ʃe ligature are two
more, **u** and **r**: exactly the confusable pairs that the eye cannot separate without a crib.
The working key for this hand is `key196.json`.

One thing is not yet accounted for: about five figures stand between the clear *auquel* and the
`12`, where the crib has nothing. Either the two copies diverge slightly at that point — the
duplicate need not be word-perfect — or those figures are being mis-segmented by eye. Settling it
needs the glyph segmenter (`seg.py`) rather than the eye, and it is the first thing to do when
this is picked up: the crib fixes the plaintext, so the segmented boxes of these two lines can be
*labelled* letter by letter, which is exactly the labelled atlas the confusable shapes need.

## Crib 2 — the ending  *(does not hold: see "The tail crib fails" below)*

f. 201 comes **out** of cipher for its last three lines, where f. 196 stays in cipher to the foot
of the page:

> … qu'après demain l'on commencera la batterie de quinze canons, dont il y a apparence qu'ils se
> rendront. Mayans, par accoustumé à bien assiéger les places …

That clause is therefore a second crib, this time at the end of f. 196's block.

## Why this matters

1. **Two cribs**, at the head and the foot of a 32-line block, on a leaf nobody has read.
2. **The pair is in depth.** The same plaintext enciphered twice, by a cipher with several
   homophones per letter, means that at every position the two figures must decode to the same
   letter. Aligning the two blocks turns a one-copy reading problem into a constraint-satisfaction
   problem with double the evidence — and, in practice, a transcription slip in one copy is
   corrected by the other. That is worth more here than any improvement to the language model,
   because transcription is what limits this cipher (see `NOTES.md`).
3. It fixes the date: the despatch reports the opening of a siege, the previous one being of the
   29th of the preceding month, with a battery of fifteen cannon to open "après demain".

## The two copies appear to share figure choices

Setting f. 201 up the same way (`flatten.py`, `fitlines.py`, `center2.py`; the fit is cleaner than
f. 196's, 35 lines at 122 px) and comparing the first cipher lines against f. 196 shows something
the depth argument did not predict: the **same code groups fall in the same relative places**.
f. 196's line 5 runs `… 14 he t 49 …` and f. 201's line 3 runs `… 14 he t ✚ 49 …` — the same
`14` (*que*), the same `he t`, the same `49`, in the same order.

If the two copies are figure-for-figure identical, they are not in depth in the classical sense
(different homophones for the same plaintext); they are two fair copies made from one enciphered
draft. That is *less* powerful cryptanalytically — but for this problem it is just as useful,
because the limit here is transcription, not cryptanalysis: two independent readings of the same
figure sequence catch each other's misreadings directly, figure by figure, with no alignment
needed. Confirming it needs both blocks transcribed.

Set-up state: `f196_lines.txt`, `f201_lines.txt` (fitted and offset-corrected),
`hi/f196flat.png`, `hi/f201flat.png`, `key196.json`, `cipher_f196.txt` (4 lines).


## f. 196's own figure set, started from the crib

f. 196 is not in f. 18's hand (f. 18's exemplars read it as noise), so it gets its own set,
`exemplars/manifest_f196.json`, seeded from this crib.

Line 4 of the cipher is fixed by f. 201's clear text. The code group **13** (*qui*) is plainly at box
17 — read by eye as a numeral — and it settles two things the counts could not:

* **where the line breaks.** Only one reading puts `<qui>` on box 17: *"**iue** tant travaille
  [qui]l y a gaigné"*. So line 3 ends at *…qu'il est arr-* and line 4 opens *-iue*, exactly as the
  crib's text runs. (The version beginning *rriue*, which scored slightly higher, puts the code a box
  late; a numeral on the leaf outranks a likelihood computed with another scribe's images.)
* **the spelling:** *travaille* with the ordinary double *l*, not the single *l* an earlier count
  had implied.

Boxes 1–16 pair one-to-one — *i u e t a n t t r a u a i l l e* — and are minted, with the 13
itself pinned. After the code the aligner stretches *lyagaigne* over thirteen boxes with four
splits, because the cipher line runs on past the point where the crib's clear text stops; that tail
is not minted.

**f. 196 set: 16 letter exemplars over 8 letters, plus code 13.** Next: the head of the crib on
line 3 (*il a depuis qu'il est arr-*) and its tail, where f. 201's last three lines are clear.


### Line 3: plain vertical strokes, possibly nulls

Segmented, line 3 runs clear to box 6 (*…commencement de ce siege Auquel*) and then shows thin cipher
figures interleaved with **plain vertical strokes** — boxes 7, 8, 9, 11 and 12 — before anything
resembling the code 12 (*il*) with which the crib says the cipher opens. This is the same stretch
flagged at the start of this file as "about five figures between the clear *auquel* and the 12 that
the crib does not account for".

Five unexplained figures and five plain strokes: the likeliest reading is that **the stroke is a
null or a word separator in this cipher**, which would also explain why earlier transcriptions of
other leaves kept producing "|" tokens that decoded to nothing. Not minted and not assumed — it is
testable, by checking whether dropping the strokes makes the line's box count match *il a depuis
qu'il est arr-* and the code groups land on numerals. That test is the next step on this line.


### The stroke test, run: inconclusive, and why

At gap 6 the strokes separate cleanly from the figures they touched: **five** plain vertical strokes
(boxes 18, 21, 24, 25, 30) in the cipher after *Auquel*. Two readings predict five:

* **nulls** — with *il* and *qui* written as codes 12 and 13, the crib needs 16 figures, and the 15
  non-stroke boxes nearly match;
* **the letters *i* and *l*** — *il a depuis qu'il est arr-* spelled out has exactly five of them, and
  the 20 boxes including the strokes match its 19 letters.

Forced alignment, per box: nulls **0.543**; strokes as spelled-out *i/l* **0.571**; strokes kept
with codes 0.422. (B) scores best — but it reads only **two** of the five strokes as *i* or *l* and
the other three as *e*, *s*, *e*, so its higher score is not coming from the strokes being thin
letters, which was the hypothesis. The margin is small and f. 196's own set is too thin (16
exemplars over 8 letters, with f. 18's hand as prior) to decide.

**Not decided, and not minted.** The discriminating test is a stroke in a stretch whose known text
has no *i* or *l* at that point — that would make it a null. It needs more of f. 196's text than the
head of the crib gives, which is what the tail crib is for.


## The foot of f. 201, read in full

Re-cropped to take in the whole foot of the page (the block crop had stopped just short of it). The
cipher runs to the end of a line that closes, in clear, on **"Croy"**, and the clear text continues
to the foot:

> …[je] croy **qu'après demain l'on commencera la batterye de quinze canons, dont il y [a]
> apparence qu'ils s'estonneront. N'ayans pas accoustumé à bien assiéger les places et faire aveq cest
> equipaige. Toutesfoys jusques icy ils sont toutte à Mons[ieu]r…**

(An earlier, lower-resolution reading gave *se rendront* for *s'estonneront*; at this size the
long-s and the *-tonn-* are plain, but the word is flagged for a second look.)

This is the tail crib for f. 196, and it is the richest stretch of known text anywhere in the item for
the letters still missing: *quinze* (q, z), *il y* (y), *aveq* and *equipaige* (q), *jusques icy*
(q, y). Four to five lines of f. 196's cipher should carry it.


## The tail crib fails — the two copies diverge at the end

This file claimed from the start that ff. 196 and 201 carry **two** cribs, at the head and at the
foot. The head crib is verified (the code 13 lands on a numeral and fixes the line break). **The foot
crib is not, and the evidence now says it is wrong.**

f. 196's foot is five lines of cipher, 141 figures at the calibrated gap, ending mid-page. f. 201's
clear tail — *"[je] croy qu'après demain l'on commencera la batterye de quinze canons … ils sont
toutte à Mons[ieu]r"* — is 188 tokens even with *que*, *qui* and *il* taken as codes. Two independent
measures say they are not the same text:

* **the count:** 141 figures for 188 tokens is 0.75 per token, where every verified alignment in this
  item has run at 0.95–1.0;
* **the codes:** f. 196's foot shows 13, 13, 12, **36**, **35**, 12. 35 is *parce que* — verified on
  f. 18 against f. 19's *"par ce que"* — and f. 201's clear tail contains no *parce que*; nor does it
  have any use for 36.

So the two copies share their opening and part company before the end: either f. 196 was cut short or
reworded, or f. 201's clear ending was written fresh rather than copied. **The foot of f. 201 is not a
key to the foot of f. 196**, and nothing has been minted from it. The "pair in depth" claim holds only
for the stretch both copies actually share — which is now the thing to measure, by aligning f. 201's
cipher block against f. 196's line for line from the verified head.
