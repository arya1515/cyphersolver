# fr. 15572 f. 173 — to the King: the credit has run out. Cipher read in stretches

Canvas 180 right; foliated 173 (an older 182 struck above). One clear line, then 33 lines of cipher
filling the page, in Mayenne–Forget Cipher-1, the same hand and key as ff. 143 and 154.
Glyph transcription in `f173_cipher.txt`, raw decoder output in `reading_f173.txt`.

**The clear opening:** *"Sire, Vostre Majesté a esté suffisamment advertie par les deux dernières
despesches de Monsieur du Mayne [cipher]…"* — so this is a despatch to Henri III, reporting on
Mayenne's own two last letters.

**The cipher, as it reads.** It is the credit side of the same crisis f. 154 describes:

> … **nécessité** … **se voyant frustré de la venue** … **qu'il y a plus de di[x] sepmaines** …
> **par les chemins, et n'ayant eu aucun moyen de** … **qu'il a esté contraint de** … **n'a[yant]
> autre … que d'envoyer … pour la seconde fois** … **de ceste ville** … **en cest affaire** …
> **ses obligations** … **la ville qui … n'ont faict que** … **en leurs escus** … **qu'ils disent
> avoir de** … **qu'ils ne seront** … **ne contribution pour ce** … **entièrement** … **les
> particuliers ne se sont pas trouvés** … **car il ne s'est peu trouver un seul** … **soubs les
> obligations de** … **aucun de ses [voisins]** … **et amis qui [sont] par deçà, qui ne se sont
> voulu aucunement obliger ny respondre** … **luy et sa** … **quelques marchands** … **qui avoient
> de luy** … **comme il a dict par** … **qui ont voulu prendre ceste confiance** … **ma foy** …
> **que de se contenter de** …

**What the despatch is.** The writer tells the King that the money cannot be raised: no private
person will lend, not one man could be found to go surety under the obligations, neither the
neighbours nor the friends "par deçà" would bind themselves or answer for him, the town will
contribute nothing, and only a few merchants — who already hold his paper — would take the risk on
his word. It is the same April–1586 pay crisis as f. 154, seen from the lender's end, and reported
upward to Henri III instead of sideways to Villeroy.

**Coverage, honestly.** Roughly half the words come out as clean French and the argument runs
end to end; the rest is single-figure noise. No proper name is recovered from this leaf: "Monsieur
du Mayne" is in the clear line, not in the cipher.

**A method note.** This leaf cost an hour to a bookkeeping trap: strips of three lines label rows
from a fitted line grid, and when the grid's phase drifts the label can sit one row off, which makes
a left half get joined to the wrong right half. The fix that works is to **tint alternate rows**
when cutting (`hi/f173g0.png` and the rest): left and right halves of one row then carry the same
background wash, so the join is certain by eye and no numbering has to be trusted.
