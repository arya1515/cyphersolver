# R9410 f.244r: splitting the merged look-alike signs n, d, #

Method: every line cropped in halves from the 3468x4624 scan, upscaled to 2400 px, autocontrast; then
close zooms (160x130 px windows at ~15x) and a template-matched gallery of all double-cross shapes
(template = the # of "guten", l.16). Line positions drift upward ~5 px/line on the left, ~3.5 px/line on the
right (page slant). Output: `r9410_v2.txt` (new codes K, U).

| code | shape | count v2 | value | evidence |

The earlier note "d = v in dwvyv5#48 vertragen" was a transcription slip: on the image l.3 begins K, then a
separate U-form # (v), then `wvyv5#48`. So v in vertragen is the U sign, and K is a separate word.
K's contexts (before vertragen, after "mit"/"gegen", "des K Frankreich") fit a nomenclator word such as
"(dem) Kunig" / "Frankreich's king" or a pronoun (uns/inen); not fixed here. A small δ with a double-cross
tick written above it (l.10 `4d48`, l.25 `jowd48`, l.29) is still d; the tick is a later marker, not a sign.
The lollipop b (δ with a ring on the stalk) is distinct from d and reads **h** (xpEbpq "sich hin-", t3Uwvb5dy48
"zu verhalten").

## n (π) - not split

All ~56 π were compared (line crops plus side-by-side zooms at l.8, 9, 15, 18): flat top bar overhanging to the
left, two legs, left leg curved. No shape difference between the n that read **b** (halben, bedanckt, liebe,
kombt, bruederlich, besorgen, geben, haben, lieben) and the n that read **w** (willen, antworten, wil, was, wie,
wol, wissen). n is one sign = **b/w** (Upper German b/w interchange); keep one code.
