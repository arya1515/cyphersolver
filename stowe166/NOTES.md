# Stowe MS 166: Edmondes to Burghley, 1592–1594 (DECODE R7770–R7775)

Status: in progress — read in part

Catalogue entry: "Lord Threr? to unknown recipient, 5 ciphertexts". DECODE's author field is the
**addressee**: the letters are to the Lord Treasurer (Burghley). Stowe MS 166 is volume 1 of the papers of
**Thomas Edmondes**, Elizabeth's agent with Henri IV (BL catalogue: "Papers of Thomas Edmondes, Agent at the
French Court, 1592-1596"). The letters open "It may please yor L." and report the King's words to the writer
first-hand ("as it pleaseth himself to tell me", "the K. thereuppon saide vnto me"), dated from the King's
camps (Blois, Tours, the camp before Laon). They are Edmondes's despatches, or copies of them. The DECODE "sender"
values (John le Rios, John Symmons, "Permell of Dieppo", "One of Dieppe") come from later dockets and endorsements.
They do not match the hand or the content. R7771 (ff. 56–57) is part of the same group.

## The cipher

A simple substitution of English, word divisions kept, with a few homophones and some variant letter forms.
There are also single code signs for persons. It was broken from the words ω4ʋ = the, α8 = of and ωα = to,
with "difficulties" and "empeachement" as the first long words. Same key in all six records, 1592 to 1594,
and two different hands.

| δ | a | б / 6 | g (also a) | + | b |
| 2 | c | ш | d, u/v | ʋ | e |
| 8 | f | 4 | h | ǂ | i |

Code signs: **9** (standing alone) = a person, most likely the Count of Soissons in 1592 (R7770, R7772);
**n** = the new suitor for Catherine de Bourbon (R7770), unidentified; **£ / £2** = most likely the Duke of Guise in 1594
(Champagne and Reims in R7774, Provence against Épernon in R7775); **4̇** unexplained.

## Readings

**R7770, f. 50** (after "the K. continueth his resolution to goe to Saumur to see his sister, not so much for
that cause (as it pleaseth himself to tell me) as especiallye"):
> [4̇] to breake of the marriage betwene her and [9] wch he tould me he is resolued not to permitt but to geive
> her vnto [n] of whom he said he hath great hope that he will render himself of the religion. [9] is herewth
> enraged and extreemelie discontented.

**R7771, f. 57** (after "…by the Pope, will appeare vnto yor L. by the enclosed"):
> [9]: is glad to here of, hoping to be no further troubled wth the clamor of the Catholikes for the seekinge of him.

**R7772, f. 58v** (after "…had gonne forwarder, whereupon this preuention"):
> [9]: I doe secretelie learne that Monsr Montmorancy hath made great instance, as a condition whereof he
> required the performance before he would come, to haue the yong prince of Condé deliuered into the handes of
> Madame d'Angolesme his sister in law to be vnder his custodie.

**R7772, f. 59**, 4 lines: **not read.** Only fragments: "…papistes…", "…to the place…of the constable (?)…
of the …", ending "…the crowne, but wherein he hath nothing effected, the K. being resolued not to assent thereunto" (the last part in clear).

**R7773, f. 60v** (after "but the K. thereuppon saide vnto me"):
> that his faith is not vendible.

**R7774, f. 109v**:
> choise expreslie to negotiate therein, for the difficulties of empeachement wch he knoweth he will geive to
> the same in respect of his owne interest against [£] pretending for the gouernment of Champaigne, [?] hauing
> no desire it should nowe take place, the rather for that he hath renewed his intelligences in Amiens and
> Rheins by the meanes of the duke; the said townes…

f. 110 has no cipher.

**R7775, f. 112**:
> [£2]: execute an enterprise vppon Rheins wch would be the ca[…]inge of the place and him.
Then in clear: "The King hath a disposition to geue him the Gouernment of Prouence to embarque him against the D. Espernon…".
