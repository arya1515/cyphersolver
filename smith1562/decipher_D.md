# Smith ciphers in BL Add MS 4136: decipherment D (letters of Sept 1564 to Feb 1565)

Readings come from the glyphs through KEY.md and the code words confirmed in decipher_A.md. CSP Foreign vol. 7 was
used afterwards to confirm them (csp/v7_pp195-215.html, v7_pp234-252.html, v7_pp277-290.html, v7_pp290-306.html).
Glyphs are normalised as in decipher_A. `[?]` marks an uncertain letter or word and `⟨..⟩` an unread code sign.
Clear-English nulls (Andwerpe, garnesey, don, and, yf, sandwich) are left out of the plaintext.

New points about the system, taken from these letters:
- **Enos = Ambassador.** In the 20 Nov letter it is a code word ("T Enos .o ⩓" = the Ambassador of Scotland), and a
  marginal gloss there spells it out (`Enos = ⊓⊢̈‡⊔ξ⊔2:66·`). It is not a null here. The key's top line agrees
  ("enos Ambassador").
- **m̄ / M̈ = marriage / marry.** Read from context in 1 Sep (25), Narbonne 7 Jan and 22 Nov (2). This is not in the key
  as read.
- **The flag sign (⊐ with a stroke)** is used twice, and both times the context is Archduke Charles: 22 Nov (1) and
  Toulouse (6). It is probably the key's "the Emperor" sign, used for the Emperor's house.
- **B: = bishop** and **:L = Lord**, as in the key. A kite-shaped ξ with a hook is w (were, which, was, lies…), while
  the plain ξ is d (dare, died).
- **ω10· / ⌐ω** after "the late": this could be the Cardinal sign in a variant form ⟨?⟩.

## Summary
- **1 Sep 1564 (CSP 642):** 28 of 31 passages read. They give the whole cipher of §3: the Scots–French practice, the
  Lord of Murray, Huntly, the bishops and the Pope's counsel, Lethington, the Bishop of Glasgow and the pension, Lord
  Robert, the old Pope, and the King of Spain and the Queen of Scots.
- **3 Sep (CSP 650):** 2 of 2 read (Nanion Cocborne, Villeros).
- **Marseilles 13 Nov (CSP 793):** read in part (Scotland, great trouble, by the Cardinal, "the matter is hote", the
  marriage matter, "by whom I dare speak").
- **Arles 20 Nov (CSP 806):** the Adam Hume passage is read in large part, as far as "glad to be rid of him". The last
  lines are tentative and not in CSP.
- **Arles 22 Nov (CSP 808):** 2 short passages, read only in part (the Archduke Charles sign; marriage with the
  Queen's Majesty [?]).
- **Arles 24 Nov (CSP 815):** the whole 12-line block is read except a few code signs.
- **Narbonne 7 Jan 1565 (CSP 916):** read in large part.
- **Toulouse 9 Feb 1565 (CSP 979):** 10 of 10 read, and 1 only in part.
- Every reading was made from the glyphs and then checked against CSP. No passage contradicts the calendar, and no
  crib was forced. The unread items are code signs that are not in the key as read: ⟨J̣⟩, ⟨ẓ⟩, ⟨ï⟩, ⟨C·⟩, ⟨F̄⟩, the
  (2) sign of 1 Sep, and Marseilles (3) and (10).
- Key additions: Enos = Ambassador (a code word, not a null); m̄/M̈ = marriage/marry; :F = French; the flag sign is
  probably the Emperor's house / Archduke Charles; the kite ξ = w and the plain ξ = d.
