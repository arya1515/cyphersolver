Alias conventions as in AGENT_BRIEF. On this page the ring with a dot in it (◉) is used for V/U all the
way through and is written `e` (the key's "filled eye"). The bold "B" (b = Q) and the open "ß" (B = B) are
both frequent and easy to tell apart. `#` is written as `H` (A) or `#` (M), depending on which gives French.
The zigzag T (`t`) hangs well below its own line, so it often shows up in the crop of the next line. It
belongs to the line whose baseline it starts from.

The page starts mid-sentence, continuing from f. 46r, and ends mid-sentence ("qu'il"), continuing on f. 47r.

## Notes on doubtful points

* L03: the first sign is a faint, thin double stroke, unlike the heavy LL bar (compare L21, where the heavy
  bar gives "merveilleuse"). Taken as a filler or null (`|`). Read as LL it would give nothing.
* L04: "doumage" is spelled with o-u-m. "il m'en complaist" (con-sign + p-l-a-i-s-t) is a clean read, but its sense
  is uncertain ("he goes along with me in it"?).
* L11: `C r X Y 8 L` = "croine" is read as "croy ne [faict]" ("as I believe he does not"). The words are uncertain;
  the letters are clear.
* L14: "dien" is missing the final t.
* L15: after "il" there is a 4-with-flag null (`f`) before "le".
* L20: the sign after "dict" is **not in the key**. It looks like a looped ascender, like a script "ƒ" or "ℓ"
  with a closed loop at the top, standing on a long horizontal bar. It stands for a name or title ("le tout
  luy doibt venir du costé dudict ___"). From context it is probably l'EMPEREUR or another power the
  astrologers named. It is not the heart (person) sign.
* L25: the first letter of "fantasie" is a tall, thin, long-s-like stroke with a small foot hook. It looks
  more like `J` than like the looped ρ used for F elsewhere (e.g. "fantasie" in L21, "feist" L23). Either a
  cramped F or a fourth F form.
* L27: "le songea en heure qu'il a songé d'aultres fois plusieurs choses que luy sont advenues". "Songea en
  heure" is read as "dreamt it at the [same kind of] hour at which he has dreamt other things that came true".
* L09: K in "avoir" has a small superscript flourish. L17: "doibt" has a small hook after it. Neither
  affects the reading.

## English summary

* Gramont says the Pope grants whatever he asks on the King's behalf, whether or not there is any profit in
  it and even when it does him harm. The Pope is acting in good faith; otherwise he would be the greatest
  fool alive, giving at every turn with no hope of return.
* Clement VII is deeply superstitious. Pope or not, and though he should not believe the soothsayers, he
  greatly fears any bad prediction about himself.
* Several diviners have foretold that he will be deposed, or will die within the next year, and that it will
  all come from the side of a party written with an unknown code sign (probably the Emperor).
* This has him badly unsettled. The night before he left Rome (for Bologna, October 1529) he had a dream,
  which Gramont is sending to Montmorency. It frightens him all the more because he dreamt it at the same kind
  of hour at which earlier dreams of his came true.
* He told the dream only reluctantly, and to one man only. Gramont goes on to ask that it be kept secret
  (continued on f. 47r).
