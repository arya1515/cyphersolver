## Notes on glyphs and doubts
* **`#` is used for both A and M.** At this resolution I could not reliably tell the "loose ǂǂ" (A) from the "compact #" (M), so context decided each case. A: pourch**a**ssoit, l'**a**ccordant, succed**a**st, br**a**s, dif**a**nce, **a**voir, t**a**nt, sc**a**vez, **a**urez, gr**a**nde, **a** qui, **a**ffaire, **a**ucun, It**a**llie, **a**dvenir, **a**lliez, f**a**icte, l'**a**y. M: a**m**itie, te**m**ps, **m**ectre, **m**ais, de**m**eure, **m**inistres, co**m**me, **m**onsieur, ad**m**iral, **m**is. On this page A is the more frequent value.
* **4 (I) is sometimes written as a plain cross "+"**, with at most a faint diagonal. I read these as I: "**i**l est" (L12), "fo**i**s" and "qu'**i**l" (L14), "prof**i**t" (L15). I aliased them as `4`. Every other "+" is D.
* **The "4-with-flag" null** looks here like a cross with a triangular or ring head on a foot (almost "♀" on a base). It is easy to mistake for ♀ (O), but it is a null in every case: L4 "| f s'arrester", L5 "dif ~ f | ferer", L6 "suc f cedast", L21 "af ~ f faire", L22 "f vous", L24 "par | f lles".
* **"-o-" null**: nearly always filled, "-●-" (L4, L5, L7, L10, L12, L17, L21, L22, L25, L31, L34).
* **CON** (`c`) also stands for *com*: "conme il" (L19), "conplexion" (L20), "conbien" (L34).
* **L5**: in parti**c**ularitez the glyph after 4 looks like a blot "●" (normally V). It is probably a blotted ∞. I aliased it as `C`.
* **L18**: in pro**c**ede the C is a small ∞ that could be taken for the ring "o".
* **Redundant E**: L2 "consil**e e** estoit" (W R) and L6 "de(e)lai" (`+ L l`) each have one E too many. It is probably a slip or a word-end marker.
* **L4** "en voi(?)": `R 8 k g Y` sits between separators, and the zigzag after it is the T of "toutesfois". "en voi[e]" or "en voi[t]" are both possible. This phrase is the least certain on the page.
* **L13**: after "entier" is a small hooked glyph "ɥ". I took it as a variant caret E ("entiere"), but it is uncertain.
* **L22**: "vous" is written `n g k R s` (v-o-u-e-s), with one letter too many. The standard form is `k X n s` (L22 first occurrence, L24).
* **L27**: after "le dict" is a **large sign** like a big "f" or "ρ" with a crossbar (aliased `!`). It is not in the key. It must be a code sign for a person or power. The sense ("he strives only to get him out of Italy") strongly suggests **the Emperor**.
* **Hanging zigzags**: several tall zigzags (T) hang down into the next line. They were given to the line they belong to (for example the end of L2 and L26, and L9 into L10).
* The heart `<P>` (L8, L33) is clearly the **King** (Francis I): "la bonne volunte du <P> & de l'amitie qu'il lui portoit", and "en faveur du <P> pour les alliez & confederez".

## English summary
* Continuing his report of an audience, Gramont says the Pope admitted he knew perfectly well that the push for "the said council" (the general council the Emperor was urging) was meant to ruin him.
* The Pope meant to grant it in principle but then stall on points of detail, so that the delay would end in a breakdown.
* Since Gramont had assured him of the King's goodwill and friendship, the Pope wishes to throw himself entirely into the King's arms and trust him alone.
* Because the times do not allow any open show of distrust, the Pope agrees that whenever it is needed and to the King's advantage, the King and his ministers may say, and have it said, that he has little reason to trust the Pope. The King should go boldly ahead as he sees fit, provided the goodwill underneath stays whole. The Pope knows the temper of the people they deal with calls for such things.
* Gramont repeats his old advice: with good words the King will get whatever he wants from the Pope. He adds that there is no need to fear the Pope will build on this or profit from it with "the said [sign]", presumably the Emperor. The Pope's only aim is to get him out of Italy, whatever it costs him.
* The Admiral (Chabot de Brion) will have little difficulty getting the Pope to agree to whatever can be done for the King's allies and confederates, given how Gramont has disposed him.
* The sentence runs on to f. 46r ("& combien que pour ...").
