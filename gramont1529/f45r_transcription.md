## Continuous reading (lightly modernised punctuation, original spelling kept)

Monseigneur, je voy que tant par mes lettres du treziesme et du septiesme de septembre [que par la] depesche de la
bulle des quatre decimes et aultres choses que vous aporte le maistre d'hostel du roi de Navarre, aurez prins
seureté que, si j'ai par ci devant escript au [Roy] et à vous la bonne volunté de nostre sainct pere, je l'ai plus
faict avecques claire congnoissance d'icelle par effectz que par legiere creance ni aultre volunté extraordinaire
qui me meust à ce. Et vous puis dire que, quant plus je vois avant, tant plus je conferme mon oppinion, d'aultant
que je cognois ordinairement et à clair que la plus grande envie que nostre [d]ict sainct pere ait est de demourer en
vraie amitié et union avecques le [Roy], et que quelque bonne parolle ni demonstration qu'il puisse avoir de
[l'Empereur (?)], cela lui croist une diffidance qu'il a conceue, tellement qu'il ne peult se persuader que d'aultre
chose que mal et inconvenient lui puissent advenir de ce costé-là, bien qu'il montre que le tout soit au contraire.
Et pour le faire fier, n'i a nui (?) d'aucune chose [comme] ce que le [Roy] me commanda lui dire du concile general,
lequel il craignoit sur toutes les choses de ce monde, combien qu'il monstrast et monstre à ung chascun qu'il en
ait grande envie et qu'il le pourchasse. Et au commancement, et avant qu'il me prestast entiere foi, il m'en
voulsist bien faire acroire aultant. Le tout debatu et mes raisons ouïes, il fut contrainct se declarer [... f. 45v]

(The words "[que par la]" before *depesche* are not in the cipher; the clear "tant par mes lettres" wants a
"que par" and the cipher opens directly with "depesche", so either it was elided or "tant ... que" is left
hanging. The two heart signs are rendered [Roy]; the heart is only a "person" sign in the key, but "escript au
[heart] et a vous" and "le [heart] me commanda" make the King the natural reading.)

## English summary

Gramont tells Montmorency that his letters of 13 and 7 September, and the dispatch of the bull for the four
tenths (décimes on the French clergy) and other business carried by the King of Navarre's maître d'hôtel, should
have assured him of the Pope's good will, which Gramont says he reported from observed deeds, not credulity. The
further he goes, the more certain he is that Clement VII's chief wish is to stay in true friendship and union with
the King. Whatever fine words or gestures come from the other side (a code sign, almost certainly the Emperor),
they only deepen the Pope's distrust: he cannot believe anything but harm can come to him from that quarter,
though he pretends otherwise. Nothing won the Pope's trust more than what the King ordered Gramont to tell him about
a general council, which Clement feared above everything, even though he professed publicly that he wanted one
and was pressing for it. At first, before he trusted Gramont fully, the Pope tried to make him believe that too;
but after discussion and hearing Gramont's reasons he was forced to declare himself (continued on f. 45v).

## Glyph and key notes from this page

* **Null `f`** (triangle or ring on a stem with a foot bar): clearly distinct from ♀ = O (small ring over a cross). It
  is a null wherever it occurs (L01 twice, L04, L15, L22, L23, L29, L32; e.g. `+ f W` = "de", `f l R` = "le").
  Read as ♀/O it gives nonsense ("doe pesche"). It must be Lasry's "4 with a flag" null.
* **`#` (M) and `ǂǂ` (A)** cannot be told apart reliably in this hand. About 20 instances were settled by context;
  A is at least as common as M (navarre, vrai, grande, creance, faire, au, a). Treat every `#` as M/A.
* **R (E) and K (V)** look alike in three places: demourer (L15), d'aultre (L20), and the reverse in gener- (L25).
* **B (ß)** takes several shapes: an 8 open at top left, a plain "S" (bien, L22), a "9" with a tail (combien, L27).
* **An "ʌn" variant of n = V** in "ung" (L28).
* **`◉` / filled eye = V**, as in the key (tout L23, vous L05). The **lozenge = S** can look filled when blotted (L01
  "pesche", L02).
* **Unknown sign `?`** (a cross with a loop on its right arm, Lasry's second "unknown") appears once (L18) in
  "qu'il puisse avoir de [?]". The context wants a person, most likely the Emperor.
* **L14:** "nostre [4 i C t] sainct pere". The first glyph looks like a plain 4, but "dict" is expected. This is
  probably a slip by the cipher clerk.
* Double-letter signs confirmed: `=` LL (bulle, parolle, icelle, tellement, conseille), `N` NN (bonne), `S` SS
  (congnoissance, puissent, puisse, pourchasse), `P` PP (oppinion), `U` RR (Navarre). `c` = con/com
  (congnoissance, inconvenient, commanda, combien, commancement). `z` = X (extraordinaire), `y` = Z (aurez).
* Many small "T" marks sitting between lines are the T-bar tops of `J` glyphs in the line below, not separate signs.
