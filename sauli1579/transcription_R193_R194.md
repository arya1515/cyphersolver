## R193 p.1 (fol. 236r)

Clear text summary: "Ill.mo et R.mo Sig.re". Don Juan de Idiáquez in the name of H.M. (Philip II) told the writer
that he had made the request to H.M. in the name of His Holiness (per l'impresa d'Inghilterra); H.M. wished to
satisfy His Holiness but lacked means for this enterprise, needing great preparations and men and the aid (gratie)
offered in the name of H.S.; the nuncio says forces were weak for such an enterprise, deferred until the King had
made himself master of Flanders, easier and safer; talk of the galleys and the subsidy of the galleys, "fanteria di
Italiani", hopes for the "felici progressi di S. M.tà". Endorsement bottom left: "Al Card.le di Como" (Tolomeo
Gallio, secretary of state). No date or signature on this page (letter continues).

## R193 p.3 (fol. 238r) - a separate letter, clear copy / decipherment

No digits on this page. A clean-hand letter "Ill.mo et R.mo S.or mio Oss.mo": after sending the dispatch for Mons.
Marco at Madrid, received the enclosed paper from the Consigliere Vasches (Vázquez); the negotiation (jurisdiction
to be left to His Holiness, without intervention of H.S.'s ministers; the "temperamenti" offered by Mr Montio(?)),

"Presidente è pietra scandali et mal sodisfatto della Corte romana, et chi lo diffende et copre lo fa per amicitia et
forse per bene, ma a me pare conveniente avisar quel ch'io scopro et sento, riammi(?) poi al vero". Then: having
dismounted at Elvas, found the letter of the Archbishop of Évora(?) ... "Di Elvas alli 1?0 [10?] di Xbre 1580".
Signature (second third): "Humil.mo et dev.mo S.r A. Card.le Riario". No cipher passages.

## R194 p.1 (fol. 281 / 296 stamp)

Clear text summary: no heading on this page. On the occasion of receiving the arms (?) and letters sent to H.M.
about "li capitani d'Irlanda", as Mons. Nuntio had written, the writer invited H.M. to give a good and sure
intimation of his will on "tal impresa" (which H.S. desires for the service of God and the Christian religion and
the honour and greatness of H.M.); the King answered that he had very good will and inclination, but it needed
great provision of money, and that H.S. not only did not help with new graces but denied the usual ones; that he
was attending to Flanders and then to this enterprise; the writer replied that H.M. could well with his great forces
attend to both; to the Flanders matter, "da quelli d'Inghilterra"; on the truce ("tregua") nothing yet; the danger to
the whole weight "della Christianità". No date or signature (continues on p.2).

## Summary

- 0 unglossed cipher passages in R193 (pp.1-2) and R194 (pp.1-2); R193 p.3 is a clear/deciphered letter with no digits.
- Probable code words not in the key: **16 = Inghilterra** (twice), **72 = denari?** (once), a stretch for
  "religione cattolica" (R194 p.2), and a recurring 4-digit prefix XX71/XX61 (6371, 7771, 7361, 7761) + 9 placed before
  "per ..." / "di la" phrases, unexplained (code for "forze"? nulls?).
- The number 1300 is written in plain numerals inside a cipher passage (R193 p.2 #4).
