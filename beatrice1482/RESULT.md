# Beatrice d'Aragona: four prior decipherments and one misclassified letter

Research result, 20 September 2026. This is a source identification and catalogue correction, **not a new cryptanalytic solution**. The user-specified set comprises four cipher letters whose readings were published in 1877–78, and a plain Italian letter dated 1505, not 1504. No fresh cipher key or exhaustive symbol-by-symbol validation is claimed.

## What the letters say

### R1140: a proposed intervention against Venice

The published reading describes Matthias's proposed assistance to the Italian league. For 100,000 ducats sent to Milan, he would arrange 16,000 combatants in the Brescia and Verona districts: 10,000 Swiss and 6,000 cavalry. Another 500 cavalry, including 100 hussars, are ready, but a route must be arranged. He does not wish to declare himself openly against Venice before his relationship with the league is settled.

Examples of the 1877 decipherment (spelling retained, line breaks removed):

> uno expediente optimo et necessario per lo favore et salute de tuta la lega

> Dece milia Svizari et sei milia cavalli del Signor Re di Hungaria.

The manuscript's clear opening begins “Piu diffusamente da parte dela Mta del Re mio consorte scrivo…”; its alternating plain/cipher blocks line up with the edition's alternating roman/italic text. The ending dates it “posonij ultimo … 1482.” The editor explicitly says that the italic words were written in secret signs. This is positive evidence of an earlier decipherment, not merely a bibliographic match by date.

### R1141: the league must supply money and act decisively

Beatrice reports Matthias's displeasure at the news from Italy and his criticism of the league's unwillingness to spend. She repeats the Swiss and cavalry proposal, asks for money to be sent to Milan, and calls for effective cooperation rather than endlessly restarting negotiations.

> ali avari che per sparagnare uno grosso perdeno lo ducato

The final cipher phrase is printed as:

> essere uno Jocare de balla cum retornare sempre ali principij

This is the metaphor of playing ball and always returning to the starting point. The PDF image reads “balla”; the machine-extracted “holla” is an OCR error. The manuscript and edition share the opening reference to Eleonora's letter of the 14th and the closing date 8 June 1482. The printed note again identifies italicized text as cipher. The 2008 secondary article's “8 luglio” is not the primary edition's date.

### R1142: the secret negotiation and a replacement cipher

Only the first written page contains graphic cipher. The next three written pages are plain text concerning Ippolito's appointment to Esztergom, opposition from Cardinal Ascanio, and the efforts of Bartolomeo. Berzeviczy prints this part but explicitly omits passages already printed in MDE III p. 67 and IV p. 367.

MDE IV supplement no. 45 matches the first page's plain framework around the encrypted runs: “Ce sono piaciuti anche li altri advisi…”, “per potere pigliare in questo tale ordine…”, and “li sera observato ad unguem.” It supplies a reading about bringing Andriolo to Hungary, paying his travel expenses, and honoring the king's promises. Its Hungarian heading identifies the negotiation as the Djem/Cem affair; that identification is the editor's, not a newly recovered explicit name in the cipher.

The printed text contains **“Virtus”, “Fortis”, and repeated “M.”** embedded awkwardly in the Italian. These are visible in the PDF, not OCR inventions. Their cryptographic role remains unverified. Do not drop them as supposed nulls or claim the 1878 text is a flawless modern edition.

The fifth image continues a sentence from the fourth and matches MDE III no. 54:

> Con la cyfra havemo antiquamente con Vostra Signoria … ne li mandamo qui alligata un altra nova

She explains that the current letter uses the old cipher and encloses a new one for future correspondence with herself and Matthias. Thus the edition split one physical letter into separately printed pieces; its no. 54 is not evidence for an extra ciphertext in this five-record set. MDE III no. 53 is a related same-date letter and should not be substituted for this manuscript's text.

### R1143: Ippolito, Ascanio and Andriolo

The opening encrypted passage discusses the support for Ippolito in Milan and Bari, against the account offered by Cardinal Ascanio's envoy, the abbot of Forlì. The letter also requests that Andriolo be sent promptly for the king's business.

The physical evidence is unusually good: image P1 shows a separate handwritten decipherment laid over the first cipher block; P2 shows the same page with the slip moved aside. P4 and P5 similarly show the final written page with the short decipherment slips covering and uncovering the cipher. These are **alternative photographs of the same leaves**, not five different written pages. There are three written pages plus the address side, in six photographs.

One short slip reads:

> che quello Andriolo se conferisca qua

The corresponding 1877 text prints the request in the same context. The long slip begins “che don hippolyto vostro figliolo…” and matches the subject and wording of the edition. The slips' precise date and scribe have not been established; “manuscript decipherment” is safer than assigning them a date without palaeographical work.

### R1154: “because we have no cipher”

The plain Italian letter asks Ippolito to forward correspondence to the cardinal of Esztergom using a trusted messenger. The messenger should appear to be traveling on Ippolito's own business. Beatrice thanks him for the company of his household official Francesco, and asks for continuing assistance and advice.

The decisive passage is:

> Et per non esser certa che le littere pervengano secure, et per non havere cifra: non ce extendemo altramente per questa

“Because we are not certain the letters will arrive safely, and because we have no cipher, we do not go into further detail in this letter.”

The date ends **“M° quingentesimo quinto”**. The photograph's date blocks and docket also say 1505. The plain text and its typography are normal Italian chancery writing and abbreviations; they are not substituted cipher symbols. The second image is the address side. The DECODE classification and 1504 date are therefore contradicted by the object itself, independently supported by Berzeviczy no. CCCII.

## Scope and limits

- All 18 images were acquired with the existing authorized cookie. Body pages and the address-side contact sheet were inspected. The script records the image names; local image filenames preserve DECODE image IDs.
- Text identification used dates, salutations, clear wording, transitions into and out of cipher, and attached decipherments. No exhaustive glyph transcript, recovered key, measured decipherment percentage, or ciphertext-only solve was produced.
- The 1482 and 1486 alphabets look different; the old/new-cipher statement makes pooling all four letters under one key especially unjustified.
- R1142's printed “Virtus/Fortis/M.” markers remain an editorial and cryptographic question. They do not erase the existence of the older reading.
- Tomokiyo's Cryptiana index was retrieved and checked; no dedicated Beatrice entry was found there. This is not an exhaustive negative claim about all his publications.
- DECODE's archive-permission warning was honored: manuscript images stay in the ignored local `img/` directory and are not reproduced on the site.

The correct research outcome is **prior readings located, catalogue premise corrected**. These five records should not be represented as five newly solved or five wholly unread ciphertexts.
