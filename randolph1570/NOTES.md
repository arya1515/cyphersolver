# Randolph to Sussex, Edinburgh, 5 July 1570 (catalogue: "Randolph to Sussex 1569", DECODE R4931)

Status: read (read at the time; the contemporary decipherment is f. 278, DECODE R4932; every cipher run read here)

## The document

- BL, Cotton MS Caligula C II, f. 277r-v. DECODE R4931 (2 images). Its two images are byte-identical to images P3
  and P5 of DECODE R4930, the "key" record, which is really Caligula C II ff. 76-77 (Burghley's notes on the
  Norfolk/Ross letters) and holds no key for this cipher.
- The letter is dated "At Edenborowe the 5te of Julye 1570", signed "Tho. Randolphe". DECODE's 1569 is the
  volume span.
- Mostly clear English, five cipher runs: f.277r ll.3-10 (~69 words), l.16-18 (Morton), l.27 (the money), l.29
  (the soldiers' pay), f.277v l.14 (Grange and the Scottish Queen).

## The decipherment is f. 278 (DECODE R4932, status "Decrypted")

f. 278 is a half-leaf docketed "5 July 1570": a clerk re-copied every cipher word, divided by strokes, and wrote the
plaintext over each; the short runs are deciphered at the foot in clear. The first pass here missed it, because the
catalogue pointed at R4930 as "the key record". The lesson again: open every neighbouring record with the same shelfmark.

## Prior art (contamination check)

- Boyd, *CSP Scotland* iii (1903) no. 339: an abstract of Sussex's clear copy (enclosure to Sussex-Cecil, 7 July
  1570; SP 52), noting "Cott. Calig.: Original of the same; partly in cipher, deciphered". Archive.org
  `calendarstatepa02boydgoog`, djvu lines 17866-17935 (`print/boyd3.txt`).
- Not in Tomokiyo's Elizabethan pages (his Randolph keys are the 1559-60 Sadler-Croft ones).
- So: read at the time, abstracted in print in 1903. Before f. 278 was found, a crib attack on Boyd's abstract with
  an en-1640s annealer had independently read about 70% of the words (below). The decipherment confirms every
  value that attack recovered.

## The cipher

Word-divided homophonic substitution; signs are Latin letters, figures and invented marks, several per vowel.
Code signs: ⊟ = "the S[cottish] Q[ueen's] partie in Scotland", θθ = "the S. Queene", ΠΠ = "the Q. of England",
ⅲ (struck +++) = the Earl of Morton. Values confirmed by the f. 278 glosses include
`=` c · c u/v · D r · q s · ɛ n · δ t · ϙ o · Ӿ o · 4 d · # i · 8 e · ʒ(tailed) a · 3 f · Π g · ⍏ n · U h · Ω t ·
ϖ m · λ m · Ӿℓ w · ≡ i · Γ h. `transcription.txt` is the token transcription; the glyph classes x/ꭓ/Ӿ are merged
there, which is why the ciphertext-only anneal stalled.

## Reading (clear roman, deciphered *italics*, from f. 278 checked against the f. 277 cipher)

f.277r: "I have receaved your L. lre of the seconde of this present, and what maye answer some parte thereof your L.
hathe I trust seen by myne wrytten from hens the same daye, wch in effecte as this. *The S. Q. partie in Scotland
wyl reke ther owne [?] the S. queene handes [?] fortefy, have litle confidens [in] the Q. of England, that so ofte
changeth her course; and now of late of all, whereas they were determined to have made a regent, dyd staye it*
full sore agaynst their willes, *upon hope of better comfort, and shall be forced to yeld [to] ennemies, and
[be] conforted [with] murderers and traytors, bothe of kyng and regent, and to obbey her whome nether by lawe,*
duetie or conscien they thinke them selves bounde unto. This your L. shall reyde not spoken of my selfe, but this
is a great parte of their greif..." [the words in brackets and "reke", "handes", "fortefy" are the clerk's glosses as
written; the sense of the opening phrase is uncertain in the original decipherment too]

f.277r l.16-18: "...to be sorte wth your L. *it thinke* utterlye *undon*, what so ever *be come of us*. I have
*spoken with therle of Morton*, who is of that mynde that before I have wrytten..."

f.277r l.27: "...*the mony*, whiche I knowe will marveylouslye discomforte him..."

f.277r l.29: "...that the *soldiars may be continewed in pay* untill the Convention be ended..."

f.277v l.14: "I knowe he loketh not *[for] the S. Q. home cumming*."

## Gaps

None in the cipher: every run is deciphered on f. 278. Three words of the clerk's first-line gloss ("reke",
"handes", "fortefy") are faint or doubtful; the cipher words under them are not otherwise confirmed.
