# Clear copies / printed texts of Hémard de Denonville (Mâcon) ciphers, 1536-37 — findings

## 1. Ribier, Lettres et mémoires d'Estat, t. 1 (1666) — NEGATIVE for our dates
- Full text: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_bOnmNv2ZLVoC (djvu txt saved locally as ribier1.txt, OCR)
- Vol. 1 Rome material starts Dec 1537 (letters of "C. Cardinal de Mascon, C. de Selve" to the King, e.g. "De Rome, ce dixiéme de Février 1537" = 1538 n.s., OCR line ~8717). Nothing for Sept 1536 - Apr 1537.
- Useful only as later same-embassy context (Carpi, Ambroise [Recalcati], Trivulzio).

## 2. Tomokiyo, cryptiana francis.htm — section "BnF fr.3053 (1535-1537)"
- http://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/francis.htm
- Says fr.3053 key reconstructed by Tomokiyo; same cipher in Mâcon to Montmorency, Rome 11 Apr 1537, BnF fr.3071 f.9 (no.4), broken by Lasry 2023. f.85 fragment in different cipher, deciphered f.86.
- **Key lead**: "Another cipher of Cardinal of Macon ... letter from the cardinal to the king dated Rome, 15 February 1536 in BnF Dupuy 44 (f.30-38; f.31 is a decipherment of f.31bis; f.33 decipherment of ... f.32; f.35 of f.36; f.37 of f.38)". 15 Feb 1536 may be old style = **15 Feb 1537 n.s.**, the same date as our f.21 (to Montmorency). If so, the King's letter of the same day survives WITH contemporary decipherments -> content overlap / crib for f.21 and f.16.
- Langeac 19 Nov 1535 used Mâcon's cipher (Correspondance du Cardinal Jean du Bellay vol. 2 p.156).

## 3. BnF Dupuy 265 (archivesetmanuscrits ark:/12148/cc88431m)
- Letters to/from ambassadors of Francis I; includes "à Charles [Hémard], cardinal-évêque de Mâcon, ambassadeur à Rome, Rome, du 21 fév. 1534 au 22 oct. 1537 env., trente cinq lettres (139-218)"; Montmorency letters (235 ff.); letters to Nicolas Raince incl. 21 janv. and 3 oct. 1537 (334-335); Hémard to the King copies (111-122); Rocque/Fregoso cipher + déchiffrement (152/155). Digitised on Gallica. Letters 1536-37 addressed to/around Hémard may carry replies to our despatches (Montmorency/King answers).

## 4. Lemaitre (ed.), Correspondance des ambassadeurs de François Ier à Rome (1537-1538), SHF 2024
- https://www.inshs.cnrs.fr/fr/correspondance-des-ambassadeurs-de-francois-ier-rome-1537-1538
- Edits 232+ letters from MAE Correspondance politique Rome 3 and Rome 4 (17th-c. copies), May 1537 - June 1538 (Selve era). Starts after our letters (latest target 6 Apr 1537), but **MAE CP Rome 3 is a 17th-c. copy register** that may begin earlier (1536-37 Mâcon despatches in clear). Worth checking its introduction/inventory.

## 5. BnF Dupuy 44 f.30-38 — STRONGEST LEAD (ark cc88242q; Gallica btv1b10033899d, Tomokiyo links /f56)
- Catalogue: "Au roi [François Ier] ... par le cardinal de Mâcon [Charles Hémard de Denonville], Rome, 15 févr. 1536, orig. (30, chiffré)". Hémard was created cardinal 22 Dec 1536, so "cardinal de Mâcon, 15 févr. 1536" must be old style = **15 Feb 1537 n.s.** -> companion to the King of our f.21 (Rome 15 Feb 1537 to Montmorency), and close to f.16 (Feb 1537).
- Tomokiyo: contemporary decipherments bound with it: f.31 deciphers f.31bis, f.33 deciphers f.32 (2nd block on), f.35 deciphers f.36, f.37 deciphers f.38. Different cipher from fr.3053, but the deciphered plaintext is the King's version of the same news -> content crib for f.21/f.16.

## 6. Lasry/Tomokiyo GL.htm (http://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/GL.htm)
- "Mascon's Cipher: BnF fr.3071 f.9 no.4 ... cardinal de Mascon a Montmorency, Rome, XIe avril 1537. *The same as Mascon's cipher from BnF fr.3053." Lasry's key for fr.3071 = key for fr.3053 (already in hand per project notes). Also fr.3081 f.41 / Dupuy 265 f.132 (29 May 1535) other Mâcon cipher.

## 7. MAE Correspondance politique Rome 3-4 (17th-c. copies) — check early quires
- Lemaitre (CTHS chapter https://books.openedition.org/cths/4082 ; HAL https://paris1.hal.science/hal-03893726/) : volumes long attributed to Mâcon alone; contain Selve's letters "between Easter 1537 and June 1538", jointly with Mâcon to the King. Easter 1537 = 1 April, so a joint letter of ~6 Apr 1537 (our f.35 date) could be present in clear. Copies are in clear (17th c. copy of decrypted file copies).

## 9. Other checked / weak
