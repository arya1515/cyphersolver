# BL Add MS 32256 f. 235, DECODE R9218: Schaeblin to Mr Jones, The Hague, 16 Jan 1753

Status: explained (not a cipher, read in full), 21 Sept 2026. Catalogue item 72 (class A), removed.

**Record.** DECODE R9218, "Mr. Jones (Hague) to Ben. Schaeblin", 1753, Non-decrypted, 2 pp., nomenclator, numerical,
images behind login. Fetched with the stored DECODE cookie (`img/`, git-ignored; `rec9218.htm` likewise).

**What it is.** No cipher anywhere on the two pages. A plain English letter, and the direction is reversed: it is
**from C. Ben. Schaeblin at The Hague, 16 January 1753, to Mr Jones** ("To Mr Jones" bottom left, signature bottom
right). Schaeblin is compiling a new *French* cipher for the British service, modelled on "the English Cypher":

- He sends a list of the ministers currently employed by the courts of Europe for insertion, asks for additions and
  omissions, and lists names in the English cipher whose holders are dead or out of office.
- Places: he kept all those of the English cipher and added the European settlements in Asia, Africa and America,
  "as those places will chiefly be the subject of French papers that may be wanted to be put into cypher".
- Size: the same as the English one, 3000 cyphers (numbers), with several blanks.
- Disguise without a second cipher, each method opened by a "Change the Cypher" group as in the last English
  ciphers and closed by another mark: write each number backwards (1753 → 3571); first figure to the end
  (1753 → 7531); last figure to the head (1753 → 3175); add a dummy figure at the head or end (21753, 17536).
  Methods not to be mixed or to follow one another without returning to the natural way.
- A week after the answer he will send a fair copy.

So DECODE's "nomenclator; numerical" describes the cipher the letter is *about*; the numbers on the page are
only the worked examples on 1753. Full text: `transcription.txt`.

**Who.** "Mr Jones" is presumably the Secretary of State's office (Add MS 32256 is in the Newcastle Papers,
diplomatic correspondence of 1753); Schaeblin is otherwise unidentified here (one web search, nothing). Not graded
further: the reading is the letter itself.

**Doubtful.** "minded" (line 7 of f. 235v) probably = "mixed" or "mingled"; the sense is not affected.

**Prior art.** None needed; the letter is in clear. The French cipher it announces (3000 groups, c. 1753) may be
among the SP 106 / Add MS key records on DECODE (94 in the series, nearest R9219, 1760): not checked, not part of
this target.

**DECODE corrections.** Sender and receiver swapped; not a ciphertext (cleartext English letter about making a
cipher); date 1753-01-16; status should be Decrypted / not applicable.
