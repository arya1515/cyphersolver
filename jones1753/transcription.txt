Sr.                                                    Hague Janry 16. 1753.

I have yr Honr of sending you inclosed a List of Names of most of the Ministers actually employed
by the several Courts of Europe, in publick Affairs, which I intended to insert into the new
[French, interlined] Cypher, and beg you will send it me back with the Additions or omissions
that may be thought proper; I have added a List of names, that though in the English Cypher
are either dead or no more in a publick Capacity, & in regard to wch I beg to be instructed.
I did not think it necessary to send you also a List of the Places, Countries &c. I kept all
those that are found in the English Cypher, & added many more, particularly ye names of most
of the European Settlements in Asia, Africa, & America, as those places will chiefly be ye
Subject of French Papers that may be wanted to be put into Cypher. As for ye rest, I shall
make it in ye same manner as ye English, with ye same number of Cyphers, viz. 3000. and bring
likewise in several Blanks. In Order to disguise it sometimes, without ye help of a Second
Cypher, I propose several Methods, for Instance, by taking ye Figures of every Cypher
backwards, E.G. 1753. write 3571. and vice versâ in decyphering it; Item, by putting ye first
figure at ye End, E.G. 1753. write 7531. Item by

[f. 235v]
by putting ye last figure at ye head, as 1753 write 3175. & both vice versâ in decyphering.
Item by adding any indifferent figure at ye head or end of each Cypher, wch is then only to be
taken off in decyphering, E.G. 1753. write 21753, or 17536. each of these Methods might be
marked where it begins, by two or three Cyphers like the Change the Cypher [underlined] in the
last English Cyphers; & some other marks, where it changes again into the natural way. To avoid
Confusion, I wd add a Note that these different Methods are not to be minded [= mixed?], or to
follow one another immediately, without Changing before into ye natural way. I beg ye favour
you will let me know, wch of these several Methods I may make use of, & as soon as I am
honoured with yr Answer, I shall only want a week's Time to put it entirely in order, &
transmit a fair Copy of it. I shall do my Utmost Endeavours to make it as compleat as possible,
& flatter myself to succeed as far as can be expected. -- I have ye Honour &c

To Mr Jones.                  &c &c                                   C. Ben. Schaeblin
