key not found: the Segreteria di Stato’s code, groups 14–2460

London, Paris → Naples · numerical code · April 1816 – October 1819 · attempted, not solved

Castelcicala to the Marchese di Circello, 1816–1819

Archivio di Stato di Napoli, Ministero degli affari esteri 2337, items 4–37; DECODE R9553, R9555–R9557, R9582–R9586: nine despatches, thirteen pages, in figures between clear openings and closings.

The images were viewed and three pages transcribed. The sibling records that DECODE suggests might hold the plaintexts were checked. They are in other codes.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026

On this pageThe documentsThe cipherWhat was triedWhat would finish itSources
Summary. The catalogue listed nine DECODE records as despatches of the principe di Castelcicala “to an unknown recipient”, with a DECODE note that they might belong to plaintexts in box 2317. The images name the recipient. R9553 is addressed to the Marchese di Circello, the Neapolitan Secretary of State for foreign affairs, and is headed “Cifra della Segreteria di Stato”. The ministry’s general code was used, not a personal key. The code runs from 14 to 2460 without word division, and whole phrases repeat within one letter. That points to a codebook of words and syllables. The only box 2317 record on DECODE, R9549, uses a different code (a 1798 Vienna despatch and a letter signed “de Baptiste”). The decrypted records in box 2337 are telegrams and letters of the 1850s and 1860s in other codes. No edition of the despatches was found. 331 groups from three pages give 208 distinct values, far too few to break a code of about 2,500 entries without its book. Status: not solved. The despatches could be read with the 1816–19 Segreteria code or Circello’s deciphered copies, if either survives in the Naples archive.

01 The documents

Fabrizio Ruffo, principe di Castelcicala, represented the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in London and then in Paris (1815–1832). DECODE dates the nine records 1 April, 3 June, 6 September and 30 October 1816 (London and Paris), 14 November 1817, and 3 April, 13 July and 28 October 1819. R9586 is undated. The letters close in clear, for example “Ho l’onore di essere con profondo ossequio di V. E. … Parigi 14 Nov.re 1817”, signed “Il Principe di Castelcicala”. R9553 is numbered 2314 and has “Eccellenza” at its head, the note “Cifra della Segreteria di Stato” in the corner, and the address “Ecc.mo Sig.r Marchese di Circello, S. S. S., Napoli” at its foot. DECODE leaves the receiver blank. The correction is queued for DECODE.

02 The cipher

The groups are numbers from 14 to 2460, separated by dots. Most lie between 1350 and 2460, and low groups such as 14, 40, 86 and 154 are rare. In the 1819 letter a few groups are written with a trailing zero (15680, 18300, 23530), probably a mark on the base group. Inside R9553 the run 1901 2445 1652 1633 occurs twice, 2358 1726 1454 twice, and 1999 1531 four times. Repeats like these belong to a codebook, not to a letter cipher.

03 What was tried

  • Box 2317. The only record from that box on DECODE is R9549, which has cipher pages beside clear text. Its code is different: its common groups are 441, 486, 563, 1144 and 1951, and it stops near 1999. None of these is frequent in Castelcicala’s letters. It gives no key here.
  • Decrypted siblings in box 2337. R9550–R9552, R9568–R9581 and R9589–R9590 are later material. R9550, for example, is Pasquale Massone’s despatch from Washington of 28 October 1859, in a syllabic code with an interlinear reading. They are not the 1816 code.
  • Print. No edition of the ciphered despatches came up. The Treccani biography of Fabrizio Ruffo cites the ASNa papers but prints no decipherments.
  • Ciphertext only. 208 distinct groups in 331 means most values occur once or twice. The other catalogue entry from the same box (no. 166, 15 records, 51 pages) probably uses the same code. Even with it, there would be too little text to rebuild a book of about 2,500 entries.

04 What would finish it

The despatches could be read with the Segreteria di Stato’s codebook of 1816–19 or the ministry’s deciphered copies. Both would sit in the Archivio di Stato di Napoli, in the Esteri series for the Paris legation or in the cipher office’s papers. Either would make the reading mechanical.
