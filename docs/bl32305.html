Paris 1719 and “Carré” 1742–45 code letters

British Library, Add MS 32305, catalogued as “unidentified cipher keys”: in fact a codebreaker’s file of intercepted letters, on DECODE as R2963–R2979. The catalogue here grouped six of those records as one Paris correspondence of 1719–1749.

All twelve records R2963–R2974 were viewed. They are several unrelated letters. Most were deciphered between the lines at the time. Two codes stay unread: the Paris letters of 7 and 10 February 1719, and the letters to Mr Carré in Pall Mall, 1742–45.

Daniel Bourdeau · attempted 21 September 2026

Summary. Catalogue item 78 listed six DECODE records from BL Add MS 32305 under one unknown sender in Paris. The images show a working file, not a correspondence. R2963 (a German letter, 1719) and R2974 (a letter cipher, November 1749) carry full decipherments written over the figures at the time, and so do four neighbouring records outside the item. R2969, listed as the nearest key, is a frequency count headed “Carré 1742”, not a key. The open material is two codes. The Paris letters of 7 and 10 February 1719 (R2964, R2965) are clear French with runs of 3-figure code. The 7 February letter has 354 groups, 191 distinct values, 116 of them seen once, and the highest is 900. The decipherer of the time glossed about a dozen common values, and those glosses are not in alphabetical order, so the code is two-part and the known values bracket nothing. The Carré letters (R2968, R2970, R2972) are entirely in figures, with no gloss and no crib. Neither code can be broken from this much ciphertext. Their key, a clear copy, or many more letters in the same code would be needed.

02 The Paris letters of February 1719

The writer reports Lord Stair’s formal entry into Paris and his audience with the King, and tells his correspondent that he will stay in England “jusqu’au retour de S. M. Brit.” The clear text uses a family cover (“vos Parens”, “votre Cousin”, “le caractère des esprits de votre Famille”), and the second letter opens on the coming war with Spain and Cardinal Alberoni. The persons are figures marked with a cross: 27, 258, 285, 611.

The decipherer glossed 36 = re, 88 = pas, 95 = le, 184 = que, 243 = vous, 347 = a, 531 = pour, 652 = qu’il and 786 = s, the same values in both letters. That is 33 of the 354 groups of the first letter. In a one-part code, where values run in alphabetical order, these would bracket the rest. Here re (36) comes before pas (88), le (95) and que (184), and a comes at 347, so the code is two-part and its order says nothing. With about 700 groups in the two letters for a code of some 900 entries, most values occur once, and the decipherer stopped where anyone would.

03 The Carré letters, 1742–45

Letters addressed to “Mr Carré in Pall Mall”, entirely in 3-figure groups up to about 800, with no clear text and no gloss. The decipherer’s whole work is the frequency count on f. 13 (686 twenty times, 212 nineteen times, 106 eighteen, with dotted guesses de, de, la for the first three). No crib is known, and the same verdict applies.

04 What would open it

  • The key of either code, or a clear copy of any of the letters, in the Newcastle or Carteret papers (BL Add MS 32686–33057) or SP 107/SP 78 at Kew.
  • More letters in the 1719 code: its common values are known, so a few thousand groups would let the syllables be built up from context.
