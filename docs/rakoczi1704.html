Four letters “of Ferenc Rákóczi II”: three were written to him

MNL OL G15 Caps. C. Fasc. 39 pp. 115, 312, 346 and P237 Festetics 10. d. 2. 42–44 (DECODE R852, R912, R922, R483). The catalogue listed all four as ciphered despatches of Rákóczi to an unknown recipient. The three French letters are reports sent to him from the Polish court, and a preserved key reads them. The Latin one really is his. It was deciphered line by line when it arrived.

Daniel Bourdeau · solved in part 21 September 2026

On this pageThe documents and the keysR922: Dantzig, 28 OctoberR852: DecemberR912: “au quartier”, 5 NovemberR483: Rákóczi, Lwów, 27 June [1711]What remains uncertainSources
Summary. Catalogue item 44 grouped four DECODE records attributed to Ferenc Rákóczi II, dated only by the volume's range, 1704–1711. Three of them (R852, R912, R922) address “Monseigneur” and “Votre Altesse”. They are intelligence reports to Rákóczi, like DECODE R902 (Groffey to Rákóczi, 1707). Applied unchanged, the key preserved as DECODE R639, headed De Monsieur de Bonac et Graffei, reads all three. It gives values for 2,653 of 2,839 transcribed groups, and the output is French with the right names in the right places: Danzig, Stanisław, the King of Sweden, the Tsar, Rehnskiöld, the Palatines of Belz and Ruthenia, and Tököly. The fourth record (R483) is a Latin letter in Rákóczi's name, dated Lwów, 27 June [1711], from his Polish exile after Munkács fell. It uses a different key and carries a full contemporary interlinear decipherment. The unread residue in the French letters comes mostly from errors in DECODE's digit transcription. The writer is unsigned, and one letter names Groffey in the third person. R483's key has not been rebuilt.

R639 is a one-part numerical nomenclator. Codes 10–120 are letters and endings, 121–460 syllables and common words, and 461–560 titles, peoples and places. The twelve odd numbers from 97 to 119 are nulls. Coverage alone proves little here, because nearly every number from 10 to 560 has a value. The test is that the output reads.

02 R922: Dantzig, 28 October

Monseigneur, je me suis souvenu depuis quelque temps de l’honneur d’escrire à Vostre Altesse … fait à Madame la Palatine de Belz … une copie authentique de cette vente au sieur Kray … pour joindre le Roy de Pologne … l’avantage de luy continuer mes services à la Cour de Pologne … je suis avec tout le respect possible, Monseigneur.

The writer reports a sale carried out for the Palatine of Belz (Elżbieta Sieniawska) and says he has kept out of the business as far as he could. He mentions Tököly and orders for carbines. He is about to leave to join the King of Poland. He asks that the sieur Kray be given orders that would let him go on serving Rákóczi at the Polish court. The second page deals with money and with someone who has been taken prisoner.

03 R852: December

The writer acknowledges letters from Rákóczi and will pass letters on “au sieur Groffey”. Groffey is named in the third person, so he is not the writer of this letter. The rest is news of the Diet and the marshal, the Germans and the Dutch, Volhynia and “vos ennemis”. Letters from Ráday and Count Bercsényi (“Berthoti”) are to be forwarded by way of Cracow. Of the three, this transcription is the noisiest. beaucoup and serviteur keep appearing where letters should be, which points either to digit slips or to a slightly different issue of the table.

04 R912: “au quartier”, 5 November

J’ay eu l’honneur … le Roy Stanislas … les Svedois, le Palatin de Belz, le grand Général … la pacification … une nouvelle élection en Pologne … le Czar … escrite au maréchal Rehnskiöld … Monsieur le Palatin de Russie … avec un profond respect et un zèle parfait.

The letter is a survey of Polish and Swedish politics. It covers the Grand and Field Hetmans, the Muscovites, the King of Sweden's plans for a pacification, the Cossacks and the Emperor. It also discusses Rákóczi's letter to Rehnskiöld, the affair behind R902. A regiment Rákóczi was asked to supply has become a grievance that King Stanisław and the Germans hold against him.

05 R483: Rákóczi, Lwów, 27 June [1711]

This is a different system: values run to about 800, and the plaintext is Latin. The whole letter carries an interlinear decipherment in a contemporary hand, which DECODE transcribed as 166 lines, many of them queried. It opens “Leopoli vigesima septima Junii”. It speaks of Vienna and the allied powers, the Emperor and Pálffy, a manifesto published “in omnibus Hungariae comitatibus”, and cannon brought up against “arcem nostram Munkácsiensem”. Munkács surrendered on 24 June 1711, so the year is 1711, and DECODE's “7 June” is probably a misreading of the date. The R639 table gives nonsense on this text, as expected.

06 What remains uncertain

  • 186 groups in the French letters are unread (I). They are joined groups, values above 560 or one-digit slips in the DECODE transcription. They need the images to resolve.
  • The years 1707 for the French letters rest on their content, the Rehnskiöld affair shared with R902 of 15 October 1707 (C).
  • Writer: unsigned. R912 and R922 are consistent with Groffey, but R852 is by someone else who writes to Rákóczi about Groffey (M).
  • R483: the key has not been rebuilt, and a clean Latin text needs the images, since DECODE's transcription of the interlinear is heavily queried.
