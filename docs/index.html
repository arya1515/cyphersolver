★ Adjudicated · September 2026

The Voynich manuscript: hoax, cipher or language, unanswerable — but the field can be narrowed

Six computational tests on the public transliteration against eleven languages and implemented hoax generators, each re-run by an adversarial checker, and five literature sweeps. The object is a genuine early-15th-century codex. The text is not a plaintext, one-to-one substitution, transposition or vowel-less rendering of any European language tested: its conditional character entropy sits at 2.2 to 2.9 bits under four transliteration alphabets against a floor of 3.3 for every language.

What survives is exactly two hypotheses, and they are not yet separable: a verbose or slot-template encoding of short units with meaning behind it, or a structured meaningless text produced by a copy-and-modify procedure. Every statistic that separates Voynichese from language is reproduced by at least one model in each camp. The write-up lists the five tests that would separate them.

2.2–2.9bits of conditional entropy, against 3.3 for language
1.7–2.3×more rigid word grammar than any language tested
0.09 bitsH(language | scribe): Currier A/B is almost the hand

The Chinese gold bar cryptograms — ten of everything

Sixteen strings on seven bars said to certify $300,000,000, unread for ninety years. They contain almost exactly ten of every letter of the alphabet: chi-squared 1.25 against uniform where chance predicts 25, about a trillion to one. No cipher can flatten a distribution past what randomness allows, so nobody has read them because there is nothing to read.

Elizabethtown, NY · 1883too short to break

The Debosnys cryptograms — a French syllabary, and why it stops there

A convicted wife-killer’s invented script, about 1,200 glyphs, unread since he was hanged. Three passages transcribed. The cipher poem is twenty lines of rhyming couplets, and line length and rhyme both make the glyphs French syllables. Every testable crib failed against controls that would have caught a hit, and the whole corpus sits at the unicity distance of such a cipher: it falls only to a source from his papers.

final glyph matches in 9 of 10 couplets · 0 of 9 across · planted 1,000-glyph syllabary: 0% recovered

Rome → Spain · 1542model class excluded

The Vatican cipher of April 1542 — identified as an Antonio Elio cipher

Farnese to the nuncio Poggio, four folios, 6549 digits, open since Lasry set it in 2019. Still unread — but now named. It is a polyphonic-syllabic cipher of the kind Antonio Elio built for Paul III’s chancery, and five independent measurements match that design, including a doubled-digit suppression that reproduces the chancery’s own written rule against doubling consonants. The transcript everyone works from has lost the word division its solved sibling’s transcript kept.

02 Priority queue

Everything open, from all three source lists, on one scale. Odds is the estimated chance of a full reading; effort is the work needed to find out. A cheap decisive test outranks an expensive long shot, so a coin-flip that resolves in an hour sits above a one-in-four that costs a week. Badged entries come from Elonka Dunin's famous unsolved codes and Schmeh's Top 50.

Worth continuing no archive access needed; ranked by odds against effort, the next step named in each

1

Telegram from Switzerland, “BLUME SALAMANCA”1937

evenan hour of compute

Zurich to London and on to Spain, 8 January 1937, from the firm of Werner Oswald, who had close ties to Franco's side. In progress, September 2026. The first telegram is transcribed here from Schmeh's photograph, 123 groups, and checks against the 125 words on the form. It is a transposition of Spanish: index of coincidence 0.070, and Spanish fits the letter counts far better than French, English or German. Excluded, with every search first proven on planted Spanish: rail fence, skip and route transpositions (107,594 readings) and single columnar. A C# solver built for double transposition breaks planted messages of this length completely up to key width 17 (same key) or 11 × 14 (two keys); the real run over that range is next. It is not solved, and the second telegram, which is not public, would help most.

ADFGVX residue of the Eastern Front1918 top 50

incremental winsmedium effort

Keys published, decoder built, page 100 reproduced from scratch. Page 132's key is identified and WIEDERHOLE and TELEG emerged unprompted, so a deeper garble model (more than two insertions, deletions with substitutions, column-boundary shifts) should finish it; page 146 shows partial words on a length-17 key. Near-certain incremental wins; a low chance of reading all twenty-two.

Copenhagen cryptogramc.1950s top 50

not a simple substitution

Three lines found behind an 1835 portrait of a Danish general, 107 characters. Attempted September 2026. Transcribed twice (20 and 25 symbols), attacked in ten languages under six reading conventions and a word-separator hypothesis with a 5-gram annealer and dictionary re-ranking, then confirmed with 300 restarts. Nothing readable: best −2.7 nats per letter, while matched controls of the same length in Danish, German, English, Latin and Swedish are recovered at 96–100% and −1.4 to −2.1. Either not a simple substitution of those languages, or both readings share an error only the original slip could fix. Full write-up →

·

Scorpion letters1991 top 50

below unicity distance

Two Zodiac-style cryptograms, 70 symbols with 53 distinct and 180 with 145 distinct. Attempted September 2026. Every S5 repeat falls at a multiple of 16; S1 shows a weak period-5 signal (p = 0.04). Both carry more key information than the English text has redundancy (249 vs 224 bits, 682 vs 576), and matched controls return fluent English at 3–13% letter accuracy, so no ciphertext-only solution can be verified. A claimed 2018 solution is consistent with the repeats but scores worse than the controls' false solutions. Needs S2–S4. Full write-up →

·

Abwehr agent Koehler's messages, New York to Berlin1944 top 50

skipped: intractable

Five messages, 924 letters, from the Dutch double agent Walter Koehler. Attempted September 2026. Every tractable class is excluded against controls: substitution and transposition by the index of coincidence; Vigenère, Beaufort and autokey at every period in German, English and Dutch; a running key from book text, which would show in the letter counts (true cases gain at least 7.5 nats, these score below uniform); and any Enigma, because the counts are too uneven for machine output (χ² 57.9, p = 0.0003). What survives is a book key through a mixed alphabet table, the documented prayer-book system, or a hand one-time pad. Neither falls without the book or the FBI's plaintexts. The attempt also corrects one letter of the circulating text.

·

Censorship manual steganogramsWW2 top 50

image resolution

A British censorship manual (TNA KV 2/2424) gives the plaintext of a map and a fashion drawing but not the readings. Attempted September 2026. The one published fragment (AATHUT → LLESFE, alles fertig) fixes the map's shift direction, and the exact dot-dash sequence any German wording would leave is now computed, so a reading can be checked mark by mark. The tram bands themselves are ordinary map fill; the Morse is pen marks beside them, and those are 0.1–0.3 mm — 2–5 pixels in the best public photograph, where dots and dashes merge. Needs a 1,200 dpi scan of pp. 14 and 17. The manual's “French shorthand” points to Duployé for the signature.

Debosnys cryptograms1882–83 top 50

attempted, not solved

About 1,200 glyphs in an invented script, left by a man hanged in 1883. Three passages transcribed here, 969 glyphs. The cipher poem is rhyming couplets — the last glyph matches within 9 of 10 couplets and across none of the boundaries — and line length and rhyme both point to a French syllabary. Every crib that could be tested failed against controls that would have found a hit: the poem on the same page, 7,821 couplet windows of French verse, Delille's Aeneid, Moore. The corpus sits at its unicity distance and a planted syllabary of the same length recovers 0%, so it falls only to a crib from his papers. Full write-up →

·

William Perwich to Lord Arlington1670

found solved

Solved in October 2025 by Matthew Brown, and independently by Lasry, Biermann and Tomokiyo: a 20-column transposition with nulls. This entry had called it a substitution, and that was wrong — the eight q's, seven of them nulls, add 106 to the in-place chi-squared, and on the plaintext cells it is an ordinary 28. Reproduced here from the transcription: rows 2–21 are the columns, rows 1 and 22 are null lines, a keyless quadgram climb recovers the order, and 414 plaintext cells read out “the souldiers grumble much that the king is of late growne cool towards them…”. Only the nomenclator numbers remain.

·

Vatican Challenge, Part 5 — Farnese to Poggio1542

family identified

Identified as a polyphonic-syllabic cipher of the kind Antonio Elio built for Paul III's chancery — the chancery that sent this letter. Six sessions: the Meister keys verified from the page scans and excluded on structure, the letter dated from its own cleartext (15 April 1542), the sibling and relative records located in DECODE, and a unit-level attack (segmentation by EM, then annealing) shown by a matched control to fail on the segmentation step. Still unread; needs the 400 dpi images behind the DECODE login, the key in Chigi M II 49, or the clear register copy.

D’Agapeyeff cipher1939 famous

diagnosed

196 digit pairs, and the author admitted he had forgotten his own method. Five independent lines — frequency against the author's own worked control, no transposition signal, no periodicity, repetition far below language, and the book's own null rule swept — all say near-uniform random. Consistent with a botched encipherment rather than a lost key.

Kaliningrad bottle postfound 2015 top 50

transcription

The blocker is transcription, not cryptanalysis: it exists only as two photographs and nobody has ever published the character string. Attempted and abandoned honestly — cursive n, u, v and w are barely separable and the verso bleeds through. Two observations stand: the primed consonants are exactly the Russian palatalisation set, and the text carries dotted initialism groups that would be the natural home for Bible citations under the outstanding Synodal-Bible claim.
