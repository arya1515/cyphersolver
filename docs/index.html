01 Write-ups

United States · 1808solved

Armstrong to Madison — the coded postscript

Forty-nine groups of the 1,600-element "THE = 972" diplomatic code, read in full after reconstructing 580 groups from the State Department's own pencil decodes on NARA microfilm M34 roll 13.

"Russel ought to be the consul: he is an American by birth … Next to him in fitness is O'Mealy, but he is, like Warden, an Irishman."

France · 1629solved

Richelieu to M. de Rancé — BnF Français 3829

Ciphertext-only recovery of a homophonic alphabet with a doubling mark and nomenclature; then found to agree word for word with the decipherment Avenel printed in 1858, which the catalogues had missed.

"Castor voudroit bien que la [duchesse de Chevreuse] peust estre attrapée près de la frontière…"

Ireland / England · 1634–35alphabet recovered

Maltravers to Ormonde — a regular block cipher

Doubled letters written with consecutive figures betray a regular key (consonants three figures each from 7, vowels from 64, nulls 91–111). Every spelled word reads and the nomenclator falls into place from the 1634 Irish Parliament.

"he was angry [with the Lord Deputy] … brought no letters … upon his motion [Ormonde] is to be a councellor"

Brussels · 1659–60explained

Hyde's ciphered superscriptions — not a cipher at all

The four "undeciphered addresses" on Hyde's letters to Barwick decode to nothing under the full Hyde–Barwick key printed in 1721 — because, as the 1724 editor states, they were numbers "signifying nothing … only to puzzle the Enemy".

"some Persons … have wondered what was the meaning of them" — Life of Barwick, 1724

1Richelieu → M. de Rancé, BnF Français 3829 ff. 87 & 891629solvedHomophonic alphabet, doubling mark and nomenclature recovered ciphertext-only; the reading agrees word for word with the decipherment later found in Avenel (1858), which the catalogues (DECODE R9461–R9462) had missed.
2Armstrong → Madison, coded postscript1808solved49/49 groups; code table of 580 groups reconstructed. Bonus: the letter of 22 Feb 1808 largely read. The distinct code of the 20 Feb 1808 letter remains open.
3Charles II → Duke of Hamilton1650offline onlyFour letters, 102 groups, no redundancy and no printed key (the Camden Society volume of 1880 prints them undeciphered). The key survives: National Records of Scotland GD406/1/2197, “Keys for ciphers used in the correspondence of the Duke of Hamilton” (5 items, open); the letters are GD406/1/10573–10576. Solvable only by ordering copies from Edinburgh.
4Colbert passages (Mélanges Colbert)1665–74stuckThree short passages (Ratisbon 1665; Charost, Calais 1673; abbé de Gravel → Maulevrier 1674). No key online; the twelve reconstructed Colbert-series keys all fail; too short for cryptanalysis. Only route left is finding a deciphered sibling letter by paging Gallica volumes.
5Prince Maurice → Prince Rupert, Worcester, 7 July 16451645offline onlyFull text printed in Warburton (93 groups, max 398); the two known Rupert keys of 1644–45 do not fit and the text is far too short to break. The key would sit in Rupert's own papers (BL Add MS 18980–82) or among the 49 Digby keys in Add MS 72438 — all digitised, all offline since the British Library cyber-attack (IIIF returns 403); DECODE copies need an account and BL permission.
6Royalist intercepts, BL Add MS 72438 ff. 9–101646offline onlyThe ciphertexts are not available online at all (DECODE R8623–8624 are private; BL images offline). The same volume holds Weckherlin's 49 captured Digby cipher-keys, so this is a key-matching job the moment the images return, not a cryptanalysis problem.
7Maltravers → Ormonde1634–35alphabet recovered59 figures. Nulls 91–111 and a regular block alphabet recovered from the consecutive-figure doublets; angry, letters, Crosby, a councellor read and the sentences match the events of 1634–35 (Kildare's quarrel with Wentworth; Ormonde sworn of the Council, Jan 1635). Nine nomenclator codes inferred from context only.
8Hyde superscriptions1659–60explainedHyde → Barwick letters printed in the Vita Barwick (1721) with their full key. The superscriptions decode to nothing (three use numbers above the key's range); the 1724 editor states they were dummy numbers "only to puzzle the Enemy". Not a cipher.
9Thurloe intercepts (Dutch, French, English)1653–56stuckFour pieces: Beverning–Vande Perre to Boreel 1653 (136 groups, Dutch), and three 1656 Brussels/Bruges intercepts of 16–50 groups. All 1653–60 keys on cryptiana tried; Dutch/French hill-climbs on the 1653 letter give only fragments. Too short; originals (Bodleian Rawlinson A) not online.
10Stepney → Earl of Manchester, Vienna 23 March 17021702offline onlyOriginal located and transcribed from Yale Beinecke OSB MSS fc37 (24 groups; MS reads 413 not 412). Not the Manchester THE = 454 key; Manchester had asked for "Mr. Stepney's cipher" in Aug 1701, so the key is Stepney's office cipher — TNA SP 105/106 or BL Add MSS 7058–78, not online.
