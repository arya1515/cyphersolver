★ Famous case closed · September 2026

The Chinese gold bar cryptograms are unsolved — because there is nothing in them

Seven bars said to have been issued in Shanghai in 1933 and to certify a deposit of $300,000,000, stamped with sixteen strings of Latin letters. Published by the IACR in 1996, carried on Elonka Dunin’s list of famous unsolved codes ever since, and never read.

They were never read because they are not an encryption. The 263 letters contain almost exactly ten of every letter of the alphabet — twenty-one of the twenty-six appear exactly ten times. That is flatter than randomness itself permits, and no cipher can do it: substitution and transposition both preserve the plaintext’s frequency skew, and even a one-time pad is still random sampling. Somebody counted out ten of each letter and arranged them to look like cryptography — chi-squared 1.25 against uniform where chance predicts 25, about a trillion to one, and not one of 200,000 simulated strings came out this flat.

The documentary case now agrees from a second direction, and it was not recorded here before: the aircraft stamped on the bars is a model that entered service in 1934, after the date they claim; the general named was promoted in 1936; the year is Gregorian rather than Republican; and some characters are simplified forms not official until the mid-1950s.

Elizabethtown, NY · 1883too short to break

The Debosnys cryptograms — a French syllabary, and why it stops there

A convicted wife-killer’s invented script, about 1,200 glyphs, unread since he was hanged. Three passages transcribed. Rome → Spain · 1542model class excluded

The Vatican cipher of April 1542 — identified as an Antonio Elio cipher

Farnese to the nuncio Poggio, four folios, 6549 digits, open since Lasry set it in 2019. Still unread — but now named. It is a polyphonic-syllabic cipher of the kind Antonio Elio built for Paul III’s chancery, and five independent measurements match that design, including a doubled-digit suppression that reproduces the chancery’s own written rule against doubling consonants. The transcript everyone works from has lost the word division its solved sibling’s transcript kept.

02 Priority queue

Everything open, from all three source lists, on one scale. Odds is the estimated chance of a full reading; effort is the work needed to find out. A cheap decisive test outranks an expensive long shot, so a coin-flip that resolves in an hour sits above a one-in-four that costs a week. Badged entries come from Elonka Dunin's famous unsolved codes and Schmeh's Top 50.

Attack now ranked by odds against effort

1

Telegram from Switzerland, “BLUME SALAMANCA”1937

evenmedium effort

Zurich to London and on to Spain, 8 January 1937, from the firm of Werner Oswald, who had close ties to Franco's side. In progress, September 2026. The first telegram is transcribed here from Schmeh's photograph, 123 groups, and checks against the 125 words on the form. It is a transposition of Spanish: index of coincidence 0.070, and Spanish fits the letter counts far better than French, English or German. Excluded, with every search first proven on planted Spanish: rail fence, skip and route transpositions (107,594 readings) and single columnar. A C# solver built for double transposition breaks planted messages of this length completely up to key width 17 (same key) or 11 × 14 (two keys); the real run over that range is next. It is not solved, and the second telegram, which is not public, would help most.

2

Copenhagen cryptogramc.1835 top 50

lowlow effort

Found behind an 1835 painting of a Danish general and sent to the American Cryptogram Association, which never broke it or even wrote it up. A hundred and seven characters over twenty-five symbols, almost certainly simple substitution and almost certainly not English. That makes it a multilingual reading problem rather than a cryptanalytic one, which is the angle nobody has pushed.

3

Scorpion letters1991 top 50

lowmedium effort

About two hundred and fifty symbols over two homophonic ciphers in Zodiac style; the remaining letters are withheld by law enforcement. The Z340 break showed what searching transposition alongside substitution can do, but this is a quarter of that length.

4

Japanese telegram decoded by Yardleyc.1920

partiallow effort

Yardley printed the plaintext, so the scheme is attackable, but Japanese diplomatic traffic of 1920 ran on codebooks. One message can only recover the entries it happens to use, so a complete answer is ruled out by construction.

·

Abwehr agent Koehler's messages, New York to Berlin1944 top 50

skipped: intractable

Five messages, 924 letters, from the Dutch double agent Walter Koehler. Attempted September 2026. Every tractable class is excluded against controls: substitution and transposition by the index of coincidence; Vigenère, Beaufort and autokey at every period in German, English and Dutch; a running key from book text, which would show in the letter counts (true cases gain at least 7.5 nats, these score below uniform); and any Enigma, because the counts are too uneven for machine output (χ² 57.9, p = 0.0003). What survives is a book key through a mixed alphabet table, the documented prayer-book system, or a hand one-time pad. Neither falls without the book or the FBI's plaintexts. The attempt also corrects one letter of the circulating text.

·

Censorship manual steganogramsWW2 top 50

image resolution

A British censorship manual (TNA KV 2/2424) gives the plaintext of a map and a fashion drawing but not the readings. Attempted September 2026. The one published fragment (AATHUT → LLESFE, alles fertig) fixes the map's shift direction, and the exact dot-dash sequence any German wording would leave is now computed, so a reading can be checked mark by mark. The tram bands themselves are ordinary map fill; the Morse is pen marks beside them, and those are 0.1–0.3 mm — 2–5 pixels in the best public photograph, where dots and dashes merge. Needs a 1,200 dpi scan of pp. 14 and 17. The manual's “French shorthand” points to Duployé for the signature.

·

Debosnys cryptograms1882–83 top 50

attempted, not solved

About 1,200 glyphs in an invented script, left by a man hanged in 1883. Three passages transcribed here, 969 glyphs. The cipher poem is rhyming couplets — the last glyph matches within 9 of 10 couplets and across none of the boundaries — and line length and rhyme both point to a French syllabary. Every crib that could be tested failed against controls that would have found a hit: the poem on the same page, 7,821 couplet windows of French verse, Delille's Aeneid, Moore. The corpus sits at its unicity distance and a planted syllabary of the same length recovers 0%, so it falls only to a crib from his papers. Full write-up →

·

William Perwich to Lord Arlington1670

found solved

Solved in October 2025 by Matthew Brown, and independently by Lasry, Biermann and Tomokiyo: a 20-column transposition with nulls. This entry had called it a substitution, and that was wrong — the eight q's, seven of them nulls, add 106 to the in-place chi-squared, and on the plaintext cells it is an ordinary 28. Reproduced here from the transcription: rows 2–21 are the columns, rows 1 and 22 are null lines, a keyless quadgram climb recovers the order, and 414 plaintext cells read out “the souldiers grumble much that the king is of late growne cool towards them…”. Only the nomenclator numbers remain.

·

Catokwacopa newspaper advertisements1875 top 50

readings audited

Two Standard advertisements whose lines are two order-preserving halves of one abbreviated phrase. The disputed question was how much the omission rule lets a reader invent, so it was measured. The pairing is structural (no random re-pairing in 100,000 fits the lengths). Consonant-initial words always start in the 8 May half. An open-vocabulary search forces DYING DECLARATION, REPEATED and OLD CAP BROKE AT CORNER LEFT INSTEAD, finds exact CHANGE ADOPTED and HOLIDAYS EXAMINE where published readings needed misprints, and shows MASTER PUPIL and SIGNED are emendations. Decisively, of 1,645 names only CONINGTON, JOWETT, SHIRLEY and HERTFORD fit their frames: the Oxford reading is right in outline. Lines 9, 12, 23 and 29 are not decided by the letters.

·

Vatican Challenge, Part 5 — Farnese to Poggio1542

family identified

Identified as a polyphonic-syllabic cipher of the kind Antonio Elio built for Paul III's chancery — the chancery that sent this letter. Five independent measurements match that design, including a doubled-digit suppression that recovers the chancery's own written rule against doubling consonants. Five model classes excluded, two against matched controls the same code solves. Still unread; needs the key or the manuscript's word division.

·

D’Agapeyeff cipher1939 famous

diagnosed

196 digit pairs, and the author admitted he had forgotten his own method. Five independent lines — frequency against the author's own worked control, no transposition signal, no periodicity, repetition far below language, and the book's own null rule swept — all say near-uniform random. Consistent with a botched encipherment rather than a lost key.

·

Maltravers to Ormonde — the last nine codes1634–35

partial

The alphabet is recovered and every spelled word reads. The nine nomenclator codes still resting on context need either the real key or more ciphertext, and neither is online.

·

Thomas Urquhart’s encrypted poems17th c. top 50

provenance

The structure of the August 2026 claim checks out — Wilcock records thirty-two proquiritations from petitioners hiding behind their initials, and the distich is two lines of thirty-two. But the provenance objection holds on an independent copy: the 1653 book ends in an errata table and contains zero runs of eight or more numbers, as does Wilcock 1899. The ciphertext could not be located in the source it is said to come from.

·

Kaliningrad bottle postfound 2015 top 50

transcription

The blocker is transcription, not cryptanalysis: it exists only as two photographs and nobody has ever published the character string. Attempted and abandoned honestly — cursive n, u, v and w are barely separable and the verso bleeds through. Two observations stand: the primed consonants are exactly the Russian palatalisation set, and the text carries dotted initialism groups that would be the natural home for Bible citations under the outstanding Synodal-Bible claim.

·

Colbert passages, Thurloe intercepts, D’Estaing, Le Tellier1653–1779

too short

Four separate items that fail the same way. Each is a short passage — sixteen to two hundred and seventeen groups — with every known period key already tested and failing, and hill-climbing yielding fragments only. None is long enough to solve unconstrained, and the originals are not digitised.

·

Voynich manuscriptc.1420 famous

not tractable

~38,000 word-tokens and six centuries of failure. No proposed reading has been independently verified, and the competing hypotheses — natural language in invented script, constructed language, cipher, hoax — remain unresolved.

·

Kryptos, passage K41990 famous

not tractable

97 characters. The plaintext was recovered from Sanborn's own papers in 2025 — explicitly not a cryptographic solve — and the archive sold at auction for $962,500. The method remains unbroken and the plaintext unpublished.

·

Zodiac Z13 and Z321970 famous

not tractable

Z408 fell in 1969 and Z340 in December 2020. What is left is thirteen and thirty-two symbols, far too short for any answer to be verifiable.

·

Dorabella cipher1897 famous

not tractable

Eighty-seven characters. Wase's 2023 statistical study finds it unlikely to be monoalphabetic English or Latin at all, which undercuts most of the century of proposed solutions at the root.

·

WWII pigeon cipher, Bletchingley1942? famous

not tractable

135 letters. GCHQ's position is that without the codebook the message cannot be decrypted, nor any claimed solution verified.

·

Enigma message of 10 January 19451945

not tractable

A single message, and compute-bound. The comparable unsolved M4 messages fell only to distributed computing.

·

Lüderitz consular telegram1911

not tractable

A five-figure codebook. One message recovers nothing without the book.

·

Phaistos disc, Linear A, Indus script, Rongorongo and the restc.2600 BC – c.1800 BC famous

not tractable

Undeciphered writing systems, not concealed messages. These are problems for linguistics, and several may not encode language at all. Meroitic can be read aloud without being understood, which is the clearest illustration of the difference.
