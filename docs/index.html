Working notes · September 2026

Unsolved Historical Ciphers

I pick an old cipher nobody has read, dig up whatever the archives still hold, and see how far an AI and a laptop can get with it. Sometimes a four-hundred-year-old letter opens up. More often it does not, and then the interesting part is finding out exactly why, and what would have to turn up for it to give way. This is the running log.

Targets from Tomokiyo's catalogue, Elonka Dunin's famous unsolved codes and Schmeh's Top 50.

★ Adjudicated · September 2026

The Voynich manuscript is probably elaborate nonsense

It is a real book from the early 1400s, but the text is not a language in disguise. Every simple cipher of every European language we could test is ruled out: the writing is far too predictable, letter to letter, for any of them. Two explanations are left standing, a rigid glyph-by-glyph encoding with something behind it, or carefully made meaningless text, and the numbers lean toward meaningless. The write-up names the five tests that would settle it.

2.2–2.9bits of conditional entropy, against 3.3 for language
1.7–2.3×more rigid word grammar than any language tested
0.09 bitsH(language | scribe): Currier A/B is almost the hand

02 Priority queue

Everything open, from all three source lists, on one scale. Odds is the estimated chance of a full reading; effort is the work needed to find out. A cheap decisive test outranks an expensive long shot, so a coin-flip that resolves in an hour sits above a one-in-four that costs a week. Badged entries come from Elonka Dunin's famous unsolved codes and Schmeh's Top 50.

Worth continuing no archive access needed; ranked by odds against effort, the next step named in each

1

Two anonymous letters to English Catholics in France, SP53/16 nos. 78 and 791585?

goodmedium effort

One to Mr Tempest, a priest in Paris, one to Dr Barret, president of the Rheims seminary, in the same hand, both endorsed by Phelippes and never read. Tomokiyo's transcriptions are online: 507 and 644 groups, 132 and 102 distinct, 34 and 13 hapax. That low hapax count is the signature of a homophonic alphabet with a small nomenclator, the shape of the Mary Queen of Scots letters that fell in 2023 and of the Richelieu reading on this site. If the two letters share one key the text doubles. Next step: test the symbol overlap between them, then a homophonic annealer in English, French and Latin with a planted control of the same length. Added to the queue September 2026, not yet attempted.

2

Telegram from Switzerland, “BLUME SALAMANCA”1937

evencompute in progress

Zurich to London and on to Spain, 8 January 1937, from the firm of Werner Oswald, who had close ties to Franco's side. In progress, September 2026. The first telegram is transcribed here from Schmeh's photograph, 123 groups, and checks against the 125 words on the form. It is a transposition of Spanish: index of coincidence 0.070, and Spanish fits the letter counts far better than French, English or German. Excluded, with every search first proven on planted Spanish: rail fence, skip and route transpositions (107,594 readings), single columnar, and now double columnar in the forward direction, same key at widths 2–30 and two keys at 2–16 × 2–16 (best −5.9 per quadgram where Spanish scores −3.9 and shuffled letters −6.3). The reversed direction and the divide-and-conquer attack for widths 15–25 are running. The second telegram, which is not public, would help most.

Feuquières to Catinat, Pignerol, 25 January 16911691

not solved, design fixed

Attempted September 2026. The 1819 Mémoires de Catinat print the 418 groups; collated with the Munich page images (three corrections to Tomokiyo’s copy). The editor says what it is: Feuquières concerting the surprise of Veillane fixed for 27 January, and Catinat’s own memoir for the operation survives as a crib. The code is a two-part “petit chiffre” of at most 366 entries, letters below 100, no column structure. Same size class as Chaulnes and the same wall: on a matched control every objective prefers fluent nonsense to the true key, and giving the solver 60 % of the code completes only 63 % of the rest. Bazeries’ 1893 book, read in full, confirms he deciphered it and that the petit chiffre of 1691 had 367 groups, but prints neither the text nor the table. The reading is in his papers at the Service historique de la Défense; that, or a second letter in the code, would open it. Notes →

Huang Xing telegram, the scheme corrected from the frames1916

scheme found, corrected

Second session. Frame 0247 read at 500 dpi gives a 46-character plaintext, and one superfluous kana at position 106 turns out to have hidden the repeats: with it dropped, 行 three times, 速 twice and 電 twice are identical kana triples. So the code is deterministic and the vowel is not a free homophone, which overturns the first reading; the seven row collisions are different characters. A row-to-digit permutation test against telegraph-code order is at chance. 42 codebook entries recovered; the kana-to-digit table needs a second telegram. Write-up, corrected →

Regent Moray to John Wood, the Scottish ambassador in London1568

undetermined

BL Add MS 32091 f.213, 13 July 1568, 134 groups over 32 symbols in Tomokiyo's transcription. The Catalogue of Additions says what the letter does: refuses Wood's recall and sends Border news. Attempted September 2026. A 5-gram Scots annealer, built from the Diurnal of Occurrents, the Privy Council register and Pitscottie, reads five of six planted 134-letter controls of the same symbol profile at 93–98%; on the letter it returns gibberish, scoring below every solved control, and English, French and Latin models do no better. No symbol behaves as a null or a word separator, Tomokiyo's variants are distinct symbols, twenty dragged cribs (the quene, Lethingtoun, Herreis…) are all rejected, and a word-code mask has no power at this length. Thirteen per cent of genuine Scots passages score below the solver's false optimum, and Border news is names, so the result is undetermined, not excluded. Needs the page, offline since the BL cyber-attack, or a second letter in the cipher.

Abwehr agent Koehler's messages, New York to Berlin1944 top 50

skipped: intractable

Five messages, 924 letters, from the Dutch double agent Walter Koehler. Attempted September 2026. Every tractable class is excluded against controls: substitution and transposition by the index of coincidence; Vigenère, Beaufort and autokey at every period in German, English and Dutch; a running key from book text, which would show in the letter counts (true cases gain at least 7.5 nats, these score below uniform); and any Enigma, because the counts are too uneven for machine output (χ² 57.9, p = 0.0003). transcription

The blocker is transcription, not cryptanalysis: it exists only as two photographs and nobody has ever published the character string. Attempted and abandoned honestly — cursive n, u, v and w are barely separable and the verso bleeds through. Two observations stand: the primed consonants are exactly the Russian palatalisation set, and the text carries dotted initialism groups that would be the natural home for Bible citations under the outstanding Synodal-Bible claim.

Colbert passages, Thurloe intercepts, D’Estaing, Le Tellier1653–1779

too short

Four separate items that fail the same way. Each is a short passage — sixteen to two hundred and seventeen groups — with every known period key already tested and failing, and hill-climbing yielding fragments only. None is long enough to solve unconstrained, and the originals are not digitised.

·

Voynich manuscriptc.1420 famous

not tractable

Adjudicated September 2026, not deciphered. Six computational tests on the transliteration against eleven languages and implemented hoax generators, each re-run adversarially, plus five literature sweeps. A plain or simply enciphered European language is excluded on transliteration-robust entropy (h2 2.2–2.9 bits against a 3.3 floor); Rugg's grille and free-edit self-citation are disfavoured; a verbose or slot-template encoding and a structured meaningless text are left roughly even, with the tests that would separate them. ·

Kryptos, passage K41990 famous

not tractable

97 characters. The plaintext was recovered from Sanborn's own papers in 2025 — explicitly not a cryptographic solve — and the archive sold at auction for $962,500. The method remains unbroken and the plaintext unpublished.

·

Zodiac Z13 and Z321970 famous

not tractable

Z408 fell in 1969 and Z340 in December 2020. What is left is thirteen and thirty-two symbols, far too short for any answer to be verifiable.

·

Dorabella cipher1897 famous

not tractable

Eighty-seven characters. Wase's 2023 statistical study finds it unlikely to be monoalphabetic English or Latin at all, which undercuts most of the century of proposed solutions at the root.

·

WWII pigeon cipher, Bletchingley1942? famous

not tractable

135 letters. GCHQ's position is that without the codebook the message cannot be decrypted, nor any claimed solution verified.

·

Enigma message of 10 January 19451945

not tractable

A single message, and compute-bound. The comparable unsolved M4 messages fell only to distributed computing.

·

Lüderitz consular telegram1911

not tractable

A five-figure codebook. One message recovers nothing without the book.

·

Phaistos disc, Linear A, Indus script, Rongorongo and the restc.2600 BC – c.1800 BC famous

not tractable

Undeciphered writing systems, not concealed messages. These are problems for linguistics, and several may not encode language at all. Meroitic can be read aloud without being understood, which is the clearest illustration of the difference.

Berthier to Napoleon, 22 December 18121812evenAttempted September 2026, and it moved a long way. The plaintext has been in print since 1912: Chuquet published Berthier’s December 1812 letters to the Emperor from the very carton the cryptogram sits in, AF/IV/1643. His Dalmatian papers are at the Service historique de la Défense, not the Archives nationales.

Verified here that the one published opening reads “Minister Chen, He Mi” — but He Mi is a separate codebook, not a transformation of the standard telegraph code, so there is no key space to search. Telegram from Huang Xing to Lin Hu and Li Genyuan1916scheme foundListed as “solved but specific scheme unknown”. Three kana per character, each kana carrying one digit in its gojuon consonant row while the vowel is a free homophone, so every digit has five spellings and the surface text barely repeats. Consonant-row triples collide seven times against 1.03 expected (p = 0.006); vowels collide at exactly chance (p = 0.70). The Foreign Ministry’s filed decode and its annotated Japanese reading recovered from JACAR.
Hyde’s ciphered superscriptions1659–60explainedNot a cipher. Dummy numbers, as the 1724 editor of the Life of Barwick states outright.

Ferdinand III and the Cardinal-Infante1634–40found solvedCarried here as blocked on a DECODE login. It had in fact been solved by Thomas Ernst in October 2017, in the comment thread of Schmeh’s own Top 50 post: a digit-pair code on the Habsburg AEIOU motto, each non-numeric sign encoding its count of strokes or semicircles. Nine years in print. The second target here to prove already solved in the open literature.
Chinese gold bar cryptograms1933no messageNot an encryption. The 263 letters contain almost exactly ten of every letter of the alphabet — chi-squared 1.25 against uniform where chance predicts 25, about a trillion to one. No cipher can flatten a distribution past what randomness allows. Corroborated independently by the documentary case: the aircraft depicted entered service in 1934, and some characters are simplified forms from the 1950s.
