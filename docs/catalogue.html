Catalogue of unsolved historical ciphers

What is still in cipher in the digitised French diplomatic volumes, and nobody has put on a list. Each entry is scored 1–5 for historical importance (what the text could add), solvability (odds of a full reading with the material online) and difficulty (the technical work), and the priority is a weighted blend you can reweight. Class A means siblings with decipherment in the same volume, B a partial key or known family in print, C no key and no sibling. Entries that have since been read, partly read or closed here carry an outcome tag and sit at the foot of the default order; the solved catalogue has the readings.

Scores are judgements from catalogue descriptions and the literature, not from the leaves: 9 of 32 entries have been viewed on the image, and each carries the check that would confirm it is open. Data: catalogue.json · text: CATALOGUE.md.

Correspondents. Gaspare Sormano, Gian Gioacchino de Passano (sieur de Vaulx, 'Jean Joachim') → François I · Ferrara → France · Italian
Shelfmark. BnF fr. 3096 nos. 63 (ff. 115, 116, 123), 65 (ff. 119-120), 66 (ff. 121-122, "duplicata") · btv1b9060015d · canvas = folio + 4; no. 63's third sheet is bound out of order at f. 123 (docket f. 123v); siblings no. 62 (f. 113, interlinear decipherment) and no. 67 (f. 124, marginal decipherment); no. 64 (f. 117) has no cipher
Status and prior art. Attempted 18 Sept 2026, letters NOT read but the key is VERIFIED. 22 glyph values: line 1 of the f. 124r crib is 13 glyphs against 13 letters of the contemporary marginal decipherment ('n capitaneo sui') and reads glyph by glyph; line 2 ('...eri nominato') confirms n, a, t, o independently and adds e, r, i, o, m; the f. 113r interlinear gloss spells capitaneo with the same glyphs five weeks earlier. Independent check without the 1529 decipherer: no. 66 gives in clear 'il parlar suo fu di sorte ch Jo Sormano mi parue quasi' where no. 65 ciphers it, and the key returns 17 of those 21 letters, then reads the next six glyphs as 'chiaro' (a stretch ciphered in both copies, so previously unread), giving h, q, u and a third o. Homophonic symbol alphabet, 3-4 forms per common vowel. A pure-Python glyph pipeline (sips to BMP, components, ink-profile bands, cleanup, tuned blurred-mask metric) segments correctly (161 components for 161 crib letters, which proves the cipher is letter-for-letter) but cannot classify shapes: 26% from line-1 templates, 40% blind. The binding constraint is the 1960s microfilm at 50-60 px per glyph. Documentary findings: no. 66 duplicates no. 65 but enciphers DIFFERENT stretches, so the pair cribs itself; no. 63 is complete, its third sheet being f. 123 (numbered '3', dated 'penultimo de Febraro M D XXVIIII' = 27 Feb, signed, docket f. 123v); no. 64 has no cipher; f. 116v carries a third unrecorded gloss 'de Venetiani'. Clear text of all three letters read: the embassy to Alfonso I, the reverence to Renee de France and Ercole d'Este, the Duke asking time over 'so great and honoured an offer', the fallback on Marshal Trivulzio. The offer's content is ciphered in both copies and is NOT read. Six approaches tried and recorded, all failing at the same limit (native resolution confirmed maxed at 8657x5876 per opening; no key sheet in the volume). The sixth is a caution: language-model Viterbi decoding returns fluent Italian-looking text that is entirely invented, so any claimed reading of the both-ciphered conclusion must show per-glyph evidence. External evidence (18 Sept): 'Joachim de Vaulx' identified as Gian Gioacchino de Passano, confirmed three ways, including Potter's note tying BnF fr. 3096 fo. 119-121 to Passano writing from Ferrara on 23 Feb. The status line's substance (Alfonso refusing Naples and the captaincy) is NOT supported: the documented power de Vaulx carried was to propose the league, and no source attests a Naples offer; not catalogued. Molini's Documenti di storia italiana II prints other Sormano letters IN THIS CIPHER with their contemporary decipherments set in italics (13 Apr 1529, anc. 8499 fo. 94, and three more); aligning those italics against the manuscript glyphs would give a long crib and the full alphabet. Molini does not print nos. 63, 65, 66.
Why it matters. Ferrara's defection from France before the Peace of Cambrai. The embassy's charge is now fixed from the King's own letters: de Vaulx was dispatched with 'pouvoir de proposer aux Venitiens, Fleurentins, duc de Millan et de Ferrare ... une si saincte salutaire alliance' - a general Italian league against Charles V under papal headship with France and England. No. 67 belongs to the same operation, buying the imperial Swiss and landsknechts out of the Kingdom of Naples into the league's pay.
Scores. Two deciphered siblings within a month; alignment should give the key in an afternoon. Content is minor.
Verify first. How the scores were set

Importance asks what a full reading would add: 5 for a first-hand report of a major event by a principal witness (du Bellay in London 1529, Lanssac at the Polish election), 2 for routine business. Solvability asks whether the material online is enough: 5 when deciphered siblings of the same hand and year sit in the same volume, 1 for one short letter in an unknown language. Difficulty is the technical work at the keyboard: 1 for aligning a known sibling, 5 for a statistics-only attack on a large homophonic nomenclator. The default priority weights importance 0.4, solvability 0.35 and ease 0.25 (ease is 6 minus difficulty), rescaled to 0–10; move the sliders to see the order change. Every score is a judgement made before viewing the leaf, so treat the order as a work plan, not a finding.

Checked: catalogue descriptions and item numbering; presence or absence of a decipherment item in each volume; shelfmarks from the IIIF manifests; fr. 16127 and fr. 3484 f. 34 on the image. Not checked: the other leaves; Tomokiyo's François I article; the Scheurer, Savasse and Mousset editions; DECODE. User must verify each entry on the image and in the literature before attacking it.
