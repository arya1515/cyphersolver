Thomas Urquhart’s Cyphral Octastich and Distich

The Jewel (1652), final leaf · eight lines and a “Decagram”, 285 numbers to 201 · Logopandecteision (1653), “The Cyphral Distich”, 64 numbers to 70 · Schmeh’s Top 50, no. 28

The octastich rule published as a Claude Fable 5.1 result on 31 August 2026 is reproduced here from the EEBO-TCP text and the public transcription, without the claimed plaintext, and passes shuffled controls; the distich rule does not reproduce.

Daniel Bourdeau · September 2026

Summary. Urquhart printed a verse under the octastich saying the key was “gather’d out of my Exskybalorum” and that he loved “an aphaeresified treason”: the cipher takes word-initials from the book it is printed in. Vals AI’s 31 August 2026 post stated the rule: the k-th number is a word index into page k of the 1652 Jewel, and Urquhart nearly always took the first word on the page that began with the letter he wanted. That last habit makes the rule testable without any plaintext: whether number n on page k is the first word of its own initial is a fact about the page. Run on the TCP text with the printer’s misnumbered pages counted physically, Schmeh’s 272-number transcription aligns with 231 first-occurrence hits out of 267, against 69 % for the same sequence shuffled and 43 % for random pages, and five of the eight lines read as English without reference to Vals’ text: Great Lord, mantaine that regal familie … And grant that he may beare the supreme sweigh … Where English, Scots and Irsh are borne and bred … Our Emperour, King, Monarch and Protector. The other three lines and the Decagram fail here only because the public transcription is short by about thirteen numbers in them. The distich claim, i-th number into the i-th Proquiritation, gives 34 of 64 first-occurrence hits, chance for texts that short, and no English. Status: octastich rule verified and five lines read independently; complete ciphertext needed for the rest; distich open.

01 The two poems

The distich stands under the heading “THE CYPHRAL DISTICH” after the thirty-two Proquiritations that close Logopandecteision, followed by six lines promising that “an honest skilful man” will find in it “his own heart’s wishes, and the Author’s minde”. Reticuli Labs showed on 1 September 2026, and the 1834 Maitland Club reprint confirms, that this page is in the 1834 Works (p. 417) and not in the British Library’s 1653 copy. The octastich stands on the last leaf of The Jewel (1652), a leaf missing from every digitised copy; its public transcription is Klaus Schmeh’s, from a scan sent him in 2014, and the HCPortal record holds a scan with the verse beneath it, which a reader quoted in 2019:

To this Octastick if you will subjoyn / A Decagram of this same stuff of mine, / All gather’d out of my Exskybalorum, / You’ll find a Rule by which, with great decorum, / You may most comfortably regulate / Your actions, thoughts and speeches; and know that / I love an aphaeresified treason / Better then any prosthesized reason.

Aphaeresis is the loss of a word’s first letter. Urquhart says, in his way, that the cipher is made of initials taken out of his own book.

02 A test that never sees the plaintext

A book cipher of this kind is normally judged by whether the letters it yields make sense, which invites the objection that a determined reader can make sense of anything. Urquhart’s habit removes that objection. If he scanned each page from the top and stopped at the first word beginning with the letter he needed, then for the right page the number he wrote is the index of the first word of its own initial. That is a property of the number and the page alone. For a wrong page, or a wrong rule, it holds at the base rate, which in this book is about 43 % for a random page and index because small indices are common and the first few words of any page are first occurrences by construction.

The EEBO-TCP transcription of The Jewel was parsed into word lists per page. The 1652 printer numbered pages 34–35, 38–39, 42–43 and 46–47 twice and skipped 60 and 110, so pages are counted physically from p. 1. Schmeh’s transcription cannot be laid one number per page without gaps, so the numbers were aligned to the pages by a dynamic programme that may skip a page (a number the transcription lost) or drop a number (one it invented), scoring each placement by the first-occurrence test. The best path places 267 numbers with 17 skips and 5 drops; 231 of the 267 are first-occurrence hits.

Line 1 alone makes the case: 28 of its 33 numbers are exactly the first word of the needed initial on physical page k, and the other five are one word off (25 for 26, 18 for 17, 23 for 22, 75 for 74) or a TCP variant, which is the size of difference one expects between a modern tokenisation and a 1652 page.

Where the public transcription is intact the reading agrees with Vals’ published plaintext, including the nine letters of line 5 that neither decoder can read (conerthto, pages 149–157), which are therefore a defect of the cipher or of the 1652 print. Sweigh is Scots swey, “sway”; mantaine is the period spelling. A royalist prayer for Charles II, written in London in the spring of 1652 by a prisoner of Worcester, hidden in a book dedicated to the vindication of Scotland: entirely in character.

04 The distich does not follow

The same test applied to the distich under Vals’ Proquiritation rule, using Reticuli’s tokenised Proquiritations, yields 34 first-occurrence hits in 64 and the string NTMAMNDN_OHTISEABRPTTSGSNTAATVTD, not O God uphold King Charls the second. Texts of thirty to a hundred words give high base rates, so 34 of 64 is chance. Page-index variants on either book, with either numbering, with the second line restarting at page 1, all sit inside the shuffled control. Reticuli’s ten infeasible positions stand. The distich is a different construction, or the same construction on a text not yet tried, and it remains unread.

05 What remains

  • The complete 285-number ciphertext. Schmeh’s public transcription is short in lines 2, 6, 7 and the Decagram. The HCPortal record has the scan but serves it only to browsers; the Internet Archive was offline during this session; Vals’ verification files are unpublished; the 1983 Jack and Lyall edition prints the leaf. With the numbers, the alignment becomes a straight one-per-page decode and the last three lines can be read and controlled the same way.
  • The 1652 leaf itself, for the ±1 positions and the nine letters of line 5.
  • The distich.

06 Method and reproduction

In urquhart/: pages.py parses the TCP XML of A95749 into pages under both numberings; align.py runs the alignment and prints the reading and the per-token table; the controls are in the notes. The TCP text comes from the Text Creation Partnership’s GitHub mirror.
