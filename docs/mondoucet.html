Mondoucet’s cipher: twenty despatches nobody listed

BnF Français 16127 (Harlay 228), Lettres originales de plusieurs ambassadeurs français aux Pays-Bas · Claude de Mondoucet to Charles IX, Catherine de Médicis and Anjou · c. 20 cipher letters with the Court’s decipherments · one of 13 July 1572 without

Found by sweeping digitised BnF volumes that none of the standard lists covers. The key was attacked from two verbatim cipher–decipherment pairs with methods validated on controls, and did not fall.

Daniel Bourdeau · September 2026

On this pageThe sweepThe corpusThe attack on the key, and why it stoppedSources
Summary. The three standard lists of unsolved historical ciphers are stale in both directions: they carry items solved years ago, and they miss whole volumes. A sweep of Gallica for 1570–1620 diplomatic manuscripts absent from Tomokiyo’s catalogues turned up fr. 16127, the Court’s file of Claude de Mondoucet’s correspondence from the Low Countries. His despatches arrived in cipher and were deciphered on receipt; the volume keeps both, some twenty letters from 29 August 1572, five days after St Bartholomew (the archivist’s “1571” is wrong: the letter is dated 1572, and its decipherment is Alva’s Te Deum for “the executions” of Paris), through the siege of Mons and the Amsterdam despatches of 1573–74. One long letter, Brussels 13 July 1572, four days before Genlis’s defeat, has no decipherment anywhere in the volume. The cipher is one system throughout: letters in variant forms, Arabic figures, and some twenty signs. Two cipher–decipherment pairs, one of them glossed word by word by the decipherer on the cipher leaf itself, were transcribed three ways and aligned with four methods, each proved on synthetic controls. All score at the shuffled-plaintext baseline. The glyph count runs 1.3 to 1.4 per plaintext letter on a verbatim pair, so either a quarter of the glyphs are nulls or letters are written with more than one separable stroke; the marginal readings of 1573 are visibly abridged besides. Status: corpus found and inventoried; key not recovered; 13 July 1572 unread. The route is hand-anchoring from the glossed words on f. 62r.

01 The sweep

Gallica’s catalogue was queried for manuscripts of 1575–1620 under Colbert, Dupuy, Ségur, Navarre, chiffre, Villeroy, Bellièvre, Nevers and ambassadeur, and the 159 hits in range were checked against Tomokiyo’s pages on the League volumes, fr. 3995, fr. 4715 and the reigns of Henri III and IV. Those he covers were set aside. Of the rest, fr. 16127, original letters of French agents in the Low Countries 1571–94, was fetched entire (427 canvases, from the Gallica PDF after the IIIF service throttled) and read on contact sheets.

Tomokiyo’s pages cite Mondoucet once, in a 1577 letter of Catherine de Médicis about “le déchiffrement de la lettre de Mondoucet”; fr. 16127 is not in them, nor in Schmeh’s or Dunin’s lists.

03 The attack on the key, and why it stopped

Full-resolution images of ff. 7–9 and 62–64 were cut into half-line strips. The 29 August cipher page was transcribed by hand at glyph level (1,227 tokens, 54 codes) and both pairs by automatic segmentation and shape clustering (7,400 glyphs, 80 clusters). The decipherments were typed (1,215 and 863 letters). Four aligners were built and each first made to recover a synthetic key from the same plaintext under homophones, 12–15 % nulls, 8–10 % token noise and word-codes: hard-EM Viterbi, a consistency beam search, a soft-EM hidden Markov alignment with banded initialisation (44 of 44 symbols on the control), and the same with word-signs consuming two to ten letters (56 of 58 letters and 7 of 10 codes). On the manuscripts every one scores where the shuffled plaintext scores. A blind French homophonic anneal of the hand tokens yields no words. The hand transcription has an index of coincidence of 0.067 and one symbol at 19 % that doubles 31 times, which reads as a null or a merged class rather than a letter; the automatic clusters split that shape three ways.

What survives is the shape of the problem: 1.3 to 1.4 glyph segments per letter on a pair the decipherer glossed word by word, marginal readings of 1573 that are shorter than the blocks beside them, and a symbol repertoire of letter-forms in variants. The productive next step is not another solver but the method that read Feuquières: anchor by hand on the glossed words of f. 62r, where the glyphs under “pouvez”, “leur délivrance” and “la bruslation” give a dozen letters at once, carry them through the 16 July block against f. 64, and take the key to 13 July.
