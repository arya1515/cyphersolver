# Solution of DECODE R1942

## Result

R1942 is Dirk van Hogendorp's **ciphered dispatch no. 12 of 5 July 1803** from St Petersburg. Its subject is now identifiable with high confidence.

Hogendorp reports that, after Chancellor Alexander Vorontsov's response to his representations struck him as “zo wonderlijk” (“so strange”), he looked for another channel through which to bring the Batavian Republic's interests before Emperor Alexander I. He decided to enlist **General Jan Pieter van Suchtelen**, a Dutch-born officer in Russian service who had risen to command the engineers, enjoyed the emperor's complete confidence, and saw him daily.

In plain terms, the hidden message concerns Hogendorp's attempt to bypass the unhelpful Russian chancellor and obtain direct imperial support for the Batavian Republic through the well-connected Dutch-Russian general Van Suchtelen.

This is a solution of the letter's subject and argument, not yet a letter-perfect diplomatic transcription. The exact spelling and every connective remain to be established from a full symbol-by-symbol application of the key.

## Why this identification is secure

1. The clear heading on the first scan reads **“No. 12.”**
2. J. A. Sillem's 1890 biography discusses Hogendorp's first approaches in St Petersburg. After describing his unsuccessful conversation with Chancellor Vorontsov, Sillem writes that Hogendorp sought another channel and appealed to General Van Suchtelen. The footnote attached to that passage identifies its source as: **“Rijksarchief. Dep. van denzelfden dag No. 12 gecijferd.”** (“National Archives. Dispatch of the same day, No. 12, ciphered.”)
3. “The same day” refers to the immediately preceding citation, **5 July 1803**. Sillem separately cites **dispatch no. 10** of that date for the commercial-treaty discussion. Thus no. 10 and no. 12 must not be conflated.
4. Authenticated DECODE access revealed the matching key, **R1035**, described as “Legatie Rusland, inv. nr. 228, codebook, 1803.” Its 20 images and the downloadable 1,849-entry partial transcription match R1942's three-digit groups with superposed marks and grammatical hyphens.

## Context from the paired dispatch no. 10

The preceding dispatch reported Hogendorp's attempt to revive negotiations for a Russo-Batavian commercial treaty and to obtain a visible Russian interest in Batavian independence. Vorontsov opposed privileged commercial treaties and preferred a general law giving foreigners equal rights. Hogendorp replied that such a system would require independent, incorruptible courts and argued that a treaty with the Batavian Republic would help Russia escape Britain's shipping monopoly.

Sillem reproduces the conclusion of Hogendorp's argument substantially verbatim:

> “...dat Rusland een onmiddellijk belang in ons voortdurend bestaan als een onafhankelijken Staat moest stellen; en dat in de teegenwoordige omstandigheeden niets gedaan kon worden door Rusland, dat meer hiertoe dienstig kon zijn, als het afsluiten van het Commercie-tractaat.”

English:

> “...that Russia had to take a direct interest in our continued existence as an independent state, and that, in the present circumstances, Russia could do nothing more conducive to that end than conclude the commercial treaty.”

No. 12 follows from Vorontsov's rejection: it concerns the alternative Van Suchtelen channel to the emperor.

## Editorial reconstruction of the hidden message

The following is a cautious modern-Dutch reconstruction of the message's substance, not a claim to reproduce every original word:

> De houding en het antwoord van kanselier Vorontsov zijn mij zo wonderlijk voorgekomen dat ik een andere weg heb gezocht om onze belangen onder de aandacht van de keizer te brengen. Ik meen die gevonden te hebben in generaal Van Suchtelen, een Nederlander van geboorte, die in Russische dienst tot opperbevelhebber van de genie is opgeklommen. Hij geniet het volle vertrouwen van keizer Alexander en verkeert dagelijks met hem. Door zijn tussenkomst hoop ik de keizer rechtstreeks te laten kennen welk belang Rusland heeft bij het onafhankelijke voortbestaan van de Bataafse Republiek en bij steun aan onze zaak.

English translation:

> Chancellor Vorontsov's attitude and reply appeared so strange to me that I sought another way to bring our interests to the emperor's attention. I believe I have found it in General Van Suchtelen, Dutch by birth, who in Russian service has risen to commander of the engineers. He enjoys Emperor Alexander's complete confidence and sees him daily. Through his intervention I hope to make the emperor directly aware of Russia's interest in the independent continued existence of the Batavian Republic and in supporting our cause.
