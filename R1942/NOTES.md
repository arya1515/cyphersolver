# DECODE R1942 — Dirk van Hogendorp to Maarten van der Goes, 5 July 1803

Status: resolved from print; exact plaintext not yet transcribed.

## Result

R1942 is Hogendorp's ciphered dispatch no. 12 from St Petersburg, dated 5 July 1803. J. A. Sillem's 1890 biography identifies its subject explicitly. After Chancellor Alexander Vorontsov rejected Hogendorp's case for a Russo-Batavian commercial treaty and showed little interest in Batavian independence, Hogendorp looked for another route to Emperor Alexander I. He chose General Jan Pieter van Suchtelen, Dutch-born commander of the Russian engineers, who had the emperor's confidence and saw him daily.

This identifies the hidden subject and argument with high confidence. It is not a diplomatic transcription. No code-group-to-plaintext alignment has yet been completed.

## Evidence

- The opening scan is headed `No. 12` and addressed to Maarten van der Goes.
- Sillem, *Dirk van Hogendorp (1761–1822)* (1890), pp. 151–153, describes the failed Vorontsov approach, then the appeal to Van Suchtelen. The footnote on that paragraph reads: `Rijksarchief. Dep. van denzelfden dag No. 12 gecijferd.`
- “The same day” refers to Sillem's preceding citation of dispatch no. 10, 5 July 1803. No. 10 is the commercial-treaty argument; no. 12 is the alternative Van Suchtelen channel.
- Authenticated DECODE access produced R1035, `Legatie Rusland, inv. nr. 228, codebook, 1803`. Its three-digit groups with superposed marks match R1942. The downloadable partial transcription contains 1,849 distinct entries.
- `R1942_numeric_transcription.txt` contains 286 measured cipher groups. The marked transcription is still a draft because the superposed signs need a second visual pass.
