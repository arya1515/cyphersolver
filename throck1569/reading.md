# Throckmorton to Moray, Greenwich, 20 July 1569: reading of the whole letter

BL Add MS 33531 ff. 79–80 (DECODE R8348). Read 2026-09-21 from the DECODE images. **Plain type** is the clear text of
the letter. *Italics* are the cipher passages, deciphered here with Tomokiyo's key (see NOTES.md) and checked
against the contemporary interlinear gloss wherever the gloss survives. A water stain has faded the gloss on
ff. 79r and 80r, but not the cipher.

Conventions: [N], [L], [Z] etc. are single signs for persons or titles. They are resolved where the gloss gives
them. [?] marks a cipher word not securely read. Spelling is modernised only where the cipher's own spelling is
uncertain.

Page order: f. 79r (DECODE image 1), f. 79v (image 3), f. 80r (image 2), f. 80v (image 4).

## f. 79r

Heading, later hand: "ane Cipher lre from England in ye yeir 1569".

What was *intended and resolved here concerning the* [N = Queen of Scots] *[?] for her restauration, as for the
disposition of her in marriage, your* [L = Lordship] ... thoroughly understand by Mr John Woodes narration. Since
which time you did *hear also by a letter sent from me to the Lord of Liddington in your* [?] of the same matter
absolutely and breefly, wherein [code] *did [?]* ... *How your frendes which here aforesaid were of a* contrarie
minde for the whole, were violently constrained *and by* great necessitie *urged to condescend and thereunto*. So
as *now my good Lord the matter is not in state of deliberation, but rather requires expedite [?] having good
opportunitie for execution*. With further by all convenient means I doe wishe that *you should concurre with your
frendes* [Z] *here, who see so far into your case*, and are so content for the same, as they desire nothinge so
earnestlie as your Lordship's pr[ocuring] *in that matter*, and that to be donne in suche sorte, *both for the*
[code] *and good affection, as it may not appear after [code] to the* [N = Queen of Scots] *or to such as have
interest in her*, that you will doe nothinge frendly but compulsorilie, whereby the frendship, good affection borne
you and thestimacion of you shalbe changed into worse conceiptes, and so gradatim your happines, tranquillitie,
and anie good else, shalbe turned into turmoile, calamitie, and division, *and the prosperitie of your
neighbours* and frendes eyther converted into greate dissention, or into a miserable transformation. Now (as
matters have ben used and conducted) the *protestants your frendes have credit and meanes to assure all things
there and here in good termes*. And suche may be your *impugninge or [double?] proce[edinge]* ...

## f. 79v

*as yours and ours adversaries* shall [code] *possess the instrument [?], and all other circumstances* to the ruine
of Religion, you, and your countrey also, and to worke the lyke effectes amongest us moost dangerouslye. You may
perhaps amongest other perswasions be moved to thinke, that as you *find a faction there to impugne their
designe*, so there shall not want Instrumentes *and a party here to runne the same course*. My Lorde I will not
dissemble with you, and if I would you can gesse at the truthe. *Some there be* [code] *[who?] graunt which will
to their* [Z] *power withstand this matter*. Others will geve the lokinge on; and some will cary twoe faces in a
hoode, lokinge which way the matter is lyke to wende. [M] *of all these sortes that will the end*, to hasten apace,
not to be lefte alone, [code] *when they shall see the ablest and the greatest runne and concurre together in the
favour of the* [N = Queen of Scots, M, La] *for the purposes aforesaid*. And albeit you may thinke [W = the Queen of
England] *will not so hastily favour her promotion*, yet youre experience paste dothe teache you *that* [W] *could
never accord her overthrow*, [Z4] or be contented to see her lyfe dispossessed of her state. *For I graunt if her
majestie would have runne the direct course against her, perhaps the same would have taken good successe*, but
this neutralitie can not stande as the worlde standeth. Some light you maye see and thereby conjecture the reste
*by her majesties letter written to you* [code] *at that time*, whereby also you may be resolved of what affections
they

## f. 80r

they be in this matter *which have the principall credit about her*. [codes] *I can assure your lordship there is
not one, no, not one*, which are of moment, that dothe *not allow of this case, and maketh hast to shew his
gratitude*, leste he might be demed slowe. If I could by anye other course espye anye other thinge than your owne
overthrowe, I would not write so peremptorilie unto you, neyther perswade you to geve place where peradventure your
conscience may be combred, and some question of overprecise objections may arise to calle your honor in dowbte. We
see so far to the beginninge, the middeste and the endinge, and doe forsee all dangers and eventes, as I thinke you
can not, *but [?] your self to concurre with your frendes*. Therefore settinge aside argumentacion, [Z La] *[code]
wishe you should with speed [?] send hither the Lord of Liddington, who undoubtedly is both the wysest and the
sufficientest man may you send* to provide for you and all the reste substancyally and assuredly. And when you shall
leave to be advised by him and to truste him, you shall procure unto yourselfe greatteste perill, and to the whole
state of your countrey the grettest hazarde. No mans frendship will be more imbraced than yours, no mans estimacion
more preserved. [code] *Contrarywise if you desist or become an adverse party, you will be so incombred both from
hence, from thence, and from all other places,* your neighbours, as no man can advise you what to doe. Therefore
God send you to directe your course for the beste. And so I commit you and all yours

to almighty Gods counsell. At Grenew. the 20th of July 1569.

Your L. to use and commande. *N. Throkmorton* (signature in cipher)
