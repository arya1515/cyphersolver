## Notes on method and on signs

- `seg/v2831p3_glyphs.json` already holds per-glyph boxes for all 28 lines of p.3 (src `img/v2831_p3r.jpg`,
  offset [900,870]). Cropping the page by those boxes and drawing the box index over a 5x strip is far more
  legible than the stacked `img/crops/stack2831p3_L*.png` thirds, and it gives a stable numbering to argue about.
- Shape pairs that cost the most time here (see GLYPHS_1/2.png): "z angular" (= r) and "3 rounded" (= o) are
  both 3-like - only the top stroke tells them apart; the epsilon (= o) and the loop-c (= p) are likewise close;
  "8" (= g/n), "9" (= h) and the "g" with a descender loop are three different signs. Where the reading above
  uses 3 for r it says so.
- Values confirmed again on this page: x' = do ("quando"), d+o = st, o-bar = t, a = m, f = a, b^a = na,
  b^e = ne, b. = ce, n+4 = ss, 8 d n = gli, s 9 // = che, c // 3 (or c // z) = per, c // q^s = pero,
  // n+4 4 q^e = essere, r 8 x' = -ando, r p-str E q^e = -atore.
- New or newly useful groups from p.3: 7 E 3 m^e = "forse" (L02, L05); x E c 3 n = "dopoi" (L06, L09);
  m^o 8 v E q^e = "signore" (L08); 6 E d o-bar r = "volta" (L06); 6 4 x 6 o-bar 3 = "veduto" (L11);
  a E d o-bar 3 = "molto" (L09); r v n a 3 = "animo" (L11); 7 3 6 s o-bar 3 = "fructo" (L12);
  o-bar n E b^e = the "-tione" ending (L14, twice).
- Content: the page keeps to the Neapolitan business of p.2 - "messer Francisco" and his going to Naples
  (L04, L10, L12), "reconciliar(e) ... con la sua Maesta" (L08), "qualche parola ad la sua Maesta" (L01),
  an "ambasiatore dicto" (L07) and, at the end, an "excusatione" (L13-L14).
