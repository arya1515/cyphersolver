# ASF, Dieci di Balìa, Responsive 2 no. 171: Galeotto Fibindacci da Ricasoli to the Signoria, 1424

Session of 18 September 2026. Target: DECODE R3754 (`Dieci_di_Balia_Responsive_2_171`, "Non-decrypted", Latin per
DECODE; actually Italian), old number 168, one page, image fetched with Daniel's DECODE cookie

## What it is

A letter in Florentine mercantile cursive, "Magnifici potenti Signori miei", signed "Vostro servidore Galeotto
de Fibindacci da Ricasoli". Galeotto da Ricasoli was a Florentine commissioner in the Romagna during the war
with Milan in 1424 (the year of Zagonara). About twenty cipher runs, some 110 signs in all, mixed into clear
Italian. Above several runs a later hand has written readings: *paolo, di madona, cintadini, il conte, sarrem
andino, a lugo, ambasciatore, madonna, el conte d'Urbino, cavalli, parenti, madonna*.

## The key is in print, on film

Yale Dataverse, Ilardi microfilm reel 58, doi:10.60600/YU/XKVOWE, "Chiavi delle cifre delle lettere ai Dieci
di Balìa dal 1424 al 1530, volume 1" (54 single-page PDFs, open download via the Dataverse API). It is the ASF
volume "Alfabeti che servono a spiegare le lettere in cifra del carteggio dei Dieci di Balìa", compiled by the
abbé Pietro Domenico Gabbrielli in 1863–64. **Page 1 is "Cifra di Galeotto Fibindacci da Ricasoli, carteggio dei
X di Balìa, filze 1, 2, 3, 7, an. 1424"**, with the letters he used noted in the margin (filza 1: 163, 165;
filza 2: 72; filza [3]: 21, 91, 92) and his caveat: "questo alfabeto è tolto da poche parole trovate in sole
quattro lettere; può essere che sia più esteso". Page 2 is a second Ricasoli cipher ("Fibindacci (de) …
commissarius, 1424", filza 1 nos. 128, 157; filza 2 no. 156). Pages 3–4 are the Latin syllabic ciphers of
"Johannes" and "Zaninus et Conradinus", 1424, filza 7.

The interlinear glosses on no. 171 are in Gabbrielli's hand: they are the "poche parole" from which he built the
key, so DECODE's "non-decrypted" means "not printed", not "never read". The letter was read in part in 1863.

## Prior art (literature check, 18 Sept 2026)

- **The key is in print.** Aloys Meister, *Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift* (Paderborn 1902),
  p. 50, "Florenz, Beispiele, 2. Cifra di Galiotto Fibindacci da Ricasoli 1424", prints Gabbrielli's table in full,
  with footnote 1: Gabbrielli noted in Oct 1863 that he built it "only from a few words in four ciphered letters",
  carteggio dei X di Balìa filze 1, 2, 3 and 4, Chiavi vol. I no. 1. Meister pp. 43-46 describe Gabbrielli's volumes
  (197 keys, 62 of the 15th c.; for the early period only 1414 and 1424 reconstructed). Digitised: ULB Münster
  https://sammlungen.ulb.uni-muenster.de/hd/content/titleinfo/3075984 (p. 50 = PDF p. 63). Reproduced as Allegato 3
  ("Fi2", 45 signs) in Judit W. Somogyi, *Verbum* 2016/1-2, pp. 195-217. Mentioned in passing by Pelling (Cipher
  Mysteries, 28 June 2011) and Knowles (comment, 8 Dec 2021).
- **The letters are not in print.** Guasti, *Commissioni di Rinaldo degli Albizzi* (1867-73) has Galeotto only in
  Sept 1423 (vol. 1 pp. 402, 487, 492: the Dieci send him to the Count of Urbino about Faenza) and one later clear
  letter, Urbino 28 Aug 1425, to the orators in Rome (vol. 2 pp. 385-6). Vol. 2 p. 215 n. 1 prints a 1424 cover-name
  list (Genero = Conte d'Urbino, Suocero = Papa, Capra = messer Paolo da Capranica). Osio, Passerini (Ricasoli
  genealogy 1861, pp. 177-181, biography only), Franceschini: nothing. No modern study of the letters themselves.
- **So the novelty is the text, not the key.** Gabbrielli read single words to build his table; nobody has printed
  a continuous reading of any of the four letters. A full reading of no. 171 would be the first.
- **Identities.** Madonna = Caterina Colonna, niece of Martin V, married Guidantonio 1424, arrived Urbino 4 March
  1424, pregnant early 1425 (Raffaello Maria born 4 July 1425; Treccani, "Montefeltro, Guidantonio"). Paolo is not in
  the nomenclator, so either a clear name or a cover-word; candidates: messer Paolo da Capranica (papal, and "Capra"
  in Guasti's 1424 list), Pietro Giampaolo Orsini, Paolo Guinigi.
