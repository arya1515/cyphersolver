# Van Spaen (Düsseldorf) to Van der Goes, 14–15 Jan 1808 (DECODE R1941; catalogue 226)


The letter is still unread: DECODE has no key for this code and nothing is in print. It stays in the catalogue as "attempted, open".

## Source

- Nationaal Archief, The Hague, 2.01.08 Ministerie van Buitenlandse Zaken 1796–1810, inv. nr. 281.
- DECODE R1941 (login images; record metadata saved in `decode/r1941.json`). The four images are photos of
  photocopies (IMG_R1941_I13398–I13401). The images are git-ignored and not committed.
  - P3–P4: the letter, headed "Exh 14 january 1808", "Hoog Edel Gestrenge Heer,", 19 lines of figures, then a clear
    closing in Dutch signed "G. C. van Spaen".
  - P1–P2: the annex, "Dusseldorf den 15 January 1808", filed "136. Exh 18 january 1808", 7 lines of figures,
    same closing. P1 is the lower half of the same page as P2 (a second photo), not a separate document.

## The cipher

A plain numerical code. There are no superscript marks, unlike Croiset's books (R1891, 1798; R1035, 1803). A few
groups are followed by a dash, apparently as a separator. Group values run from 15 to 1339, so the book has at least
1,300 entries. The DECODE note calls it "not typically a nomenclator made by Croiset". Transcription:
`transcription.txt`. Five and three are hard to tell apart in this hand.

| | groups | distinct |
|---|---|---|
| letter 14 Jan | 228 | 181 |
| annex 15 Jan | 75 | 69 |
| both | 303 | 222 |

Most frequent groups: 493 ×6, 297 ×5, 441/424/269/709 ×4. The trigram 602 441 373 occurs three times (twice in the
letter, once in the annex, where it is preceded by 269). It is probably a recurring name or title.

## What was tried (21 Sept 2026)

1. **DECODE for a key.** None of the DECODE key records dated 1780–1815 at Dutch holders is for this code. The
   nearest candidates, R1035 (Croiset 1803) and R1891 (1798), use marked three-digit groups up to 999. Applying R1035
   to the annex gives word salad (ruled out).
2. **Contemporary decipherment.** There is none on the images. DECODE says the annex "is solved" and sets the record
   to "Partially decrypted", but no transcription or DOC file is attached to the record.
3. **Print.** Colenbrander, *Gedenkstukken* V (1806–1810), was searched in the full text on resources.huygens.knaw.nl
   for Spaen, Voorstonden, Dusseldorp and Westphalen. It mentions only Van Spaen la Lecq and none of these letters.
4. **Ciphertext-only.** Not attempted. With 222 distinct groups from a code of 1,300+ entries, most groups occur once.
   A single-letter code with about 300 groups cannot be broken without a key or a crib.

## What would move it

- The DECODE owner's reading of the 15 Jan annex, which would give up to about 70 values and the book's ordering.
  If the book is one-part, that would bracket much of the letter.
- The Kingdom of Holland's legation codes, 1806–1810: NA 2.01.08 (BuZa 1796–1810) code-book inventory numbers, or
  the Van der Goes papers.

## Correction from the archive inventory (21 Sept 2026)

The Nationaal Archief inventory of 2.01.08 (downloaded PDF) describes inv. nr. 281 as *Missiven van J.F.G. van Spaen
en G. van Riemsdijk, commissarissen voor de overname der gecedeerde districten, de Lijmers, Huyssen, Malburgen en
Zevenaar, jul 1806 – mei 1809*. The letter therefore comes from the Dutch commissioners for the takeover of the
districts ceded around Zevenaar, not from an ambassador to "the Court of Westphalia". Düsseldorf was the capital of
the Grand Duchy of Berg, the other party to the exchange. G.C. van Spaen's own legation letters (Berlin, 1802–1807)
are inv. nrs. 244–248. The signature read from the preview looks like "G. C. van Spaen", but a J.F.G. reading was not
ruled out. The subject is very probably the territorial handover. That context does not give a crib.
